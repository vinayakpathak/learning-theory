## Summary

Counterexample search finds a clean obstruction to `idea_02`: any de-enumeration theorem based only on sampling candidate hidden-clean transcripts must pay an unavoidable factor `p^K`, where `p` is the hidden clean mass and `K` is the total number of clean examples needed by the transcript.

Thus a polynomial sampler is plausible only when the total hidden-clean witness length is `O(log(s/epsilon))` with favorable constants and inverse-polynomial clean-success probability. If `K = omega(log s)`, and especially if `K = Omega(s^a)`, polynomial-size hitting sets fail in a basic hidden-mask model.

This is not a separation for the atlas edge. It is a lower-bound stress test for the da Cunha-style proof skeleton and for any proposed “sample raw tuples until one is clean” replacement.

## Concrete Progress

Let a near-optimal comparator `c` have hidden clean probability

$$
p = \Pr[Y=c(X)] = 1-\eta
$$

or, in a clean-mixture proof, possibly the smaller clean-component mass `p = 1-2eta`.

Suppose a successful transcript requires `K` hidden-clean sample positions. If the wrapper outputs at most `L` candidate transcripts from observable data, and the hidden clean indicators remain conditionally Bernoulli with parameter `p`, then

$$
\Pr[\text{some candidate transcript is fully clean}]
\le L p^K.
$$

So for `L = poly(s,1/epsilon)`:

- `K = O(log(s/epsilon))` is the only regime where raw sampling can plausibly work.
- `K = Theta(log^2 s)` already gives quasi-polynomial discovery probability.
- `K = Omega(s^a)` gives exponentially small discovery probability.

This matches the existing accounting obstruction but makes the counterexample sharper: bounded randomness or a polynomial candidate menu does not remove the hidden-clean mask penalty.

## Claims Or Lemmas

**Lemma 1: Hidden-Mask Anti-Hitting Lemma.**  
In a model where candidate transcripts are chosen before seeing hidden clean bits, and every successful transcript needs at least `K` distinct clean positions,

$$
\Pr[\exists\text{ successful candidate among }L]\le Lp^K.
$$

This is immediate by union bound. Repeated indices should be counted by the number of distinct informative clean positions.

**Lemma 2: Linear Clean Witnesses Occur Naturally.**  
For the class of all labelings on a `d`-point domain under the uniform marginal, a clean weak learner with target clean error

$$
\beta = 1/2-\gamma
$$

needs information about `Omega(gamma d)` labels for a random target. Otherwise, on unseen coordinates the labels are independent of the transcript and contribute error `1/2`.

So a clean transcript can naturally have `K = Omega(d)`, even though the class is efficiently learnable from clean data in time polynomial in `d`.

**Lemma 3: Noisy Raw Labels Do Not Automatically Replace Clean Transcripts Near The Endpoint.**  
Take RCN with

$$
\eta = 1/2-\gamma/2 > \beta=1/2-\gamma.
$$

A raw transcript using one noisy label per covered point has typical noise above the weak clean-error target. To be weak-good for the clean target, it needs a downward noise fluctuation of order `gamma`, which costs

$$
\exp(-\Omega(\gamma^2 K))
$$

by Chernoff-type accounting.

## Proof Attempts

The main proof attempt is a toy hidden-mask lower bound.

Construct raw samples with hidden indicators `Z_i = 1[Y_i=c(X_i)]`, where conditionally on the observable transcript the `Z_i` are independent Bernoulli `p`. A de-enumerator outputs `L` candidate `K`-tuples of indices. For any fixed tuple,

$$
\Pr[Z_i=1\text{ for all }i\in I]=p^K.
$$

Union bounding over `L` candidates gives `Lp^K`. Therefore no polynomial-size hitting set can work in this model once `K` is superlogarithmic.

The finite-table class gives a concrete stress test for large `K`: for random labels on `[d]`, a transcript revealing fewer than `2 gamma d` clean labels cannot, in general, produce correlation `2 gamma` with the clean target. Thus a weak clean transcript may require linearly many hidden-clean labels.

## Gaps And Risks

This is a proof-skeleton obstruction, not a lower bound against strong agnostic learning.

The finite-table class is actually strongly learnable by direct denoising or empirical majority when enough samples are available. So it cannot separate weak agnostic improper learning from strong agnostic improper learning.

The hidden-mask independence assumption fails for structured classes where observed `(X,Y)` carries usable information about clean status or about `c`.

Heavy atoms and repeated samples can also defeat the argument: if the learner can query or observe the same `x` many times, majority vote may identify the clean label without ever finding an all-clean tuple.

The exact relevance to da Cunha-style enumeration depends on whether the proof needs literal clean conditional samples, clean-mixture samples, or only final hypotheses generated by such samples.

## Counterexamples Or Obstructions

The strongest obstruction is:

> If total hidden-clean transcript length `K` is not logarithmic, polynomial de-enumeration by raw tuple sampling is impossible in the hidden-mask model.

A sharper stress example uses all labelings on a `d`-point domain. Clean weak learning against a random target needs `Omega(d)` revealed clean labels. Under agnostic RCN with constant clean probability `p<1`, any candidate transcript requiring those clean labels is discovered with probability at most

$$
\exp(-\Omega(d))
$$

up to polynomial menu factors.

This refutes any broad version of the idea saying “stability, bounded randomness, or sample compression automatically gives a polynomial hitting set.” It only works if the total clean witness size, after compression and all weak calls, is logarithmic and has inverse-polynomial success mass.

Formalize the hidden-mask anti-hitting lemma as a restricted obstruction.

Then audit the relevant boosting proof to identify the true total clean witness length `K`: is it one weak call of size `m0`, or the whole adaptive transcript of size about `m0 T`?

A positive de-enumeration route should explicitly assume

$$
p^K q \ge 1/\operatorname{poly}(s,1/\epsilon),
$$

where `q` is the clean-transcript success probability after compression/randomness. Without that, the counterexample above blocks polynomial raw discovery.
