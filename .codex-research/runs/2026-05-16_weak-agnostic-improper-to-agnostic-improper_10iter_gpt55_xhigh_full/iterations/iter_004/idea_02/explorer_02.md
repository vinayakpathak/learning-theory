## Summary

The implicit-vote-selector idea helps only with one of the da Cunha-style bottlenecks. It can plausibly replace the explicit final `|B|^T` vote enumeration under extra assumptions, but it does not by itself remove the upstream hidden-clean weak-call generation problem.

Runtime diagnosis:

1. **Hidden-clean generation remains the first fatal bottleneck.** Polynomially many ordinary weak calls on observable noisy distributions need not produce the hypotheses used in the da Cunha clean-slice proof, because those calls may lie in the hard band.
2. **Final vote ERM is a second real bottleneck.** If a useful polynomial bag `B` were already available, exact ERM over `sign(conv(B))` or `sign(B^(T))` is essentially empirical 0-1 halfspace optimization over the feature map `x -> (h(x))_{h in B}`, not generically polynomial.
3. **Convex surrogate optimization is not a drop-in replacement.** It may be polynomial, but the da Cunha/noisy-validation argument is 0-1-risk based; surrogate loss can punish confident predictions that agree with the clean comparator but disagree with noisy labels.

So the idea yields a useful conditional direction: **hidden-clean generator + polynomial final selector/list + clean-population/generalization control implies the positive route.** It does not yet produce such a selector from the atlas fixed-slack source alone.

## Concrete Progress

A clean runtime separation is:

- Let `B` be the generated base-hypothesis bag.
- If `B` is produced by exhaustive da Cunha tuple enumeration, then `|B|` already contains the `n^{m0}` cost.
- If `B` is produced by only polynomially many fresh observable weak calls, there is no guarantee that `B` contains any hypothesis useful for the hidden clean conditional `D_c^+`.
- If a polynomial `B` is somehow useful, final selection over unrestricted `T`-wise votes still costs `|B|^T`, and `T = O(min{d*, log n}/theta^2)` in the audited 2026 proof.

Thus an implicit selector would need to solve both:

$$
\text{find useful base hypotheses}
$$

and

$$
\text{optimize/select a final vote from them}.
$$

Solving only the second does not resolve the atlas implication.

## Claims Or Lemmas

**Claim 1: Final vote selection reduces to halfspace ERM over generated features.**

For a finite bag `B={h_1,...,h_N}`, any real-weighted vote has form

$$
x \mapsto \operatorname{sign}\left(\sum_{j=1}^N w_j h_j(x)\right).
$$

On a validation sample, minimizing empirical 0-1 loss over such votes is exactly empirical halfspace optimization on feature vectors

$$
z_i=(h_1(x_i),...,h_N(x_i)).
$$

This is not generically supplied by the weak learner and should be treated as an extra algorithmic primitive.

**Claim 2: Convex optimization over mixtures does not preserve the known proof guarantee.**

Replacing 0-1 ERM by hinge/logistic/exponential-loss minimization is computationally attractive, but the da Cunha-style clean-slice guarantee gives a low 0-1-risk final classifier, not necessarily a low surrogate-risk classifier under noisy labels.

A classifier close to the comparator `c` may incur large surrogate loss on the noisy slice where `Y != c(X)`, even though its 0-1 excess is controlled by the clean-conditional lemma.

**Claim 3: The upstream weak-call problem is not separable from selection.**

Column generation or online convex optimization would need an oracle that, for the current gradient/loss distribution, supplies a useful base hypothesis. The atlas source only gives fixed-additive approximation to `C` under the queried labeled distribution. In the hard band, it may legally return a dummy. Therefore a polynomial implicit selector cannot assume access to the hidden clean weak hypotheses unless a hidden-clean generator is separately proved.

## Proof Attempts

A tempting implementation is:

1. Run the fixed weak learner on polynomially many reweighted noisy distributions.
2. Store the realized outputs in `B`.
3. Optimize a convex or ERM objective over votes from `B`.
4. Validate the final classifier on fresh noisy samples.

The failure is step 1. The reweighted noisy distributions are observable, but the da Cunha proof needs weak hypotheses for adaptive distributions over the hidden clean sample or clean conditional. Without knowing which examples satisfy `Y=c(X)`, polynomial observable calls need not hit those distributions.

A second attempt is to keep da Cunha’s large implicit `B` but optimize over it with a separation oracle. That requires solving:

$$
\max_{h \in B} \sum_i a_i y_i h(x_i)
$$

or a related pricing problem over all weak-learner outputs generated by all hidden tuples/seeds. For an arbitrary improper learner `A`, this is not accessible from the source guarantee; it can be as hard as the tuple search itself.

## Gaps And Risks

The idea is still valuable under extra structure. It could work if one assumes:

- a polynomial hidden-clean weak-call generator or hitting set,
- an explicit polynomial candidate list of complete final classifiers,
- or an efficient final optimizer with a matching generalization/stability theorem.

But those are additional hypotheses, not consequences of fixed `OPT + beta` weak learning.

The strongest risk is that exact final ERM over votes is computationally hard in general, while convex surrogate minimization may optimize the wrong objective for agnostic noisy labels.

## Counterexamples Or Obstructions

A clean-slice-useful weak hypothesis can be poor under noisy validation, so validating individual base hypotheses cannot replace hidden-clean boosting.

A polynomial bag can still be useless if generated from observable hard-band calls: the weak learner may return majority constants or other legal dummy hypotheses.

A data-dependent vote can fit the hidden clean empirical subset but fail on `D_c^+` unless one has VC, compression, stability, or direct population control.
