## Context

The source gives one uniform learner $A$ such that, for every instance marginal
$P$, there are marginal-dependent polynomial resource bounds and a weak
agnostic tolerance

$$
\beta_P(s)=1/2-\gamma_P(s)<1/2,
$$

where $1/\gamma_P(s)$ is bounded by some $P$-dependent polynomial. For every
joint distribution $\mathcal D$ with marginal $P$,

$$
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(A)
\le
\inf_{c\in\mathcal C}\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(c)+\beta_P(s).
$$

The target asks for one distribution-free polynomial-time improper realizable
PAC learner: for every marginal $P$, every $c\in\mathcal C$, and every
$\varepsilon>0$, output $h$ with

$$
\operatorname{err}_P(h,c)\le \varepsilon
$$

in time polynomial in $(s,1/\varepsilon,\log(1/\delta))$, with one polynomial
independent of $P$.

Restricting the source to realizable labels gives, under the actual marginal
$P$,

$$
\operatorname{err}_P(h,c)\le 1/2-\gamma_P(s).
$$

Thus the edge is not blocked by agnostic noise itself. It is a combination of:

1. weak-to-strong amplification from a marginal-dependent weak advantage; and
2. distribution-free uniformity of the final polynomial.

## Attempted Positive Routes

### 1. Full-support marginal uniformization handles resources, not weak gaps

The newer `full-support-marginal-uniformization` argument is relevant. Under
the atlas worst-case/pathwise convention for finite encoded sample transcripts,
a full-support reference marginal $P^\star$ gives one distribution-free
polynomial bound on the sample use and runtime of the single source learner
$A$.

However, the full-support argument explicitly does not transfer accuracy from
$P^\star$. Accuracy must still be read under the actual marginal $P$, where the
source only gives the weak gap $\gamma_P(s)$. The target needs arbitrary
$\varepsilon$ with a distribution-free polynomial, so a booster whose runtime
depends on $1/\gamma_P(s)$ is not enough unless there is a distribution-free
inverse-polynomial lower bound on all $\gamma_P(s)$, or some other algorithmic
route around the hidden gap.

This explains why the already-resolved strong marginal edges do not settle this
one. If the source were `efficient-marginal-nonuniform-realizable-improper-pac`,
then full-support uniformization would directly give the target. Here the
source is weak, so uniformizing the runtime of one weak call does not provide a
uniform number of calls for strong learning.

### 2. Standard realizable boosting changes the marginal

Schapire/Freund realizable boosting converts weak realizable learners into
strong improper learners. The difficulty is quantitative bookkeeping under
marginal nonuniformity.

A standard booster calls the weak learner on reweighted marginals $Q_t$
depending on $P$, the target $c$, previous weak hypotheses, and randomness.
The source gives a weak guarantee for each $Q_t$, but with its own gap
$\gamma_{Q_t}(s)$. To get a polynomial-time target by this route, one needs an
envelope such as:

$$
\inf\{\gamma_Q(s): Q\ll P,\ dQ/dP\le B\}
\ge
1/r_P(s,B)
$$

for some polynomial $r_P$ and for the bounded-density family generated by a
smooth booster. The current source definition gives no such local uniformity.
It assigns a polynomial and weak gap separately to each marginal.

For the distribution-free target, even a $P$-dependent envelope would not be
enough by itself; one would further need a distribution-free lower bound or a
second full-support-style argument that also controls the weak gaps. The
transcript argument controls runtime paths, not error margins.

### 3. Same-marginal agnostic boosting has a fixed-additive floor

Because the source is agnostic, one can try to keep the instance marginal fixed
and vary only labels, following the distribution-specific agnostic boosting
intuition. This is the strongest near-positive route, since it avoids calling
the learner on reweighted instance marginals.

The fixed-additive atlas guarantee is too coarse for this direct route. In
$\{\pm1\}$ notation, relabel under the same marginal $P$ with

$$
\mathbb E[Y\mid X=x]=a(x)c(x),\qquad 0\le a(x)\le 1.
$$

The concept $c$ has error $(1-\mathbb E_P a)/2$ on this relabeled
distribution. Applying the source guarantee and comparing to $c$ yields

$$
\mathbb E_P[a(X)\mathbf 1\{h(X)\ne c(X)\}]\le \beta_P(s).
$$

If $a=\lambda\,dQ/dP$, this implies

$$
\operatorname{err}_Q(h,c)\le \beta_P(s)/\lambda.
$$

This is a weak guarantee under $Q$ only when $\lambda>2\beta_P(s)$. Since
$a\le 1$, $\lambda\le 1/\|dQ/dP\|_\infty$. When
$\beta_P(s)=1/2-\gamma_P(s)$ is close to $1/2$, this only handles density
ratios essentially equal to $1$. It cannot focus on the small residual regions
that strong realizable learning eventually needs to clean up.

Equivalently, Feldman-style threshold agnostic boosting would need a weak
oracle at threshold $\alpha=O(\varepsilon)$, but the atlas source gives a
positive threshold edge only when $\alpha>\beta_P(s)$.

## Counterexample Search

A false witness would need to satisfy the source for every marginal, while
failing distribution-free strong improper realizable learning under some
marginal. None of the standard atlas witnesses fit.

- Pseudorandom-function classes fail the target, but they are source-negative:
  on the hard marginal, no randomized polynomial-time learner can even weakly
  learn.
- Halfspaces satisfy the target by efficient realizable learning, so they only
  separate agnostic targets, not this realizable improper target.
- Clause-satisfaction and one-way image-coordinate witnesses separate proper
  learning from improper learning. They have efficient improper learners, hence
  do not refute an improper realizable target.
- PCP active-slice witnesses also target proper-output lower bounds; their
  improper lookup learners are already strong enough for this target.

The tempting template is a rare-hard-slice construction. Put many slices into
one class, make slice $k$ weakly learnable in time $s^{k}$ but not strongly
learnable in time $s^{o(k)}$, and let a marginal-nonuniform weak learner learn
a finite prefix carrying slightly more than half of any fixed marginal's mass.
This could explain a marginal-dependent weak gap with no distribution-free
strong polynomial.

I do not regard this as an atlas-ready counterexample. It would require a
concrete concept class and a lower bound hierarchy for improper realizable
learning where every slice is still weakly agnostically learnable under its own
hard marginal. Plain cryptographic slices fail this, because they are not weakly
learnable even on the slice marginal. Proper-recovery slices fail because the
assigned target is improper.

## Depth 2 Attempt: Uniform-Gap Collapse And Slice-Hierarchy Witness

- depth: 2
- date: 2026-05-05

### Conditional Route To A True Edge

After reading the sibling marginal-strong scratchpad, I think the cleanest
positive statement is not just "boost the weak learner." It is:

**Sufficient uniform envelope.** Suppose the source learner has, after
full-support resource uniformization, a distribution-free inverse-polynomial
weak advantage on every marginal that a smooth realizable booster can generate.
For example, for some fixed polynomial $r$,

$$
\inf\{\gamma_Q(s): Q \text{ can appear as a booster marginal at size } s\}
\ge 1/r(s,1/\varepsilon).
$$

Then Schapire/Freund-style boosting gives the target. The resource polynomial
for each weak call is uniformized by the full-support transcript argument, and
the number of rounds is polynomial in $r$ and $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. The final
majority vote is improper, which is allowed.

This formulation isolates the missing ingredient: the source gives only

$$
\forall Q\quad \exists r_Q\quad \gamma_Q(s)\ge 1/r_Q(s),
$$

while the distribution-free target needs one $r$ independent of $Q$. Even an
original-marginal envelope $r_P$ would only prove the marginal-nonuniform strong
target, not this edge.

### Why Full-Support Uniformization Does Not Force The Envelope

I tried to turn the full-support marginal $P^\star$ into a compactness or
mixture argument for a uniform weak gap. The attempt seems to fail for a
specific reason.

If $P^\star=\sum_i w_iP_i$, an accuracy statement of order $w_i\varepsilon$
under $P^\star$ transfers to accuracy $\varepsilon$ under $P_i$. But a weak
guarantee has constant-scale error $1/2-\gamma_{P^\star}$, not
$w_i\varepsilon$-scale error. It can ignore components of total weight below
roughly $1/2$ and still be weak on the mixture. Thus a sequence of marginals
$P_i$ with gaps $\gamma_{P_i}$ smaller than every fixed inverse polynomial does
not contradict a polynomial gap for a carefully weighted mixture $P^\star$.

This is the same obstruction as the rare-slice idea, but phrased as a failure
of the natural proof of a uniform weak-gap theorem. The source is compatible
with a learner whose behavior is good on every fixed marginal, with an exponent
depending on where that marginal puts its mass, while no global exponent is
visible.

### A More Precise False Template

The most plausible counterexample template is a parameterized slice hierarchy.
It would be enough to build a disjoint union

$$
\mathcal X=\bigsqcup_{k\ge 1}\mathcal X_k,\qquad
\mathcal C=\{\text{default outside one slice, } c\in\mathcal C_k
\text{ on slice } k\},
$$

with the following properties.

1. For each fixed $k$, the slice class $\mathcal C_k$ has an efficient
   agnostic improper learner running in time $s^{O(k)}$.
2. There is one uniform algorithm which, given a cutoff $K$, runs the first
   $K$ slice learners in time $s^{O(K)}$.
3. For every fixed distribution-free polynomial exponent $a$, some slice
   $k>a$ is not strongly realizably learnable improperly in time $s^a$ under a
   marginal supported on $\mathcal X_k$.

Then the union would satisfy the source. For a fixed marginal $P$ over the
slice index, choose $K=K(P)$ so that the tail mass
$P(\bigcup_{k>K}\mathcal X_k)$ is below $1/2-2\gamma_P$. Run agnostic ERM, or
the fixed-slice agnostic learners, on the first $K$ slices and output the
default hypothesis on the tail. If the best concept is supported in the prefix,
the prefix learner competes with it and the tail costs at most the tail mass.
If the best concept is supported in the tail, the default hypothesis already
competes on the prefix and again loses at most the tail mass. With enough
$P$-dependent samples to estimate the prefix mass and validate the prefix ERM,
this gives an $\operatorname{OPT}+\beta_P$ guarantee for some
$\beta_P<1/2$, and the runtime is bounded by a $P$-dependent polynomial.

But the distribution-free strong realizable target could put all mass on the
hard slice $\mathcal X_k$. A single target polynomial $s^a$ would then have to
strongly learn every $\mathcal C_k$, contradicting property 3 for large $k$.

This is not yet an atlas-ready false witness. The missing object is the slice
hierarchy itself. It must be source-positive on each fixed slice, including
agnostic improper learning in time $s^{O(k)}$, while still excluding every
uniform polynomial-time strong improper learner across all $k$. Existing
witnesses miss one side of this requirement:

- PRF/noisy-parity slices are strong-hard, but they are not weakly agnostically
  learnable on their own hard marginals.
- PCP active-slice and one-way coordinate witnesses separate proper learning;
  their improper learners are already too strong.
- Pure finite-class or lookup hierarchies risk violating the sample-complexity
  equivalence if the VC dimension grows without control, or becoming uniformly
  learnable if the lookup structure is too explicit.

### Depth 3 Directions

1. Try to instantiate the slice hierarchy with a time-hierarchy or
   parameterized-cryptographic family where slice $k$ is learnable by exhaustive
   search in $s^{O(k)}$ but not by any $s^a$ learner for $a<k$. The hard part is
   keeping the representation-size bookkeeping consistent with the atlas
   polynomial notion.

2. Prove a no-slice theorem: show that any source-positive parameterized
   hierarchy of the form above collapses to a uniform polynomial-time strong
   improper learner. Such a theorem would need to use more than VC/sample
   complexity, since the computational hardness is the only remaining
   obstruction.

3. Revisit fixed-additive agnostic boosting only for realizable targets. A
   booster that keeps the instance marginal fixed and uses the
   $\operatorname{OPT}+\beta_P$ oracle without needing thresholds
   $\alpha<\beta_P$ would bypass the reweighted-marginal envelope. The current
   relabeling calculation suggests this cannot be a black-box residual-weight
   simulation, but it does not rule out a more global construction.

4. Separate the two open subproblems explicitly in any future atlas note:
   distribution-free weak-gap uniformization would prove this edge true, while
   a source-positive slice hierarchy with no uniform exponent would prove it
   false.

## Depth 3 Attempt: Pathwise Runtime And Cheap Weak Handles

- depth: 3
- date: 2026-05-05

### What I Tried To Complete

I tried to turn the depth-2 slice hierarchy into a resolved false witness. The
basic idea was to let slice $k$ be learnable in time $s^{O(k)}$, but not in
time $s^a$ for $a<k$, and then use marginal nonuniformity to choose a
$P$-dependent finite prefix of slices.

Under the atlas runtime convention, this version of the template has a
serious problem. The source learner is one uniform algorithm with
worst-case/pathwise sample and time bounds on finite encoded transcripts. Let
$P^\star$ be a full-support marginal over all finitely encoded instances. The
source guarantee for $P^\star$ gives one polynomial $p_{P^\star}$ bounding the
runtime of the same learner on every transcript that has positive
$P^\star$-probability.

Therefore a source learner cannot decide, after seeing examples from a high
slice $k$, to run an $s^{O(k)}$ slice subroutine if $k$ is unbounded at the
same representation size. That high-slice transcript also has positive
probability under $P^\star$, so such behavior would violate the
$p_{P^\star}$ resource bound. Full-support uniformization does not give a
uniform accuracy gap, but it does rule out using marginal-dependent runtime
exponents as the mechanism behind the source.

This means the depth-2 false template must be strengthened. A valid witness
cannot merely say "slice $k$ is learnable by exhaustive search in
$s^{O(k)}$." The weak learner itself must run in one fixed distribution-free
polynomial time after full-support resource uniformization. The unbounded
slice parameter may only appear in the weak advantage, for example

$$
\gamma_{Q_k}(s)\asymp s^{-k},
$$

not in the cost of producing the weak hypothesis.

### Revised False-Witness Shape

A source-positive, target-negative slice hierarchy would need **cheap
nonboostable weak handles**:

1. There is one polynomial-time weak agnostic improper learner $A$ with a
   fixed transcript-wise runtime bound, independent of the slice.

2. For every marginal concentrated on slice $k$, $A$ gives an additive weak
   guarantee with inverse-polynomial but slice-dependent gap, say
   $\gamma_k(s)=s^{-O(k)}$.

3. For every fixed marginal over slices, active targets in sufficiently small
   tail slices can be ignored, so the global weak gap remains inverse
   polynomial with a $P$-dependent exponent.

4. Nevertheless, under some distribution-free strong-realizable test marginal
   on slice $k$, learning below a fixed constant accuracy, or below a selected
   slice mass in a mixture, requires either about $s^{\Omega(k)}$ weak handles
   or solving a computationally hard residual problem.

This is narrower than the previous rare-hard-slice sketch. It is not enough
for each slice to be eventually learnable by an expensive algorithm; the weak
handle must be cheaply findable even when the marginal is supported entirely
on that slice. Conversely, if the cheap handles aggregate by any standard
boosting method in time polynomial in $1/\gamma_k=s^{O(k)}$, the hierarchy only
separates the hidden exponents. To refute the distribution-free target, one
still needs a lower bound ruling out every fixed exponent $s^a$.

### Discarded Non-Witness: Countable Lookup

There is a tempting information-theoretic toy example: on a countable domain,
learn labels on a finite set carrying slightly more than half of a fixed
marginal's mass, and default outside it. This gives a marginal-dependent weak
agnostic behavior for unrestricted labelings on purely atomic marginals.

I do not think this is atlas-ready for this edge. The atlas sample-only
counterpart of this implication is already recorded as true via the
Hanneke--Moran--Thiessen marginal-nonuniform trichotomy and VC theory. So any
computational false witness here must live on the finite-VC/sample-efficient
side and fail only because of polynomial-time implementability. Pure lookup or
all-labelings examples either violate that sample-complexity posture or hide
the representation-size parameter in a way that would need a separate schema
change.

### Positive Route Rechecked

The pathwise observation above helps only with resources. It does not prove
the missing distribution-free weak-gap envelope.

For the fixed source learner $A$, define informally $\Delta_A(Q,s)$ to be the
best weak gap actually certified by $A$ under marginal $Q$ at size $s$. The
source gives

$$
\forall Q\ \exists d_Q\quad \Delta_A(Q,s)\ge s^{-d_Q}
$$

after choosing suitable constants. The target would follow from something like

$$
\exists d\ \forall Q\quad \Delta_A(Q,s)\ge s^{-d},
$$

at least for the marginals generated by a booster. I do not see a way to get
this from full support. Accuracy under $P^\star$ can still ignore small
components, so a sequence of slices with gaps $s^{-1},s^{-2},s^{-3},\ldots$
is compatible with one inverse-polynomial gap for any fixed mixture that puts
most of its mass on a finite prefix.

Same-marginal relabeling also remains blocked by the fixed-additive floor
recorded in depth 1 and depth 2. The source can be very useful in low-noise
subproblems, but it does not supply a tunable residual oracle at accuracy
scale $\varepsilon$.

### Depth 4 Directions

1. Formalize a **budgeted weak-output class** for the uniform source learner:
   all hypotheses printable within the full-support polynomial runtime. Ask
   whether the source says that this fixed budgeted class weakly approximates
   $\mathcal C$ under every marginal with merely pointwise-polynomial edge.
   A positive theorem turning that into a uniform edge would resolve the
   implication true; a lower-bound construction for majorities over this class
   would support the cheap-handle false route.

2. Build an oracle or black-box separation where the weak oracle runs in fixed
   polynomial time, has edge $s^{-k}$ on slice $k$, and any booster using only
   polynomially many calls with a fixed exponent fails on some slice. This
   would not be an atlas counterexample by itself, but it would test whether
   the revised cheap-handle template is internally coherent.

3. If pursuing a concrete false witness, move the unbounded parameter from
   "time to find one weak hypothesis" into "number or structure of weak
   handles needed for strong learning." PRF/noisy-parity residuals still fail
   unless every marginal concentrated on the residual has a new cheap weak
   handle; a recursive hard-core/handle construction is the more plausible
   shape to investigate.

4. Recheck whether finite VC dimension plus one uniform polynomial-time
   pointwise weak learner imposes any quantitative lower semicontinuity of
   $\Delta_A(Q,s)$ over finitely supported marginals. Compactness alone gives
   positivity at fixed $s$ only if the gap function is well behaved, and it
   still does not give a polynomial lower bound in $s$.

5. Keep the target-specific distinction clear: the halfspace/Tiegel route
   resolves strong agnostic targets conditionally false, but it cannot refute
   this realizable improper target because halfspaces are efficiently
   realizably learnable.

## Depth 4 Attempt: Diagonal Gap Uniformization And Nonboostable Handles

- depth: 4
- date: 2026-05-05

### Goal And Current Normal Form

After depth 3, the resource side is no longer the plausible separation
mechanism. Let $A$ be the single source learner and use the full-support
pathwise argument only to put a distribution-free polynomial bound on the
running time and sample use of one call to $A$. In realizable distributions,
the remaining information is:

$$
\forall Q\ \exists d_Q,C_Q\quad
\operatorname{err}_Q(A)\le 1/2-C_Qs^{-d_Q}
$$

up to the usual confidence bookkeeping. A positive proof needs to replace the
pointwise exponent $d_Q$ by one exponent for all marginals a booster may call.
A false witness needs a class where every such weak call is cheap, but the
number or structure of weak calls needed for strong improper prediction has no
distribution-free polynomial bound.

### A Near-Proof By Size-Diagonalizing The Weak Gaps

The most tempting positive argument is a diagonal one. Define
$\Gamma_A(Q,s)$ to be the realizable weak advantage certified for $A$ on
marginal $Q$ at representation size $s$. Suppose there is no distribution-free
inverse-polynomial lower bound on $\Gamma_A(Q,s)$, even just over the
booster-generated marginals. Then for each degree $d$ one could choose a size
$s_d$ and a marginal $Q_d$ such that

$$
\Gamma_A(Q_d,s_d)<s_d^{-d}.
$$

If a "marginal" in the atlas were formally a size-indexed family
$Q=(Q_s)_{s\ge 1}$, this would contradict the source. Build a diagonal
marginal family with $Q_{s_d}=Q_d$ and arbitrary choices elsewhere. The source
guarantee for this one family would give some polynomial gap
$C s^{-D}$ for all large sizes. Taking $d>D$ and $s_d$ large contradicts the
choice of $Q_d$. Under this size-conditional convention, pointwise
marginal-nonuniform weak learnability would collapse to a distribution-free
weak gap, and ordinary realizable boosting would resolve this edge true.

I do not think this can be recorded as the unconditional atlas verdict yet.
The current notes use a full-support marginal to uniformize **resources**, but
they explicitly avoid transferring **accuracy** from a mixture to its low-mass
components. If a diagonal construction is represented instead as one weighted
distribution over all finite encodings, the bad size slices can be assigned
tiny weights. A weak guarantee for the global mixture may ignore those slices
and still keep error below $1/2-\gamma$. Thus the diagonal proof depends on a
size-conditioning convention, or on an additional lemma saying that source
accuracy is evaluated separately on each active representation-size slice.

### Cheap-Handle Counterexamples Also Diagonalize Against Themselves

I then tried to repair the rare-slice false template under the depth-3
constraint that weak runtime cannot grow with the slice index. The natural
repair is to give slice $j$ a cheap weak handle of advantage about $s^{-j}$
and hide the rest of the target behind a hard residual problem. This still
does not give an atlas-ready witness.

First, the source must hold not only for the original rare-slice mixture, but
also for marginals concentrated on a single hard slice and for any marginal
that follows hard slices along a size diagonal. If the only weak edge available
on slice $j$ is $s^{-j}$, then a diagonal marginal selecting $j=s$ would have
super-polynomially small advantage and would violate the source, at least
under the size-indexed reading above. Under the weighted-global reading this
objection is weaker, but then the witness must state that convention
explicitly.

Second, because the target is improper, cheap handles are hard to keep
nonboostable. If the handle finder works under every residual marginal with
inverse-polynomial advantage, an improper learner can call it repeatedly and
vote. If the residual handle is made computationally hard to find, the weak
agnostic source fails on the marginal concentrated on that residual. Existing
witnesses fall on exactly this line:

- PRF, LPN, and random-label residuals give strong improper hardness, but they
  are not source-positive because no weak handle is available on the hard
  marginal.
- PCP active-slice and one-way coordinate constructions separate proper
  learning, but the improper target can use lookup tables or aggregate the
  explicit handles.
- Countable lookup examples can show marginal weak behavior, but they do not
  respect the sample-complexity collapse already recorded for this source.

So the missing false witness is not merely a hard slice plus one easy weak
predictor. It must be a genuinely nonboostable weak-handle family: every
isolated residual marginal has a cheap inverse-polynomial weak agnostic
predictor, while no distribution-free polynomial-time improper learner can
assemble enough such predictors to obtain small realizable error.

### Current Conclusion

I cannot resolve the edge at depth 4. The positive route is close if the atlas
permits size-diagonalization of marginals, but the existing scratchpad
distinction between resource transfer and accuracy transfer blocks using that
as a proof. The negative route is now narrower: slice-dependent runtimes are
blocked, and slice-dependent weak advantages are themselves vulnerable to
diagonal marginals unless the construction is carefully tied to a

## Depth 5 Attempt: Global-Marginal Diagonal And Addressed Handles

- depth: 5
- date: 2026-05-05
- worker note: appended only to this scratchpad; no atlas files edited.

### Conditional Positive Lemma

I tried to strengthen the depth-4 size-diagonal argument. The clean lemma is
true under a stronger, size-indexed marginal semantics:

**Lemma.** Suppose a marginal is formally a family $P=(P_s)_{s\ge 1}$ and the
source guarantee is evaluated on each representation-size slice $s$ under
$P_s$. Then the source weak gaps are distribution-free inverse-polynomial.

**Reason.** If no uniform gap exists for the fixed source learner $A$, then for
each degree $d$ choose a size $s_d$ and marginal $Q_d$ with weak advantage below
$s_d^{-d}$, choosing the $s_d$ increasing fast enough to absorb constants. Form
one diagonal marginal family by setting $P_{s_d}=Q_d$ and filling the other
sizes arbitrarily. The source guarantee for this single $P$ gives a
$P$-dependent polynomial lower bound $C s^{-D}$ on the advantage for all large
sizes. Taking $d>D$ contradicts the choice of $Q_d$. With this uniform weak gap
and the full-support transcript resource bound, ordinary realizable boosting
would give `resolved-true`.

I do not think the current atlas notes justify using this as the verdict. The
definition notes speak of one marginal $P$ over the instance space, and
`full-support-marginal-uniformization` explicitly says that accuracy is not
transferred from a global mixture to low-mass components. Under that
weighted-global reading, the diagonal construction becomes
$P=\sum_d w_d Q_d$. A concept that is hard only on component $Q_d$ can be easy
on the remaining mass, so its total realizable error can be far below $1/2$
even if $A$ has almost no edge on $Q_d$. Adding random labels outside $Q_d$
does not fix this in the agnostic source: the benchmark optimum rises by about
$(1-w_d)/2$, and the additive tolerance can still ignore the low-mass component.

Thus the positive proof is conditionally complete, but only for a semantics
stronger than the one currently recorded.

### Addressed-Code Cheap-Handle Attempt

The most concrete cheap-handle template I found is an addressed code family.
For parameters $n,k$, let $N=n^k$ and use points $(i,b)$ with
$i\in[N]$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$. A concept is determined by a succinct string or
seed $u$ whose expanded codeword is $G(u)\in\{0,1\}^N$, and

$$
c_u(i,b)=\mathbf 1[b=G(u)_i].
$$

Under the uniform marginal on this slice, the all-zero and all-one baselines
have error about $1/2$. One labeled example at coordinate $i$ reveals
$G(u)_i$, and the improper hypothesis that fixes both points $(i,0),(i,1)$ and
uses a baseline elsewhere has realizable advantage about $1/(2N)=n^{-k}/2$.
This is exactly the cheap-handle behavior wanted after depth 4: one handle is
printable in fixed polynomial time in the example length, but boosting by
coordinate handles needs about $N$ successful handles. If $G$ were made locally
unpredictable, a learner running in time $n^a$ with $a<k$ should not predict a
constant fraction of unseen coordinates, so the distribution-free strong target
would fail on a large enough slice.

This still does not close the counterexample.

First, the obvious agnostic source learner is ERM over the $2N$ one-coordinate
handles and the two baselines. That has sample/time polynomial in $N=n^k$,
which is precisely the slice-dependent resource behavior ruled out by the
full-support pathwise argument. The source learner may have a slice-dependent
gap, but not a slice-dependent runtime exponent.

Second, the constant-sample version is only a realizable intuition. For an
arbitrary agnostic joint distribution, a sampled coordinate can be noisy or can
have too little mass; making a high-confidence `OPT + 1/2 - \Omega(1/N)`
statement seems to require validation or empirical comparison among handles.
That again costs handle-scale samples unless one proves a new one-sample
agnostic selection lemma.

Third, this template exposes the same marginal-semantics fork as the positive
route. If marginals can follow the diagonal $k=k(n)$ with full mass at each
size, the handle gap $n^{-k(n)}$ is not inverse-polynomial for that one
marginal, so the source fails. If the intended semantics instead packs all
sizes into one weighted global distribution, then low-mass hard slices can be
ignored by the source, but the target-negative distribution must put constant
mass on a chosen hard slice. The witness must state and exploit exactly that
weighted-global convention.

### Current State

The addressed-code candidate is useful because it separates the two missing
ingredients. A false witness needs a fixed-polynomial weak agnostic finder whose
guarantee is not obtained by ERM over all handles, and it needs a hardness proof
for improper low-error prediction even after the learner sees polynomially many
cheap handles. Standard PRF-style hardness supplies the second ingredient only
by destroying the first: if predicting unseen coordinates is hard under a
residual marginal, then the weak agnostic source is usually hard on that
residual as well.

I therefore cannot honestly mark the edge true or false at depth 5. The
positive route is a theorem under size-indexed, condition-on-each-size
marginals; the negative route has a sharper toy model but still lacks an
atlas-valid agnostic source with fixed transcript-wise polynomial resources.

### Final Depth 6 Directions And Obstacles

1. Decide the marginal convention at the schema level. If marginals are
   size-indexed families, the diagonal weak-gap lemma above should be promoted
   and this edge should likely become `resolved-true`.

2. If marginals are weighted global distributions over all finite encodings,
   add an explicit no-transfer lemma: additive weak accuracy on a global mixture
   does not force any weak edge on low-mass components, even with agnostic
   relabeling. That would explain why the positive diagonal proof is invalid.

3. Try to make the addressed-code template into an oracle separation. The oracle
   should let one sampled handle be found in fixed polynomial time with an
   agnostic `OPT + 1/2 - n^{-k}` guarantee, while preventing any $n^a$ improper
   learner from assembling low error on slice $k>a$.

4. For a concrete class, look for locally unpredictable generators with an
   explicit, noise-tolerant handle finder. The obstacle is severe: a handle
   finder that works on every residual marginal is exactly what a slow booster
   can reuse.

5. Keep PRF, LPN, and random-code candidates as negative tests rather than
   witnesses. They make strong prediction hard, but they also tend to make the
   residual weak source false.

## Depth 6 Final Attempt: Fixed-Additive Boosting Helps, Uniform Gap Still Does Not Follow

- date: 2026-05-05

### Last Positive Route Checked

The one remaining route worth sharpening is that the fixed-additive agnostic
promise is not quite as hopeless as the early "threshold floor" discussion
made it sound. In $\{\pm1\}$ notation, the atlas source guarantee

$$
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(h)\le
\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}(\mathcal D)+\beta_P(s)
$$

is equivalent to

$$
\operatorname{corr}_{\mathcal D}(h,Y)
\ge
\sup_{c\in\mathcal C}\operatorname{corr}_{\mathcal D}(c,Y)-2\beta_P(s).
$$

Thus, for a fixed marginal $P$, it is a nontrivial correlation-style agnostic
weak learner with parameters $\gamma=1$, $\varepsilon_0=2\beta_P(s)$, and
nontriviality margin

$$
\theta_P(s)=\gamma-\varepsilon_0=1-2\beta_P(s)=2(1/2-\beta_P(s)).
$$

This matters because recent agnostic-boosting formulations are designed for
such nontrivial correlation gaps. So the most optimistic conditional theorem is
now:

**Conditional theorem.** If the atlas source learner can be used by a
marginal-preserving agnostic booster whose sample/time overhead is polynomial
in the source resource bound, $1/\theta_P(s)$, $1/\varepsilon$, and
$\log(1/\delta)$, then the source gives a marginal-nonuniform strong agnostic
improper learner. Restricting to realizable labels gives a
marginal-nonuniform strong realizable improper learner. If, in addition, the
weak gaps have one distribution-free inverse-polynomial lower bound, then
full-support resource uniformization plus this booster proves the assigned edge
true.

This is the strongest positive form I can isolate under the current atlas
semantics. It improves the diagnosis: fixed-additive weak agnostic learning is
not by itself the fatal obstacle for a fixed marginal. The fatal obstacle for
this assigned distribution-free target is still that the source gives only

$$
\forall P\ \exists r_P\quad \theta_P(s)\ge 1/r_P(s),
$$

while the target, or any distribution-free boosted proof of it, needs one
polynomial $r$ independent of $P$.

### Why The Final Positive Route Still Fails Under Current Semantics

Full-support marginal uniformization supplies one pathwise polynomial bound for
each call to the single source learner $A$. It does not supply a lower bound on
$\theta_P(s)$ for the actual marginal $P$.

The depth-4/depth-5 diagonal argument would close this gap if marginals were
size-indexed families and the source accuracy guarantee were evaluated
separately on each representation-size slice. Under that stronger semantics,
bad gaps $\theta_{Q_d}(s_d)<s_d^{-d}$ could be stitched into one diagonal
marginal family, contradicting the source guarantee for that one family.

The current atlas notes instead speak of a marginal over the instance space and
use full-support mixtures only for pathwise resource control. Under this
weighted-global reading, the diagonal construction becomes a mixture
$P=\sum_d w_dQ_d$. The source weak guarantee under $P$ may ignore components of
small total mass and still achieve error below $1/2-\gamma_P(s)$ on the
mixture. Relabeling tricks do not force accuracy on a low-mass component,
because the agnostic benchmark rises on the complementary mass by the same
order that the component's error is diluted. So the diagonal weak-gap
uniformization proof remains conditional on a schema change, not a theorem
under current atlas semantics.

### Last False-Witness Check

The addressed-code cheap-handle template is still the closest false shape, but
the final pass did not make it atlas-ready.

The intended slice has $N=n^k$ addressed coordinates. A single observed
coordinate gives a cheap hypothesis with advantage about $1/N=n^{-k}$, while a
strong learner would need enough handles, or enough prediction of unseen
coordinates, to drive error below $\varepsilon$. This would naturally create
unbounded weak-gap exponents without unbounded one-call runtime.

The unresolved blockers are decisive:

1. The source is agnostic and must hold for every marginal, including residual
   or coordinate-focused marginals. A one-sample handle intuition is not enough
   to certify an `OPT + 1/2 - n^{-k}` guarantee with high confidence under
   arbitrary noisy labels.

2. If the handle finder works on every residual marginal, then an improper
   boosted learner can usually reuse it. Recent nontrivial-gap agnostic
   boosting makes this sharper: a slice with gap $n^{-k}$ is slow to boost, but
   it is not intrinsically nonboostable.

3. If predicting unseen coordinates is made cryptographically or
   information-theoretically hard, the same hardness tends to make even weak
   prediction false on the marginal concentrated on that residual. This is why
   PRF, LPN, and random-code variants remain negative tests rather than
   witnesses.

4. A target-negative argument must rule out every fixed distribution-free
   exponent $n^a$ on some slice $k>a$, while the source must still be witnessed
   by one fixed-polynomial transcript-wise learner. I do not have a concrete
   class satisfying both requirements.

Thus I also cannot mark the edge false. The false witness needs genuinely
computationally nonboostable cheap handles, not just rare hard slices or
slice-dependent exhaustive search.
