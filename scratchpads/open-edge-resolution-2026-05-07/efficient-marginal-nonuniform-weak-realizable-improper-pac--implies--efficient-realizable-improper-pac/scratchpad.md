## Executive Verdict

I do not see an atlas-ready proof or counterexample under the current
deterministic-output, pathwise finite-transcript semantics. The edge should stay
`open`.

The clean part is now well isolated. A full-support reference marginal
uniformizes the sample and running time of one call to the single source learner,
because the atlas treats efficient resources as worst-case bounds on finite
encoded transcripts. What it does not uniformize is the weak advantage:

$$
\forall P\ \exists k(P)\quad
\operatorname{err}_P(A,c)\le 1/2-s^{-k(P)}.
$$

To obtain the target through Schapire/Freund boosting, we would need one
distribution-free inverse-polynomial lower bound, or at least one polynomial
envelope controlling every filtered/reweighted marginal generated by the
booster. The source definition gives no such envelope.

The strongest false route remains a rare-block PRF/oracle construction. Its
target lower bound is convincing for a distribution-free strong learner: choose
one hard block whose level exceeds the alleged global polynomial degree. But
the source proof still fails for deterministic final hypotheses. On a nearly
balanced hard block, a deterministic hypothesis must choose an effective
orientation for unseen coordinates, while the two opposite tiny-bias marginals
are statistically indistinguishable at any fixed pathwise polynomial sample
scale. Stochastic prediction-time hypotheses would repair this particular
source-side problem, but that is not the current atlas semantics.

## Primary-Source Anchors

- Schapire 1990 proves the constructive weak-to-strong PAC equivalence in the
  distribution-free model and explicitly uses filtering to change the
  distribution seen by the weak learner. This supports the atlas boosting edge,
  but also explains why a marginal-dependent weak gap is not automatically
  enough. Source: <https://www.schapire.net/papers/strengthofweak.pdf>.
- Schapire's PAC model also treats output hypotheses as prediction rules with
  polynomial-time evaluation. This is useful background for cryptographic lower
  bounds, although the current atlas notes have not made every evaluator
  convention explicit for arbitrary improper outputs.
- Freund 1995 gives boost-by-majority and notes an extension where weak accuracy
  may depend on the instance distribution. This is a near miss: the extension
  still assumes quantitative control over those distribution-dependent
  accuracies. Source: <https://doi.org/10.1006/inco.1995.1136>.
- Benedek and Itai 1991 study fixed-distribution learnability and emphasize that
  fixed-distribution sample learnability is not primarily a computational-time
  statement. Source: <https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(91)90026-X>.
- Hanneke, Moran, and Thiessen 2025 define marginal-nonuniform learning as rates
  that may depend on the marginal but hold uniformly over concepts, and prove a
  sample-rate trichotomy. This supports the sample-only collapse, not an
  efficient computational learner. Source: <https://openreview.net/forum?id=aoVCFtox89>.
- Kearns and Valiant 1994 give representation-independent cryptographic
  hardness even for weak prediction advantage, provided the learned hypotheses
  are polynomial-time evaluable. Source:
  <https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/papers/cryptojacm.pdf>.

## Positive Routes Checked

### Full-Support Uniformization

Let $A$ be the source learner. Applying the marginal-nonuniform source guarantee
to a full-support reference marginal $P^\star$ gives a pathwise polynomial bound
for $A$ on every finite sample transcript. Since every transcript that can occur
under any marginal also occurs with positive probability under $P^\star$ for a
suitable target labeling, this gives one distribution-free resource polynomial
for a single call to $A$.

This proves only the resource part of a distribution-free weak learner. For the
actual target marginal $P$, the available accuracy statement is still
$1/2-\gamma_P(s)$ with a $P$-dependent inverse-polynomial exponent. The target
requires arbitrary $\varepsilon$ in one distribution-free polynomial time bound.

### Ordinary Boosting

Schapire/Freund boosting would prove the edge if the source already gave
`efficient-weak-realizable-improper-pac`, or if every booster-induced marginal
$Q_t$ had a weak gap bounded below by one polynomial depending only on the
original target parameters.

The source gives only:

$$
\forall Q\ \exists k(Q)\quad \gamma_Q(s)\ge s^{-k(Q)}.
$$

The booster needs a usable bound of the opposite flavor:

$$
\exists K\ \forall Q_t\quad \gamma_{Q_t}(s)\ge s^{-K}.
$$

No local compactness, bounded-density, KL-stability, or continuity assumption in
the current source note bridges this quantifier gap.

### Sample-Only Collapse

Ignoring computation, the corresponding sample-efficient edge is true in the
atlas. Hanneke--Moran--Thiessen style marginal-nonuniform rate results plus
standard VC theory give the distribution-free sample-efficient target.

This does not resolve the present edge. The efficient target needs a polynomial
time learner, and the sample-only proof may use unrestricted ERM over the concept
class. A cryptographic finite-VC class can be sample-efficient but fail even
efficient weak learning, so sample control alone cannot supply the missing
algorithm.

### Compactness At Fixed Size

For a fixed finite size slice and a fixed bounded-transcript learner, compactness
might give a positive minimum weak advantage over marginals on that slice. Even
if this holds, the minimum can shrink faster than every inverse polynomial as the
size grows.

A stronger size-indexed marginal semantics might then allow diagonal stitching:
bad size slices could be assembled into one marginal family that keeps full mass
on the active size slice. Under the current global-marginal reading, stitching
creates a mixture, and low-mass hard slices can be hidden in additive PAC error.
This is exactly why full support uniformizes pathwise resources but not risk
gaps.

## Counterexample Routes Checked

### Plain PRF Classes

Plain pseudorandom function classes are not witnesses. They fail the target
under a hard marginal, but that same hard marginal also rules out the source's
efficient marginal-nonuniform weak learner. A source learner with inverse-
polynomial advantage on the PRF hard marginal would distinguish pseudorandom
labels from random labels.

### PCP And Lookup Classes

The PCP active-slice and clause-satisfaction lookup witnesses separate proper
from improper learning. They do not refute this edge because both source and
target are improper. On polynomial-size active slices, improper memorization or
lookup-table ERM gives strong realizable learning rather than a target failure.

### One-Way Image-Coordinate Handles

The one-way image-coordinate and weak-handle witnesses also separate proper
targets. For this improper target, sparse memorization of observed positive atoms
is enough for strong realizable learning on the usual finite-coordinate blocks.
The one-way hardness forces recovery of a seed only when the final hypothesis is
proper.

### Rare-Block PRF/Oracle Candidate

This remains the best false template. A distribution-free strong learner with a
global polynomial degree $D$ could be challenged on a single block of level
$j>D+10$. With only $s^D$ samples, it sees too few coordinates to predict unseen
random-oracle or PRF labels to constant error. This target-side lower bound is
cleaner than for marginal-nonuniform strong targets because the hard marginal can
concentrate on one block.

The source side is the blocker. The intended weak learner memorizes sampled
coordinate pairs and uses a default behavior on unseen pairs. For deterministic
outputs, a nearly balanced hard block defeats this.

Take two realizable marginals $P_+$ and $P_-$ on the same block, with positive
mass $1/2+\eta$ and $1/2-\eta$, where $\eta$ is smaller than what a fixed
pathwise polynomial sample budget can estimate. A deterministic hypothesis that
uses the wrong unseen-coordinate orientation has error above $1/2$, and sampled
handles correct only $O(m/r)$ mass. But the $m$-sample transcript distributions
under $P_+$ and $P_-$ have total variation $o(1)$ when $r\gg m^2$, so no fixed
polynomial-time learner can choose the right orientation with high probability
for both.

Prediction-time stochastic hypotheses would remove this exact orientation
problem: memorize seen coordinate pairs and flip a fresh fair coin on unseen
pairs, gaining about half the covered mass without choosing a default sign. But
the current atlas nodes speak about outputting a hypothesis $h$ with ordinary
zero-one error; they do not install stochastic final-hypothesis semantics.

## Depth 2 Branch A: Distribution-Free Weak-Gap Uniformization

### Question

Test the following possible proof route:

1. Use the full-support reference marginal to make one call to the source
   learner pathwise polynomial-time and pathwise polynomial-sample
   distribution-free.
2. Try to infer a distribution-free inverse-polynomial weak advantage.
3. Feed that weak learner into Schapire/Freund boosting to obtain the strong
   distribution-free improper target.

The first step is valid under the current atlas convention. The branch question
is whether the second step is forced by the current global marginal semantics.

### Formal Shape Of The Needed Uniformization

After full-support resource uniformization, fix the single source learner $A$
and let

$$
\Delta_A(P,s)
=
\inf_{c\in\mathcal C_s}
\left(\frac12-\operatorname{err}_P(A(P\text{-samples labeled by }c),c)\right),
$$

with the usual PAC confidence bookkeeping suppressed. The source gives the
nonuniform statement

$$
\forall P\ \exists K(P)\ \forall s\quad
\Delta_A(P,s)\ge s^{-K(P)}.
$$

The distribution-free weak learner needed for ordinary boosting would require

$$
\exists K\ \forall P\ \forall s\quad
\Delta_A(P,s)\ge s^{-K}.
$$

The full-support argument changes the resource quantifier from
`for each marginal, one pathwise polynomial` to `one pathwise polynomial for all
marginals`. It does not change the displayed accuracy quantifiers.

### Clean Obstruction

The obstruction is that support controls transcripts, but weak advantage is
mass-sensitive.

Let $R=(1-w)E+wQ$ be a full-support or mixture marginal, and suppose a hypothesis
$h$ is correct on the easy component $E$ and wrong on the hard component $Q$.
Then

$$
\operatorname{err}_R(h)=w,
$$

so if $w=1/2-s^{-j}$, $h$ has weak advantage $s^{-j}$ under $R$, while it has no
useful performance at all under $Q$. Thus weak correctness under a full-support
mixture gives no componentwise weak correctness. This is unlike strong PAC
error, where an error bound of order $w\varepsilon$ under $R$ would imply an
$\varepsilon$ bound under $Q$.

This kills the most direct transfer idea:

$$
\text{full support of }P^\star
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{all transcripts are resource-controlled}
$$

but not

$$
\text{weak advantage under }P^\star
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{weak advantage under every component or target marginal.}
$$

Since the source separately promises a weak gap for each target marginal $Q$,
we do get some $K(Q)$. The present semantics provide no mechanism forcing the
exponents $K(Q)$ to be bounded as $Q$ varies.

### Why Diagonalizing Bad Marginals Does Not Close The Gap

The tempting contradiction proof is:

1. assume no uniform exponent exists;
2. choose marginals $Q_j$ whose best guaranteed weak gaps are around $s^{-j}$;
3. stitch them into one global marginal $R=\sum_j w_jQ_j$;
4. apply the source guarantee to $R$ and extract a uniform exponent.

Under the current global-marginal reading, step 4 does not force what we need.
A weak learner for $R$ may spend its entire advantage on finitely many heavy or
easy components and ignore low-mass hard components. The guarantee for $R$ says
only that the aggregate error is below $1/2$ by $R$'s own inverse-polynomial
gap. It does not say every component inherits an inverse-polynomial gap, and
weak error has no component-transfer lemma analogous to the strong
$w\varepsilon$ calculation.

This is the cleanest current obstruction: global mixtures dilute hard slices
exactly at the point where weak boosting needs a quantitative gap.

### Why Guess-And-Validate Does Not Repair It

A booster could try gap guesses

$$
\gamma=s^{-1},s^{-2},s^{-3},\ldots
$$

and validate the boosted candidates on fresh examples from the original target
marginal. This eventually works for a fixed marginal with exponent $K(P)$, but
for constant target accuracy the stopping time is roughly polynomial with an
exponent depending on $K(P)$. That gives a marginal-nonuniform strong learner,
not a distribution-free polynomial-time learner. Validation identifies success;
it does not impose a global cap on how far down the gap schedule must be tried.

### Conditional True Variants

The edge becomes true under any of the following extra hypotheses.

**Uniform weak-gap axiom.** If, after full-support resource uniformization, the
source learner satisfies

$$
\exists K\ \forall P,c,s\quad
\operatorname{err}_P(A,c)\le \frac12-s^{-K},
$$

then $A$ is an efficient distribution-free weak realizable improper learner.
Schapire/Freund boosting gives the target with an improper majority/vote
hypothesis.

**Global booster-envelope axiom.** It is enough to assume the same global
inverse-polynomial lower bound only for the filtered or reweighted marginals
that a fixed distribution-free booster can generate:

$$
\exists K\ \forall Q\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{boost}}\quad
\gamma_Q(s)\ge s^{-K}.
$$

This is weaker than a gap bound for all marginals, but it is still stronger than
the current source definition.

**Smooth-boosting plus distribution-free stability.** If the booster is smooth,
so every induced marginal $Q$ satisfies

$$
Q\ll P,
\qquad
\|dQ/dP\|_\infty\le B(s,1/\varepsilon),
$$

and the source learner has a distribution-free stability modulus

$$
\gamma_Q(s)\ge 1/r(s,B)
$$

for one polynomial $r$ independent of the original marginal $P$, then boosting
is distribution-free polynomial-time. If $r$ is allowed to depend on $P$, the
same argument gives only a marginal-nonuniform strong target.

**Size-conditional marginal semantics.** If the atlas semantics were changed so
that a marginal is a size-indexed active-slice family and a bad slice cannot be
hidden by assigning it tiny global mass, then the diagonal stitching argument
could plausibly force a uniform exponent. Under the current global-mixture
semantics, that proof is blocked by dilution.

**Finite or compact generated marginal family with effective modulus.** If, for
each size and accuracy request, the booster-generated marginals lie in an
effectively compact family and $Q\mapsto\gamma_Q$ has a computable positive
polynomial lower envelope on that family, then the usual boosting proof goes
through. This is a regularity assumption on the learner/distribution family, not
a consequence of marginal-nonuniform learnability as currently defined.

### Branch Conclusion

Current global marginal semantics plus full-support pathwise resource bounds do
not yield an atlas-ready distribution-free weak-gap uniformization theorem.
They uniformize how long one call to the weak learner can run, but they do not
uniformize how much advantage that call has. The clean obstruction is the
failure of weak advantage to transfer from a full-support mixture to its
components, together with the ability of global mixtures to dilute arbitrarily
bad slices.

This branch therefore does not resolve the edge true or false. It isolates the
extra assumptions that would make the true proof immediate, and it confirms
that ordinary boosting cannot be invoked from the current source without an
additional global gap envelope.

## Depth 3 Branch A: Rare-Block Evaluator Counterexample Check

### Question

Try to make the rare-block PRF or random-oracle construction into a valid
`false` witness for the distribution-free strong target under the current atlas
semantics.

The key distinction from the marginal-strong sibling is that the target here is
distribution-free. A target lower bound does not need one fixed rare mixture: it
may challenge the alleged learner on a single hard block whose level exceeds the
degree of the learner's global polynomial. The question is therefore whether the
source side and the output-evaluator conventions can be made current-semantics
valid.

### Candidate

Use blocks

$$
B_{n,j}=\{(n,j,u,b):u\in U_{n,j},\ b\in\{0,1\}\},
\qquad |U_{n,j}|=N_{n,j}=n^j.
$$

For a PRF key $k$, define the active-block concept

$$
c_{n,j,k}(n',j',u,b)=1
\Longleftrightarrow
(n',j')=(n,j)\text{ and }b=F_k(u),
$$

and label all off-block points by $0$. The random-oracle version replaces
$F_k(u)$ by an independent random bit for each active coordinate $u$.

The intended weak source learner tries constants and sample-discovered
coordinate handles. The intended target lower bound says that strong learning
must predict many unseen active-block labels, which is impossible for a random
oracle and PRF-hard for polynomial-time evaluable outputs.

### Distribution-Free Target Side

This half is strong if an efficient-output evaluator convention is available.

Suppose a distribution-free strong improper learner $L$ has global polynomial
sample and time bound $q(s,1/\varepsilon,\log(1/\delta))$ of degree $D$. Choose
a fixed level $j>D+10$, put the test marginal uniformly on $B_{n,j}$, and ask
for constant error, say $\varepsilon=1/64$.

For large $n$, $L$ sees only $\operatorname{poly}(n)=o(n^j)$ active coordinates.
In the random-oracle hybrid, a fresh unseen coordinate has a label independent
of the transcript, so a transcript-computable output cannot have conditional
block error below a constant. In the PRF version, any low-error hypothesis whose
predictions can be evaluated in polynomial time gives a polynomial-time
predictor for $F_k(u)$ on fresh unseen $u$, and hence a standard PRF
distinguisher.

This isolated-block proof is cleaner than the rare-mixture proof needed for a
marginal-nonuniform strong target. The rare-block construction is therefore not
blocked by the distribution-free target. It is blocked by source semantics and
by the missing explicit evaluator convention.

### Evaluator Convention Needed

The PRF lower bound needs to run the learner's printed improper hypothesis on
fresh pairs

$$
h(n,j,u,0),\qquad h(n,j,u,1).
$$

Current atlas text explicitly bounds sample use and training/output-generation
time, and it uses the ordinary language that the learner outputs a hypothesis
$h$. I still do not see a global statement that every efficient improper output
comes with a uniform polynomial-time prediction procedure.

That convention is standard for computational PAC lower bounds, and existing
cryptographic witness notes implicitly rely on it. But for this edge, it is a
real formal assumption: without polynomial-time evaluation, the PRF reduction is
not a polynomial-time distinguisher. The random-oracle version also needs an
output convention preventing a short semantic description from getting free
access to unseen oracle labels. A learner-computable evaluator supplies the
right restriction.

Thus the target half can be recorded only under one of these explicit policies:

- secure PRFs exist, and efficient improper outputs are polynomial-time
  evaluable from their printed representations;
- or the atlas accepts a random-oracle/relativized lower-bound policy together
  with the same learner-computable output convention.

### Deterministic Source Blocker

The harder problem is the source. Under current deterministic-output semantics,
the rare-block sample-handle lemma still fails.

After full-support pathwise resource uniformization, any one-call source learner
has a fixed polynomial sample bound at fixed confidence; write it as

$$
m(s)\le s^T.
$$

Choose $j$ and $n$ so that $r=n^j\gg m(s)^2$ on one active block. Fix any
active labeling $f:[r]\to\{0,1\}$ and consider two realizable marginals on the
same block:

$$
P_+(u,f(u))=\frac{1+2\eta}{2r},
\qquad
P_+(u,1-f(u))=\frac{1-2\eta}{2r},
$$

with $P_-$ obtained by swapping the two weights. Take

$$
\eta=10m(s)/r.
$$

Under $P_+$ the better unseen-coordinate default is the positive orientation;
under $P_-$ it is the negative orientation. A deterministic handle that is
correct on sampled coordinates but uses the wrong default on unseen coordinates
gets back only $O(m(s)/r)$ mass from the sample handles, so its error is still
at least $1/2$ for one of the two marginals.

The learner cannot reliably choose opposite defaults for the two marginals:

$$
\operatorname{TV}(P_+^{m(s)},P_-^{m(s)})
\le O(m(s)\eta)
=O(m(s)^2/r)
=o(1).
$$

So any deterministic learner whose unseen behavior is essentially a default
orientation fails on at least one of $P_+$ or $P_-$.

Allowing arbitrary deterministic improper outputs does not obviously evade this
obstruction. On an unseen coordinate pair, the output either behaves like a
constant/default orientation on substantial mass, or it attempts to guess the
hidden bit $f(u)$. The first case faces the $P_+$/$P_-$ indistinguishability
test. The second case is unavailable in the random-oracle core, and in the PRF
core it is exactly the kind of unseen-label correlation the target lower bound
is supposed to rule out. This is not a complete impossibility theorem for every
possible deterministic gadget, but it blocks the current rare-block
singleton/paired-handle witness.

### What Would Make The Counterexample Work

**Polynomial-time evaluable outputs.** Necessary for the PRF target reduction.
Not sufficient by itself, because the deterministic source lemma still fails.

**Stochastic prediction-time hypotheses.** This repairs the orientation problem.
The weak learner can memorize sampled coordinate fibers and use fresh fair
coins on unseen fibers. If $W$ is the marginal mass of covered fibers, then
conditional on the training transcript,

$$
\operatorname{err}(h,c)=\frac12(1-W)=\frac12-\frac W2.
$$

This is orientation-free for both $P_+$ and $P_-$. Training-time randomness
fixed into one deterministic output is not enough: when $r\gg m(s)^2$, the
random fixed labeling fluctuation is larger than the desired $m(s)/r$ edge.
Fresh prediction-time randomness is doing the work. The current atlas nodes do
not include this final-hypothesis semantics.

**Deterministic orientation assumption or certificate.** A deterministic source
proof would need an efficiently findable signal telling the learner which
unseen orientation to use, or a new deterministic orientation-free handle. Such
a certificate must have enough mass to be found under every relevant marginal,
but not enough structure for the distribution-free strong learner to exploit on
the isolated hard block. I do not see such a gadget. Simply assuming the
orientation is known, or excluding near-balanced $P_+$/$P_-$ marginals, would
change the arbitrary-marginal source semantics.

**Weaker resource convention.** If marginal-nonuniform efficiency were
distributional rather than pathwise on finite transcripts, the source learner
could spend marginal-dependent samples to estimate the tiny orientation bias.
That is not the current atlas convention and would also undo the full-support
resource-uniformization diagnosis used by neighboring notes.

### Branch Conclusion

The rare-block construction does not give a valid current-semantics `false`
witness for this edge. The distribution-free target lower bound is the easy
half: with evaluable outputs, one isolated hard block defeats any alleged global
polynomial strong learner. The source half is the blocker. Current deterministic
outputs must effectively choose an unseen-coordinate orientation, and opposite
tiny-bias marginals are statistically indistinguishable at the allowed
pathwise-polynomial sample scale.

The construction becomes a plausible conditional counterexample only after
adding convention changes: polynomial-time evaluable improper outputs for the
PRF reduction, and either stochastic final hypotheses or a new deterministic
orientation-free/certified weak handle for the source. Without those changes, no
atlas-ready rare-block PRF or random-oracle witness is available from this
branch.

### Consolidation Goal

The depth-3 branch separated the two halves of the rare-block strategy. For a
distribution-free strong target, the target lower bound is not the main
obstacle: one may challenge any alleged global polynomial learner on a single
hard block whose level exceeds that polynomial's degree. The hard question is
whether the same class is source-positive for marginal-nonuniform weak
realizable improper PAC learning under the current atlas semantics.

This branch consolidates the answer for the deterministic source side. The
current rare-block PRF/random-oracle template does not give an atlas-ready
`false` edge, because every deterministic weak-handle construction I can make
explicit either has to choose an unseen-coordinate orientation, or has to
predict the hidden hard labels that the target lower bound needs to keep hard.

### Target Hardness Is The Easy Half

Assume the usual computational-PAC convention that an efficient improper output
has a polynomial-time evaluator. Then a rare-block hard core gives the right
distribution-free target obstruction.

For an alleged strong learner with global polynomial degree $D$, choose a block
of size $r=s^j$ with $j\gg D$, put the marginal uniformly on that block, and
ask for a constant error bound. The learner sees only $m=\operatorname{poly}(s)$
fibers, with $m=o(r)$. In a random-oracle block, unseen labels are independent
of the transcript. In a PRF block, a low-error efficiently evaluable hypothesis
would predict the PRF on fresh unseen inputs and hence gives a distinguisher.

So the rare-block construction is not defeated by the distribution-free target.
The target can always isolate one sufficiently large hard block. The failure is
that the same block has not been made weakly learnable for every marginal by a
deterministic pathwise-polynomial source learner.

### Deterministic Source Obstruction

By full-support transcript uniformization, a single call to the source learner
has a distribution-free pathwise sample/time bound at fixed confidence. Write
the sample bound as $m(s)$.

On a paired hard block with fibers $(u,0),(u,1)$ and target

$$
c_f(u,b)=1\Longleftrightarrow b=f(u),
$$

choose $r\gg m(s)^2$. For a small bias parameter $\eta=\Theta(m(s)/r)$, compare
two realizable marginals on the same target:

$$
P_+(u,f(u))=\frac{1+2\eta}{2r},\qquad
P_+(u,1-f(u))=\frac{1-2\eta}{2r},
$$

and let $P_-$ swap the two weights. Under $P_+$ the better unseen default has
one orientation; under $P_-$ it has the opposite orientation. But the two
sample transcript distributions satisfy

$$
\operatorname{TV}(P_+^{m(s)},P_-^{m(s)})\le O(m(s)\eta)
=O(m(s)^2/r)=o(1).
$$

Thus a deterministic learner cannot reliably output opposite unseen
orientations for $P_+$ and $P_-$. The sampled fibers contribute only
$O(m(s)/r)$ mass. If the unseen part is oriented incorrectly, that contribution
is too small to rescue a weak edge for one of the two marginals. If the unseen
part is not oriented at all, the deterministic output must still instantiate
some fixed binary behavior, and an arbitrary marginal can expose the side on
which that behavior is wrong.

This is the source-side reason the hard-block target lower bound does not
produce a source-positive class. The source is required to work for all
marginals, including nearly balanced opposite-bias marginals concentrated on
one hard block. Under pathwise resources, it cannot spend the
$\Omega(\eta^{-2})$ samples needed to estimate the orientation when $j$ is
unbounded.

### Alternate Deterministic Handles Checked

**Public default orientation.** If the block carries an efficiently readable
orientation bit or certificate, the source learner can use it. But then a
distribution-free strong learner challenged on the same isolated block can also
use it, unless the certificate is too rare to find. If it is too rare, an
arbitrary marginal concentrating away from the certificate revives the source
failure.

**Deterministic balanced hash or tie-breaker.** A fixed hash can make the
unseen predictions look balanced under a friendly uniform marginal, but the
source quantifies over all marginals. A marginal may put more weight on the
coordinates where the hash has the wrong relation to $f$, or may choose the
opposite tiny orientation. The fixed rule is still one deterministic rule after
training.

**Training-time random seed fixed into the output.** This does not give
prediction-time stochasticity. Once the seed is fixed, the output is an
ordinary deterministic hypothesis. On a large block, the random unseen
fluctuation is typically of order $r^{-1/2}$, while the memorized-sample edge is
only $m(s)/r$. In the regime $r\gg m(s)^2$, the fluctuation swamps the intended
weak edge, so high-probability source success fails for some marginal.

**Richer deterministic improper circuits.** Improperness allows the source to
print arbitrary binary hypotheses, not just proper handles. But on unseen
fibers such a circuit either behaves like a deterministic orientation/default
rule, which faces the $P_+$/$P_-$ indistinguishability test, or it correlates
with the hidden labels $f(u)$. The latter is exactly what the PRF or
random-oracle target lower bound must rule out. I do not see a third
deterministic behavior that is both orientation-free for the source and
label-uninformative for the target.

These checks are not a theorem excluding every possible deterministic
counterexample. They do close the natural rare-block handle family: constants,
sampled lookup handles, public tie-breakers, fixed randomized tables, and
general deterministic circuits do not give the needed source-positive,
target-negative witness without adding another ingredient.

### Conditional Evaluator And Stochastic Forks

**Evaluator convention.** The PRF target reduction needs polynomial-time
evaluation of the printed improper hypothesis. With that convention, the
isolated hard-block lower bound is a plausible conditional target-negative
argument. Without it, the learner's output may be only a semantic hypothesis,
and the PRF distinguisher cannot necessarily query it on fresh inputs. This
evaluator issue affects the target reduction; it does not repair the
deterministic source obstruction.

**Fresh prediction-time randomness.** If the source node were changed so that a
learner may output a stochastic final predictor and risk is measured in
expectation over fresh prediction coins, then the rare-block source side becomes
much easier. The learner can memorize sampled fibers and flip a fresh fair coin
on unseen fibers. If $W$ is the marginal mass of sampled fibers, its conditional
expected error is

$$
\frac12(1-W)=\frac12-\frac W2,
$$

independent of the unseen orientation. This gives a marginal-dependent weak
edge without estimating the tiny sign. But this is a genuine model fork, not a
derandomization of the current deterministic PAC semantics.

**Distributional or marginal-advice resources.** If the source efficiency
bound were allowed to be distributional rather than pathwise on finite
transcripts, or if the learner received marginal advice, it could spend
marginal-dependent resources to estimate tiny orientations. That also changes
the atlas convention and would undermine the full-support resource
uniformization used throughout these edges.

### Conditional True Weak-Gap Envelopes

The positive route is still exactly weak-gap uniformization.

**Uniform envelope.** If the source learner satisfies one distribution-free
inverse-polynomial gap

$$
\exists r\text{ polynomial}\ \forall P,c,s\qquad
\operatorname{err}_P(A,c)\le \frac12-\frac1{r(s)}
$$

on realizable labels, then the edge is true. Full-support transcript
uniformization supplies one distribution-free resource bound for a weak call,
and Schapire/Freund boosting converts the resulting weak learner into an
efficient strong realizable learner. The final vote is legal because the target
is improper.

**Size-indexed marginal semantics.** If marginals were size-indexed families
$P=(P_s)_{s\ge 1}$ evaluated with full mass at size $s$, unbounded bad weak-gap

the marginal-dependent polynomial promised for that family. That would force
the uniform envelope above. The current global-marginal semantics do not allow
this proof, because a mixture $\sum_j w_jQ_j$ can hide each bad block under
small mass $w_j$, and an additive weak guarantee may ignore low-mass bad
components.

**Booster-local envelope.** A weaker sufficient condition would be a single
polynomial gap bound for all reweighted or filtered marginals produced by a
specific boosting procedure. This would be enough for that reduction, but it is
not present in the source definition, and it still needs a distribution-free
quantitative bound to reach the present target.

### Goal

Depth 4 showed that constants plus sampled paired handles do not prove the
source side of the rare-block PRF/oracle counterexample. This pass checks the
stronger escape route:

> Maybe a more general deterministic improper output can avoid choosing an
> unseen-coordinate orientation while still not predicting hidden labels well
> enough to destroy the target lower bound.

The answer is negative for the natural random-label paired block. One can turn
the orientation obstruction into a local no-go lemma for arbitrary deterministic
outputs on that block. This does not prove the edge true, because it only rules
out the leading random-label witness family. It does make clear that a false
witness needs a genuinely different deterministic weak signal, not just a more
flexible improper hypothesis language.

### Local Setup

Fix one block with $r$ coordinate fibers:

$$
B_r=\{(u,b):u\in[r],\ b\in\{0,1\}\}.
$$

For a hidden labeling $f:[r]\to\{0,1\}$, define

$$
c_f(u,b)=1 \Longleftrightarrow b=f(u).
$$

By full-support pathwise resource uniformization, any source learner that could
work under the current atlas semantics has, at fixed confidence, one
distribution-free sample bound $m=m(s)$ for this weak call. The hard-block
regime is

$$
r\gg m(s)^2.
$$

For a small bias parameter $\eta=\Theta(m/r)$, define two marginals on the same
target:

$$
P_+(u,f(u))=\frac{1+2\eta}{2r},\qquad
P_+(u,1-f(u))=\frac{1-2\eta}{2r},
$$

and let $P_-$ swap the two weights. Thus $P_+$ and $P_-$ have opposite tiny
label-mass biases, but their $m$-sample transcript distributions have total
variation $O(m\eta)=O(m^2/r)=o(1)$.

The question is whether an arbitrary deterministic improper hypothesis can
beat both $P_+$ and $P_-$ without either estimating this tiny sign or predicting
$f$ on many unseen fibers.

### Decomposing An Arbitrary Deterministic Output

Condition on a training transcript $T$. On an unseen fiber $u$, the output
hypothesis has one of four local behaviors:

$$
(h(u,0),h(u,1))\in\{(0,0),(1,1),(1,0),(0,1)\}.
$$

It is useful to express the behavior relative to the hidden target:

$$
a_u=h(u,f(u)),\qquad b_u=h(u,1-f(u)).
$$

Then the accuracy contribution of fiber $u$ under $P_\sigma$, where
$\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}$, is

$$
\frac{1}{2r}\left[1+(a_u-b_u)+2\sigma\eta(a_u+b_u-1)\right].
$$

So the advantage over $1/2$ decomposes as

$$
\frac{1}{2r}\sum_u (a_u-b_u)
+\frac{\sigma\eta}{r}\sum_u (a_u+b_u-1).
$$

The two sums have distinct meanings.

The first sum is the actual orientation correlation with $f$. Sampled fibers
can contribute at most $m$ to it, because the labels of at most $m$ fibers have
been revealed. On unseen fibers in a random-oracle block, any deterministic
orientation guess has mean-zero correlation with $f$ conditional on $T$.

The second sum is the constant-default imbalance. A local behavior $(0,0)$
contributes $-1$, a local behavior $(1,1)$ contributes $+1$, and oriented
guesses contribute $0$. But the adversary may choose between $P_+$ and $P_-$.
Thus one of the two tiny-bias marginals makes this whole constant-default term
nonpositive, with magnitude $\eta|\sum_u(a_u+b_u-1)|/r$ against the learner.

This captures the deterministic dichotomy precisely:

- If the output uses constants on substantial unseen mass, one of $P_+$ and
  $P_-$ turns that into the wrong default orientation.
- If the output avoids constants and orients many unseen fibers, then its
  success depends on real correlation with the hidden labels $f$ on unseen
  fibers.

### Why Random-Label Blocks Cannot Supply The Source

Take $f$ to be random on the block. Conditional on any transcript and any fixed
training coins, the hidden labels on unseen fibers are still independent. The
unseen part of

$$
\sum_u(a_u-b_u)
$$

is therefore a mean-zero sum over the fibers on which the output tries to make
an oriented prediction, while sampled fibers add at most $m$ favorable units.
In the regime $r\gg m^2$, the typical fluctuation of the unseen orientation
sum is order $\sqrt r$, much larger than the intended handle advantage scale
$m$.

For a fixed deterministic output, there is constant probability over the
unseen random labels that this orientation sum is at most $-m$. On that event,
even the best sampled-fiber correction cannot make the first term positive.
Choosing the worse of $P_+$ and $P_-$ also makes the constant-default term
nonpositive. Hence, for that sign, the hypothesis has error at least $1/2$ on
the block.

Equivalently, averaged over a random oracle labeling, no $m$-sample
deterministic learner can, with PAC confidence bounded away from $1/2$, produce
a deterministic hypothesis that has positive weak advantage for both $P_+$ and
$P_-$. By the probabilistic method, some fixed hard labeling $f$ and one of the
two realizable marginals make the learner fail the source weak requirement.

This argument covers arbitrary deterministic improper outputs, not just the
explicit constants-plus-handles learner. The only ways for the local block to
avoid it are exactly the convention-changing routes already isolated:

- use fresh prediction-time randomness, so unseen fibers contribute exactly
  $1/2$ error instead of a fixed deterministic fluctuation;
- spend enough samples to estimate the sign of $\eta$, which is forbidden by
  the pathwise resource uniformization when $r\gg m^2$;
- give the learner deterministic unseen-label correlation with $f$, which
  destroys the random-oracle target lower bound and, in the PRF version, is
  precisely what would become a distinguisher.

### Why This Still Does Not Prove The Edge True

The no-go lemma is local to random-label paired blocks. It rules out the
cleanest false witness: rare hard blocks whose source learner gets its weak
edge only by sampled handles and whose target lower bound needs unseen labels
to remain pseudorandom.

It does not imply a general weak-to-strong theorem. A different concept class
might have deterministic weak signals that are neither constant defaults nor
unseen guesses of random labels. For example, a class could in principle carry
public weak certificates whose marginal mass gives an inverse-polynomial edge
depending on $P$, while aggregating enough certificates for strong learning is
computationally hard. I do not currently have such a construction, and the
obvious versions either become boostable or fail the arbitrary-marginal source
test, but the local random-block no-go does not exclude them.

The positive route also remains blocked at the same quantifier:

$$
\forall Q\ \exists k(Q)\quad \gamma_Q(s)\ge s^{-k(Q)}
$$

does not produce

$$
\exists K\ \forall Q\quad \gamma_Q(s)\ge s^{-K}.
$$

Validation over gap guesses gives only a marginal-dependent running exponent.
Boosting over filtered or reweighted marginals still needs one quantitative
envelope for the weak gaps it will encounter. The source definition supplies
no continuity, bounded-density, compactness, or size-conditional diagonal
principle that would force such an envelope under the current global-marginal
semantics.

### Fixed-Size Compactness Gives The Wrong Bound

After full-support transcript uniformization, one call to the source learner
has one pathwise polynomial sample/time bound. Fix a representation size $s$.
If the size-$s$ instance slice and concept slice are finite, the learner's
success probability and risk are functions of the marginal on a finite simplex.
For a fixed learner $A$, define the best guaranteed weak advantage at size $s$
schematically as

$$
\lambda_s(A)
= \inf_{P \text{ on the size-}s\text{ slice}}
  \inf_{c\in\mathcal C_s}
  \left(\frac12-\operatorname{err}_P(A,c)\right),
$$

with the confidence parameter folded into the usual repetition/validation
bookkeeping.

The source promise implies that each isolated size-slice marginal has some
positive weak advantage. Under finiteness and continuity assumptions this
suggests $\lambda_s(A)>0$ for each fixed $s$. But the target needs much more:

$$
\lambda_s(A) \ge s^{-K}
$$

for one constant $K$ independent of the marginal and of $s$. Compactness gives
positivity at a fixed size, not an inverse-polynomial lower envelope across
sizes. The minimum can be $2^{-s}$, $s^{-s}$, or worse without contradicting the
isolated-marginal source statement: a marginal concentrated on one hard size
is allowed to have an exponent depending on that marginal, hence depending on
the selected size.

This identifies a real logical gap in the positive proof. To turn the
compactness observation into boosting, one would need an effective quantitative
modulus:

$$
\inf_s s^K\lambda_s(A)>0
$$

for some universal $K$. The current source definition contains no such modulus.

### Why Global Marginals Do Not Diagonalize The Gap Away

The tempting repair is to choose sizes $s_j$ where
$\lambda_{s_j}(A)<s_j^{-j}$, stitch the corresponding hard marginals into one
global marginal $R$, and apply the source guarantee to $R$. This still does not
force a contradiction under the current global-marginal reading.

A target concept of size $s_j$ can be made hard only on its own active block.
If $R$ gives that block mass $w_j$, then a weak learner for $R$ may obtain its
entire advantage from the complement of the block, or may ignore the block when
$w_j$ is small. Weak learning is additive around $1/2$: a low-mass bad slice can
be hidden without violating an aggregate inverse-polynomial edge. This is the
same dilution phenomenon as in earlier branches, but the fixed-size compactness
view makes the failure sharper. The diagonal argument would need size-indexed
full-mass marginals, not global mixtures.

Thus there is no current-semantics proof that marginal-dependent weak gaps
uniformize to a distribution-free polynomial gap. Guessing the gap and
validating boosted candidates also stops at the same point: it gives runtime
with exponent depending on the original marginal's hidden gap, not one
distribution-free polynomial.

The size-local hidden-gap template is exactly a way this envelope could fail:

### Last True Attempt: Boosting Needs A Weak-Gap Envelope

The clean positive route has two parts.

source learner to one full-support reference marginal uniformizes the resources

arbitrary marginal also has positive probability under the reference marginal,

\gamma_P(s)\ge s^{-k(P)}

for the weak advantage of the fixed learner on realizable labels under

$$
\exists K\ \forall P\quad
\gamma_P(s)\ge s^{-K},

or at least the booster-local version

$$
\exists K\ \forall Q \in \mathcal Q_{\mathrm{boost}}(P,s,\varepsilon,\delta)
\quad \gamma_Q(s)\ge s^{-K}
$$

for every filtered or reweighted marginal $Q$ that the booster may generate.
The source definition supplies a separate exponent for each such $Q$, not one
polynomial envelope controlling the whole boosting run.

The obstruction is intrinsic to weak error. A full-support mixture can control
transcripts, but weak advantage does not transfer to components. If a hard
component has small mixture mass, an aggregate error below $1/2$ can be earned
entirely on the complement. This is unlike strong error bounds, where an
$O(w\varepsilon)$ error under a mixture of weight $w$ gives an
$O(\varepsilon)$ component statement.

### Size-Local Hidden Gaps

Depth 6 isolated the finite-size version of the same obstruction. After
resource uniformization, fix a representation size $s$ and define a worst-case
one-call advantage

$$
\lambda_s =
\inf_{P,c}
\left(\frac12-\operatorname{err}_P(A,c)\right)
$$

with the usual confidence bookkeeping suppressed. Under finiteness and
continuity assumptions, the source may imply $\lambda_s>0$ for each fixed
size. But boosting to the distribution-free target needs an inverse-polynomial
envelope,

$$
\lambda_s\ge s^{-K}
$$

for one $K$ independent of $s$. Fixed-size compactness does not provide this.
The minima could decay as $2^{-s}$, $s^{-s}$, or worse while every isolated
size-slice marginal still has some marginal-dependent inverse-polynomial
exponent.

The natural diagonal proof also fails under the current global-marginal
semantics. If hard size slices are stitched into one marginal
$R=\sum_j w_j Q_j$, a weak learner for $R$ may ignore low-mass hard slices and
obtain its entire aggregate edge elsewhere. A diagonal contradiction would be
available only under a size-indexed full-mass marginal semantics, where each
size slice cannot be hidden by a small global mixture weight.

This leaves no current-semantics proof of a distribution-free polynomial weak
gap, and hence no proof that Schapire/Freund boosting applies.

### Last False Attempt: Rare Hard Blocks

The best counterexample template remains a size-local hidden-gap hard block.
For each block level, put a PRF or random-oracle labeling on a large paired
domain. A distribution-free strong learner with global polynomial degree $D$
can be challenged on one block whose level exceeds $D$. With only polynomially
many samples, it sees too few coordinates to predict unseen labels to constant
error. Under the usual efficient-output evaluator convention, the PRF version
would turn a low-error improper hypothesis into a predictor for fresh PRF
values; the random-oracle version gives the same obstruction information
theoretically.

So the target-negative half is credible. The source-positive half is not.

On a paired block

$$
B_r=\{(u,b):u\in[r],\ b\in\{0,1\}\},
\qquad
c_f(u,b)=1\Longleftrightarrow b=f(u),
$$

compare two marginals $P_+$ and $P_-$ with opposite tiny orientation biases:

$$
P_+(u,f(u))=\frac{1+2\eta}{2r},\qquad
P_+(u,1-f(u))=\frac{1-2\eta}{2r},
$$

and $P_-$ swaps the two masses. For $\eta=\Theta(m/r)$ and
$r\gg m^2$, the $m$-sample transcript distributions have total variation
$O(m\eta)=O(m^2/r)=o(1)$. Thus a deterministic learner cannot reliably output
opposite unseen-coordinate orientations for the two marginals.

This is not merely a failure of a constants-plus-handles algorithm. For an
arbitrary deterministic improper output, each unseen fiber has local behavior

$$
(h(u,0),h(u,1))\in\{(0,0),(1,1),(1,0),(0,1)\}.
$$

Relative to the hidden label $f(u)$, its advantage decomposes into an
orientation-correlation term and a constant-default imbalance term. The
adversary chooses the worse of $P_+$ and $P_-$, making the default imbalance
nonpositive. What remains is true correlation with the hidden labels on unseen
fibers. A random-oracle block gives no such deterministic correlation beyond
the sampled fibers, and sampled fibers carry only $O(m/r)$ mass.

Therefore the natural rare-block PRF/oracle witness is not source-positive
under deterministic final-hypothesis semantics. Fresh prediction-time
randomness would fix exactly this issue by making unseen fibers contribute
error $1/2$ in expectation while memorized fibers supply a small positive edge,
but the present source and target nodes use deterministic improper outputs.

### Failed Deterministic Source-Positive Variants

Public certificates do not solve the problem. If every marginal must expose a
certificate with enough mass for weak learning, then a distribution-free
improper strong learner challenged on the same isolated block can also exploit
it. If the certificate is rare, an arbitrary marginal can avoid it and revive
the source failure.

Fixed hashes, public tie-breakers, or training-time random seeds fixed into the
output also do not solve it. Once the final hypothesis is deterministic, an
adversarial marginal can concentrate on the side where the fixed rule is wrong,
or choose the opposite tiny orientation. A training-time random table has
unseen-label fluctuations of order $r^{-1/2}$, which swamp the intended
sample-handle edge $m/r$ in the hard-block regime $r\gg m^2$.

Sparse one-sided handles are likewise insufficient. Memorizing observed
positive mass helps when the default orientation is already favorable, but an
arbitrary marginal can make the positive and negative masses nearly balanced
with a bias too small to estimate from the pathwise-polynomial sample budget.

Thus a false proof still needs a new deterministic hard core: one weak signal
must be findable under every isolated-block marginal, must not require
estimating an arbitrarily tiny orientation, and must not give an improper
strong learner enough information to aggregate to low error. No such hard core
has emerged in seven branches.

### Conditional True Principles

The edge becomes true under any of the following added principles.

**Uniform weak-gap envelope.** If the source learner's marginal-dependent weak
gaps are dominated by one distribution-free polynomial,

$$
\exists K\ \forall P,c,s\quad
\operatorname{err}_P(A,c)\le \frac12-s^{-K},
$$

then the source is an efficient distribution-free weak realizable improper
learner after full-support resource uniformization, and ordinary realizable
boosting gives the target.

**Booster-local envelope.** It is enough to have the same kind of polynomial
lower bound only over the filtered or reweighted marginals generated during a
fixed boosting run. This is weaker than a global envelope over all marginals,
but it is still not implied by the current source definition.

**Smooth boosting plus a distribution-free modulus.** If a smooth booster only
creates marginals $Q$ satisfying $Q\ll P$ and
$\|dQ/dP\|_\infty\le B(s,1/\varepsilon,\log(1/\delta))$, and if the weak
learner has one polynomial lower bound for all such bounded-density
perturbations, then boosting is polynomial-time distribution-free. If the
modulus depends on the original marginal $P$, the conclusion is only
marginal-nonuniform strong learning.

**Size-indexed full-mass marginal semantics.** If marginal-nonuniform learning
were interpreted as a family of size-conditional marginals with full mass on
each active size, the hidden-gap diagonal argument could force a polynomial
envelope or expose a direct source failure. That is a model change from the
current global-marginal atlas semantics.

These are sufficient principles, not consequences of the current node.

### Conditional False Forks

The rare-block construction becomes plausible only after changing conventions.
With stochastic prediction-time final hypotheses, the weak source can memorize
sampled fibers and flip fresh fair coins on unseen fibers, producing a
marginal-dependent edge from the covered mass without choosing an orientation.
With polynomial-time evaluable improper outputs, the target lower bound can
then be stated using PRFs; with a random-oracle policy, it can be stated
relativizingly. This would be a different stochastic-output edge, not the
current deterministic improper edge.

### Proposed Atlas Summary

`Open under deterministic-output atlas conventions: full-support
uniformization controls the resources for one weak call, but not the
marginal-dependent weak advantage needed by boosting. Size-local hidden gaps
show why fixed-size positivity need not give a polynomial envelope, while the
leading rare-block PRF/oracle counterexamples fail the deterministic
source-positive lemma on opposite tiny-bias marginals.`

### Proposed Atlas Proof-Status Body

**Goal.** Decide whether an efficient marginal-nonuniform weak realizable
improper learner can always be converted into an efficient distribution-free
strong realizable improper learner.

**Resource uniformization.** A full-support reference marginal gives a single
pathwise polynomial bound for one call to the source learner: every finite
sample transcript possible under any marginal also occurs with positive
probability under the reference marginal. This only controls sample and running
time. It does not control the weak advantage.

**Weak-gap envelope obstruction.** The source gives, for each marginal $P$, a
gap $\gamma_P(s)\ge s^{-k(P)}$ with an exponent depending on $P$. Boosting to a
distribution-free strong learner requires one polynomial lower bound for all
weak calls, or at least for all filtered/reweighted marginals generated by the
booster. The current definition supplies no continuity, bounded-density
stability, compactness modulus, or size-conditional diagonal principle that
would produce this envelope. Fixed-size compactness can at most give a positive
minimum gap $\lambda_s>0$; it does not rule out superpolynomial decay of
$\lambda_s$ with $s$.

**Rare-block counterexample attempts.** A rare-block PRF or random-oracle hard
core would make the distribution-free strong target fail on one isolated block
whose level exceeds the alleged learner's polynomial degree, assuming efficient
improper outputs are polynomial-time evaluable. However, the same construction
has not been made source-positive under deterministic output semantics. On a
paired hard block, opposite tiny-bias marginals $P_+$ and $P_-$ are
statistically indistinguishable at the pathwise-polynomial sample scale.
Arbitrary deterministic hypotheses decompose into an unseen-label orientation
term and a constant-default term; one of $P_+$ or $P_-$ makes the default term
nonpositive, while random-oracle or PRF labels prevent useful deterministic
correlation on unseen fibers. Stochastic prediction-time hypotheses would
repair this source lemma, but they are not part of the present nodes.

**Conclusion.** The edge should remain `open`. A true proof needs a
distribution-free or booster-local weak-gap envelope. A false proof needs a
new deterministic source-positive, target-negative hard core rather than the
standard rare random-label block.
