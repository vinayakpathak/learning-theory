## Depth 1 Branch A: Distribution-Free Reweighting Audit

### Setup And Comparison Point

I read the edge note first:
`atlas/implications/efficient-weak-realizable-proper-pac--implies--efficient-marginal-nonuniform-weak-agnostic-proper-pac.md`.
The note's diagnosis is still the right starting point. The source gives a
distribution-free weak realizable proper learner. The target asks for a
marginal-nonuniform weak agnostic proper learner. The low-noise branch is
available by coupling to clean realizable samples, while the middle/high-OPT
branch needs a legal proper fallback, neutralizer, or selector.

I compared this edge with the completed depth-7 tree for
`efficient-marginal-nonuniform-weak-realizable-proper-pac--implies--efficient-marginal-nonuniform-weak-agnostic-proper-pac`.
That tree ends unresolved. Its final obstruction is not primarily the
marginal-nonuniform bookkeeping; it is the proper middle-noise selector
problem. A same-$P$ low-noise robustification works, and legal constants,
legal complement pairs, balanced proper samplers, or public proper shadow
covers would solve the remaining regime. The source node alone did not yield
such an object.

The present source is stronger: it is distribution-free rather than merely
marginal-nonuniform. This removes one obstacle from the depth-7 tree. Calls to
the source on auxiliary or reweighted clean marginals now have the same
uniform polynomial bound and the same inverse-polynomial weak advantage. The
question is whether that extra reweighting power manufactures the missing
proper selector or neutralizer.

### Normalization

Fix a size parameter $s$. Let the distribution-free source learner $A$ use at
most $m(s,\log(1/\delta))$ examples and achieve clean advantage
$\gamma(s)>0$, where both are polynomial/inverse-polynomial uniformly over
all marginals. For every distribution $Q$ over instances and every
$c\in\mathcal C_s$, on clean labels $Y=c(X)$ with $X\sim Q$, $A$ outputs
$h\in\mathcal C_s$ with
$$
\operatorname{err}_Q(h,c)\le \frac12-\gamma(s).
$$

For the agnostic target, fix a marginal $P$ and write labels in
$\{\pm1\}$ form:
$$
r(x)=\mathbf E[Y\mid X=x],
\qquad
M(r)=\sup_{c\in\mathcal C_s}\langle r,c\rangle_P.
$$
The desired weak agnostic proper guarantee is equivalent to finding
$h\in\mathcal C_s$ such that
$$
\langle r,h\rangle_P\ge M(r)-1+2\alpha_P(s)
$$
for some inverse-polynomial $P$-dependent gap $\alpha_P(s)>0$.

### Low-Noise Branch

The low-noise branch is fully positive and even gives a $P$-independent weak
gap. Choose, for example,
$$
\alpha(s)\le \min\{\gamma(s)/8,1/(100m(s)),1/100\}.
$$
Let $\eta=\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}(\mathcal D)$, and suppose
$\eta<2\alpha(s)$. Choose a near-optimal $c^\star\in\mathcal C_s$.
Run $A$ on many fresh noisy batches from $\mathcal D$ and validate all proper
outputs on a holdout sample.

Couple one noisy run to a clean realizable run on the same instance sequence
with labels $c^\star(X)$. A batch of length at most $m(s)$ has no corrupted
label with probability at least
$$
1-m(s)\eta > 1-2m(s)\alpha(s)\ge 49/50.
$$
On this event, the noisy transcript is a clean realizable transcript, so the
source returns a proper $h$ with
$$
\operatorname{err}_P(h,c^\star)\le \frac12-\gamma(s).
$$
Then
$$
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(h)
\le
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(c^\star)+\operatorname{err}_P(h,c^\star)
\le
\eta+\frac12-\gamma(s)
\le
\eta+\frac12-\alpha(s).
$$
Repetition and validation stay polynomial. Properness is preserved because
every candidate generated by $A$ lies in $\mathcal C_s$.

This is stronger than the marginal-source depth-7 branch only in its
bookkeeping: the clean advantage and transcript bound are uniform in $P$.
It does not touch the complementary regime $\eta\ge 2\alpha(s)$.

### What Distribution-Free Reweighting Adds

The tempting new move is to use the source on auxiliary clean marginals.
Given any efficiently samplable distribution $Q$ and clean labels from a
legal concept $c$, the source gives a legal $h$ with
$\langle h,c\rangle_Q\ge 2\gamma(s)$. Therefore point masses and small
empirical supports have local proper completions whenever their labelings are
realizable by concepts. Unlike the marginal-source tree, there is no hidden
$p_Q$ runtime that can blow up when $Q$ is not the original marginal $P$.

This helps with local trace generation, but it still does not give the
middle-noise selector. The selector is a statement about the arbitrary
regression $r$, not about clean labels from a concept. If $r$ is not close to
any concept on the sampled or reweighted marginal, the source promise is
silent. Running $A$ on agnostic labels, random labels, flipped labels, or
labels chosen to oppose a public handle is an off-promise call. A valid source
learner may behave arbitrarily on such transcripts.

A black-box wrapper cannot repair this by reweighting alone. For a reweighted
distribution $Q$, the clean-transcript probability is controlled by
$\operatorname{err}_Q(c^\star,Y)$, not by distribution-freeness. In genuine
middle noise, this probability can be exponentially small in $m(s)$ under
every useful $Q$. Reweighting changes where the clean-noise obstacle is
measured; it does not create clean labels.

### Why Local Completions Do Not Globalize

The depth-7 tree left a finite-trace loophole: maybe one can enumerate small
trace labelings, call the source on the induced empirical marginals, and
validate the resulting proper concepts. Distribution-freeness makes this
loophole cleaner, but still not enough.

If the trace size is $k=O(\log s)$, all $2^k$ labelings can be enumerated in
polynomial time, and the source can produce proper completions for realizable
labelings. But such a tiny trace need not certify $P$-correlation for the
resulting hypotheses; their behavior off the trace may be adversarial under
$P$.

If the trace is large enough to uniformly control correlations at an
inverse-polynomial weak scale, then $k$ is typically polynomial in $s$, and
enumerating all $2^k$ labelings is not polynomial. Distribution-freeness
removes the auxiliary-marginal runtime problem, but not the label-enumeration
or off-support-control problem.

This is the sharp difference from the completed depth-7 tree: one obstruction
is gone, but two harder obstructions remain.

### Neutralizers Are Not Forced

For high OPT, the improper sibling edge uses constants. In correlation form,
if $\eta=\operatorname{OPT}\ge \alpha$, then
$M(r)-1+2\alpha\le 0$, so a hypothesis with nonnegative or only slightly
negative correlation against $r$ is enough. Constants provide this improperly
because the better constant has error at most $1/2$.

The proper target needs a legal concept. Distribution-free weak realizable
proper learning does not imply legal constants, complements, a balanced
proper convex hull, or a fixed-$P$ neutral sampler. Homogeneous parities give
the sanity check: they are cleanly and properly learnable, but under the
point mass at $0^n$, every legal homogeneous parity has the same value. There
is no literal balanced proper neutralizer at that marginal.

This sanity check is not a counterexample, because the target is
OPT-relative. If all concepts agree on the anchored point, then any proper
output is as good as any other under that marginal. The missing object is not
literal balance in consensus directions; it is a computational selector
modulo the OPT term.

The exact selector problem is: given samples from $r$, find some legal
$h\in\mathcal C_s$ with
$$
\langle r,h\rangle_P\ge M(r)-1+2\alpha_P(s).
$$
An optimizer always exists, so the obstruction is computational. The source
only says how to weakly follow a legal concept when its clean labels are
available. It does not say how to find an optimizer, a neutral legal concept,
or a public weak shadow from arbitrary noisy samples.

### False-Witness Audit

I do not see an atlas-ready false witness.

Halfspaces are source-positive by realizable proper learning, and Tiegel's
hardness refutes the distribution-free weak agnostic target under lattice
assumptions. But the marginal-nonuniform target can attach its own hidden
gap to each fixed marginal. The fixed-Gaussian hardness known locally is a
strong $\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$ obstruction, not a no-advantage result
against the much weaker additive tolerance $\operatorname{OPT}+1/2-\alpha_P$.
Usual halfspace classes also contain legal constant halfspaces, which solve
the high-OPT branch by the neutralizer argument.

Parity, code, and LPN-style constructions still miss the weak target. If
labels are a noisy hidden codeword $(1-2\eta)c_y$, then a wrong orthogonal
proper hypothesis has correlation about $0$ with the regression. For
$\eta\ge \alpha_P$, that neutral wrong output already satisfies the weak
agnostic inequality. For $\eta<\alpha_P$, the low-noise branch applies. To
force recovery, wrong codewords would need noticeable negative correlation,
but large binary families with uniform inverse-polynomial anti-correlation
under one fixed marginal run into Plotkin/Gram-type geometry. A separation
would need computational no-neutral-output hardness, not ordinary recovery
hardness.

PCP/FGRW and clause-satisfaction routes have the right selector flavor but
the wrong quantifiers so far. If the hard instance is encoded into the
marginal, marginal nonuniformity can hide the polynomial and gap. If all hard
slices are packed into one fixed marginal, slice mass dilutes the weak gap
unless every weakly acceptable proper output decodes the witness. Adding
public handles to keep clean weak proper learning easy tends to create the
very proper fallback needed by the agnostic target; deleting the handles
threatens the distribution-free source on marginals concentrated on the hard
slice.

One-way image-coordinate and bounded-handle witnesses show the same tension.
The handles make clean weak proper learning easy under arbitrary marginals,
but they also become validation-searchable weak agnostic fallbacks. Removing
them can restore strong proper hardness while losing the source.

### Obstacles

1. Distribution-freeness gives uniform clean weak learning on every
   reweighted marginal, but only for realizable labels from legal concepts.
2. Middle-noise agnostic labels need not provide any polynomial-probability
   clean transcript under any useful reweighting.
3. Small trace enumeration is polynomial but does not control off-trace
   behavior under $P$; large trace enumeration controls $P$ but is
   exponential.
4. The source does not force legal constants, complements, balanced samplers,
   fixed-$P$ proper ERM, or any validation-searchable OPT-relative selector.
5. Anti-neutralizer examples are not enough because the target is
   OPT-relative; consensus biases cancel out of the comparison.
6. Known hardness templates either vary the marginal, prove a stronger target
   than needed, leak neutral wrong hypotheses, or add handles that also solve
   the weak agnostic branch.

### Depth 2 Directions

1. Formalize the reweighted-trace barrier. Prove or refute: even with a
   distribution-free source, polynomially many calls on polynomially many
   small clean traces cannot guarantee a same-$P$ proper neutralizer or shadow
   cover without an additional off-support control assumption.
2. Search for a quotient-neutralizer theorem that ignores consensus
   directions. The right positive statement would derive an OPT-relative
   selector, not a literal zero-mean convex hull, from distribution-free clean
   weak proper learning.
3. Build a computational no-neutral-output witness: one fixed marginal and
   middle-noise regressions where clean labels expose easy weak proper
   handles under every marginal, but every polynomial-time proper output with
   correlation above $M(r)-1+2/\operatorname{poly}(s)$ decodes a hard witness.

## Depth 2 Branch A: Uniform Traces Still Need A Selector

### Question For This Branch

The completed depth-7 tree for
`efficient-marginal-nonuniform-weak-realizable-proper-pac -> efficient-marginal-nonuniform-weak-agnostic-proper-pac`
left the edge open because the middle-noise branch needs a legal proper
selector or neutralizer. In that tree there were three separate obstructions:

1. the low-noise coupling only works while clean transcripts still occur with
   inverse-polynomial probability;
2. calls to the realizable learner on auxiliary trace marginals may have
   auxiliary marginal-dependent running times, so local handles need not be
   reachable in the original fixed-$P$ polynomial; and
3. even reachable local handles do not automatically become a same-$P$
   proper neutralizer or shadow cover.

The present source removes obstruction 2. We now have a distribution-free
weak realizable proper learner $A$, with a uniform polynomial transcript
bound $m(s,\log(1/\delta))$ and a uniform inverse-polynomial clean advantage
$\gamma(s)$. Therefore $A$ can be run on point masses, empirical trace
marginals, reweighted marginals, or the original marginal $P$ with the same
polynomial resource bound. The question is whether removing the rate
obstruction also removes the selector obstruction.

I do not see a way to make that last jump. Distribution-free reweighting is a
real strengthening, but it only gives uniform access to clean weak proper
extensions of labelings that are already known to be realizable by legal
concepts. The middle-noise agnostic problem is different: the learner sees a
regression $r(x)=\mathbf E[Y\mid X=x]$ and must find a legal deterministic
concept whose correlation is not too far below the best legal concept. The
source still gives no rule for choosing the right realizable trace labels and
no reason that local trace extensions are neutral away from the trace.

### Normalized Target

Use $\{\pm1\}$ labels and fix a marginal $P$, representation size $s$, and
joint distribution $\mathcal D$ with conditional mean
$$
r(x)=\mathbf E[Y\mid X=x].
$$
Let
$$
M(r)=\sup_{c\in\mathcal C_s}\langle r,c\rangle_P.
$$
The target is to output $h\in\mathcal C_s$ with
$$
\langle r,h\rangle_P\ge M(r)-1+2\alpha_P(s)
$$
for some inverse-polynomial, marginal-dependent $\alpha_P(s)>0$.

The low-noise branch from Depth 1 is already atlas-suitable. If a
near-optimal $c^\star$ has agnostic error $\eta$ below an inverse transcript
scale, then one noisy call to $A$ agrees with a clean realizable call on
$(X,c^\star(X))$ with inverse-polynomial probability. Repetition and
validation return a proper $h$ satisfying
$$
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(h)
\le
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(c^\star)+\frac12-\Omega(\gamma(s)).
$$
The distribution-free source makes this branch uniform in $P$, but it was
not the hard branch.

For the complementary regime, write
$$
\eta=\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}(\mathcal D)
=\frac{1-M(r)}2.
$$
If $\eta\ge\alpha_P(s)$, the required correlation threshold is nonpositive:
$$
M(r)-1+2\alpha_P(s)=-2\eta+2\alpha_P(s)\le 0.
$$
Thus the middle-noise task would be solved by any validation-searchable
proper hypothesis with nonnegative, or only mildly negative, correlation with
$r$. Constants, complement pairs, balanced proper samplers, and public
proper shadow covers all provide such a fallback. The problem is that
distribution-free weak clean learning does not appear to force any of these
objects.

### What Distribution-Free Trace Access Buys

The strengthened source gives the following local structure.

Fix any efficiently samplable distribution $Q$ and any legal concept
$c\in\mathcal C_s$. On clean samples $(X,c(X))$ with $X\sim Q$, $A$ outputs
$h\in\mathcal C_s$ such that
$$
\langle c,h\rangle_Q\ge 2\gamma(s)
$$
with high probability. In particular, for a point mass $\delta_x$, if both
labels are realized at $x$ by legal concepts, then clean calls on
$(x,+1)$ and $(x,-1)$ can produce legal concepts taking those two values at
$x$. For a fixed finite trace $T=\{x_1,\dots,x_k\}$ with empirical marginal
$Q_T$, every realizable labeling $\ell:T\to\{\pm1\}$ has a proper weak
extension whose agreement with $\ell$ on $T$ is better than half.

This is stronger than the marginal-nonuniform source in the depth-7 tree.
There, local trace completions might be useless because the polynomial for
$Q_T$ need not be controlled by the polynomial for the original hard
marginal $P$. Here the same learner can be run on every $Q_T$ inside a
uniform polynomial. So a depth-7 rate objection is genuinely gone.

The remaining question is whether these local completions can be assembled
into one of the middle-noise objects:

1. a $P$-polynomial candidate list hitting the weak $P$-shadow of every
   comparator $c^\star$;
2. a $P$-polynomial sampler over deterministic proper concepts whose
   barycenter is approximately neutral against any middle-noise regression
   $r$; or
3. a direct relative selector for
   $\langle r,h\rangle_P\ge M(r)-1+2\alpha_P(s)$.

I do not see a derivation of any of these from the local guarantees alone.

### Why Uniform Trace Enumeration Still Does Not Globalize

There is a tempting finite-trace proof. Draw a trace
$T=(x_1,\dots,x_k)$ from $P$. Enumerate labelings of $T$. For each labeling
that is realizable, run $A$ on the empirical clean distribution over $T$ and
validate the resulting proper hypotheses on the agnostic sample. Because
the source is distribution-free, every such call is uniformly efficient.

The proof breaks in two different ways, depending on $k$.

If $k=O(\log s)$, all $2^k$ trace labelings can be enumerated in polynomial
time. But such a trace generally carries too little information about
$P$-correlation. A proper concept that weakly matches a logarithmic trace can
be arbitrarily anti-correlated with the near-optimal comparator on the rest
of the $P$-mass. Validation against $r$ can reject a bad candidate, but only
if the list contains a good or neutral one. Small-trace enumeration gives no
reason that the off-trace behavior of any generated completion is usable.

If $k$ is large enough to certify inverse-polynomial $P$-correlation, then
$k$ is typically polynomial in $s$ and $1/\alpha_P(s)$. The enumeration
contains $2^k$ labelings. The distribution-free source has removed the
runtime cost of each individual realizable call, but it has not removed the
combinatorial search over the unknown trace labeling of $c^\star$. Feeding
$A$ the observed noisy labels or the signs of empirical conditional means
does not solve this, because those labels need not be realizable. On such
off-promise transcripts, $A$ can behave arbitrarily while still satisfying
the source definition.

Thus uniformity over auxiliary marginals fixes the rate problem, not the
label-selection problem. A positive trace theorem would need an additional
proper trace selector: given a large trace and noisy or weighted labels,
efficiently find a realizable trace labeling whose proper completion has
near-best empirical correlation. That selector is essentially the missing
weak agnostic proper learner in finite-support form.

### Why Reweighting Does Not Create Clean Transcripts

Another natural attempt is to reweight the agnostic distribution until the
labels look nearly clean. Distribution-freeness makes calls on reweighted
marginals legal, so this is worth checking carefully.

Fix a near-optimal comparator $c^\star$. For any reweighted marginal $Q$,
the probability that an $m(s)$-sample from $\mathcal D$ is an exactly clean
transcript for $c^\star$ is controlled by
$$
\operatorname{err}_{Q}(c^\star,Y),
$$
not by the distribution-free nature of $A$. If the conditional noise is
middle-sized on every region where $Q$ places useful mass, the clean
transcript probability is about
$$
(1-\operatorname{err}_{Q}(c^\star,Y))^{m(s)},
$$
which can be exponentially small in the source transcript length. Reweighting
can move the noise around; it does not manufacture labels from
$c^\star(X)$. Without knowing $c^\star$, there is also no reliable way to
choose a low-noise region. Choosing regions according to a public handle or
according to the observed labels again gives only off-promise calls unless
the selected labels are realizable.

This is the same clean-transcript boundary as in Depth 1. Distribution-free
learning says that if we can present a clean realizable sample from any
chosen marginal, then $A$ works there. It does not say how to obtain such a
sample from arbitrary agnostic labels.

### The Neutralizer Attempt In Quotient Form

The most promising possible positive theorem would be a quotient neutralizer:
ignore coordinates on which all legal concepts agree, and prove that the
source forces an approximately balanced proper sampler on the remaining
directions. This would be exactly the right shape, because literal balance
is not necessary. If all concepts agree on a region and the labels oppose
that agreement, then the best comparator also suffers there, so the
OPT-relative threshold relaxes automatically.

The source, however, only gives a family of one-sided kernels. For every
clean target $c$, the output distribution of $A$ on clean labels from $c$
satisfies
$$
\mathbf E_{h\sim K(c)}\langle c,h\rangle_P\ge 2\gamma(s).
$$
This condition does not imply that the union or convex hull of the reachable
outputs contains a point neutral against every $r$. A valid weak learner may
always return a public weak handle when that handle is positively correlated
with every clean target under the queried marginal. Such a handle is
consistent with weak realizable learning. It can still be badly
anti-correlated with a middle-noise regression that opposes the handle while
making some hidden concept moderately good.

This does not give a counterexample, because if the handle is genuinely close
to every hidden concept under $P$, then the triangle inequality makes it an
agnostic shadow too. But it explains why a kernel/minimax argument is missing:
the source gives positive correlation relative to clean targets, not
symmetry, complement closure, or a public distribution over legal concepts
whose barycenter is forced toward zero in the relevant quotient space.

### False-Witness Audit After Adding Distribution-Free Uniformity

The stronger source makes negative examples harder, because a witness must
remain weakly properly learnable under every marginal with one uniform
polynomial. The earlier no-neutral-sampler template still seems like the
right shape, but I do not have an atlas-ready instantiation.

**Parity and code witnesses.** Uniform parities are cleanly and properly
learnable distribution-free, so they satisfy the source. But under the
uniform marginal, wrong parities are neutral. For noisy hidden parity
$r=(1-2\eta)c_y$, a wrong parity with correlation $0$ has correlation $0$
with $r$. In the middle-noise regime $\eta\ge\alpha_P(s)$, the required
threshold is at most $0$, so the wrong parity is already acceptable. This is
why LPN-style recovery hardness is too strong a requirement for the learner
and too weak a lower bound for this edge.

To force every wrong proper hypothesis to fail, wrong hypotheses would need
noticeable negative correlation with the hidden target at the relevant
inverse-polynomial scale. A large family with pairwise inverse-polynomial
anti-correlation under one fixed marginal runs into the usual weighted
Gram/Plotkin geometry. So a separation cannot be just a code-distance
construction; it must make even neutral valid hypotheses computationally hard
to find.

**Halfspaces.** Distribution-free realizable proper learning is available,
and known agnostic proper hardness is a useful warning. But ordinary
halfspace representations have legal constant halfspaces, so the
middle-noise branch is solved by the constant neutralizer. The hardness is
aimed at much stronger agnostic accuracy than the atlas weak additive target.

**PCP or clause-satisfaction classes.** These have the right selector-hardness
flavor, but the distribution-free source is difficult to preserve. If a
marginal concentrates on the hard active slice, clean weak proper learning
already resembles finding a satisfying assignment or proof. Adding public
handles can restore clean weak learning, but the same handles tend to become
validation-searchable agnostic fallbacks. Packing hard slices into one fixed
marginal also risks diluting the weak gap unless every acceptable proper
output decodes the hard witness.

**Signature or valid-code templates.** The best remaining false template is
a hard-to-sample valid proper family. Exact clean labels should allow a
uniform weak proper learner to identify or shadow the target under every
marginal, but noisy labels under one fixed marginal should make it hard to
output even a valid deterministic concept with correlation
$\ge -1/\operatorname{poly}(s)$. This is a genuine no-neutral-output
assumption, stronger than ordinary noisy recovery. I do not know how to
instantiate it from a standard primitive without either leaking random
neutral valid concepts or breaking the distribution-free clean source.

### Provisional Conclusion

Distribution-free weak proper learning does add meaningful uniformity over
the depth-7 marginal-source tree. It lets us run the source on arbitrary
empirical and reweighted clean marginals inside one uniform polynomial.
However, the same no-neutral-sampler obstruction remains in the middle-noise
agnostic branch. The missing step is no longer "can we afford auxiliary
marginal calls?" but rather "which clean labels should we feed them, and why
should the resulting proper completions be useful under the original
marginal $P$?"

I therefore cannot resolve the edge as true. I also cannot resolve it as
false, because the needed witness is sharper than the standard hardness
templates: it must block neutral proper outputs while retaining
distribution-free clean weak proper learnability.

### Depth 3 Directions

1. **Uniform trace selector theorem or barrier.** Formalize the finite-support
   version enabled by the distribution-free source: given a polynomial-size
   trace with weighted noisy labels, does the ability to weakly complete every
   realizable labeling imply a polynomial-time proper concept with correlation
   within $1-2/\operatorname{poly}$ of the empirical optimum? A positive
   answer would likely resolve this edge true; a negative answer would isolate
   the label-selection barrier.
2. **Quotient neutralizer theorem.** Work in the quotient of
   $L_2(P)$ by directions on which all concepts agree. Try to prove or refute
   that distribution-free clean weak proper learning forces a
   validation-searchable proper sampler whose barycenter is approximately
   neutral in that quotient. This is the cleanest possible route to using the
   stronger source.
3. **Hard valid-code/no-neutral witness.** Search for one fixed marginal and
   middle-noise regressions $r_y$ where every efficiently generated valid
   proper concept with correlation $\ge -1/\operatorname{poly}(s)$ decodes
   $y$, while exact clean labels still give a uniform distribution-free weak
   proper learner. Ordinary LPN, parity, halfspace, and PCP templates should
   be tested specifically for neutral-output leakage, not merely recovery
   hardness.

## Depth 3 Branch A: Candidate-Failure Filter

### Aim Of This Branch

Depths 1 and 2 left the same question in sharper form: the source learner is
now uniformly callable on every clean marginal, but the middle-noise branch
still lacks a legal proper selector or neutralizer. I tried to extract such a
selector from validation failure itself.

The hope is natural. Suppose we have any legal candidate $h\in\mathcal C_s$.
If validation says that $h$ is not good enough, then there is a comparator
$c^\star\in\mathcal C_s$ that beats it by almost the maximum possible weak
amount. Perhaps the examples on which the observed label disagrees with
$h$ form an auxiliary distribution on which the labels become almost clean
for $c^\star$. If true, distribution-free source uniformity would matter:
we could call the clean weak learner on that filtered marginal without any
auxiliary-marginal runtime penalty.

The calculation shows that this repair is too optimistic. Failure of $h$
creates a weak agnostic signal for $c^\star$, not a clean or low-noise
realizable one. Thus the attempted repair reduces to the same missing
proper weak agnostic selector.

### Failure Algebra

Use $\{\pm1\}$ labels. Fix a marginal $P$, a regression
$r(x)=\mathbf E[Y\mid X=x]$, and write
$$
M(r)=\sup_{c\in\mathcal C_s}\langle r,c\rangle_P.
$$
The target asks for $h\in\mathcal C_s$ with
$$
\langle r,h\rangle_P\ge M(r)-1+2\alpha_P(s).
$$

Assume a legal candidate $h$ fails this inequality, witnessed by
$c^\star$ with $\langle r,c^\star\rangle_P$ close to $M(r)$. Ignoring the
usual tiny estimation slack, failure gives
$$
\langle r,c^\star\rangle_P-\langle r,h\rangle_P>1-2\alpha.
$$
In error notation this is
$$
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(h)
-\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(c^\star)
> \frac12-\alpha.
$$

Let
$$
F=\{Y\ne h(X)\}, \qquad D=\{h(X)\ne c^\star(X)\}.
$$
On $D$, exactly one of $h$ and $c^\star$ is correct; off $D$, they have the
same error. Hence
$$
\operatorname{err}(h)-\operatorname{err}(c^\star)
=\Pr(F\cap D)-\Pr(F^c\cap D).
$$
So a failed $h$ implies
$$
\Pr(F\cap D)>\frac12-\alpha.
$$

This is the key quantitative fact. On the filtered sample $F$, the observed
label equals $c^\star(X)$ exactly on $F\cap D$. Therefore the filtered labels
have clean fraction
$$
\theta=\Pr(c^\star(X)=Y\mid F)=\frac{\Pr(F\cap D)}{\Pr(F)}.
$$
The bound above gives only weak information about $\theta$. It can be below
$1/2$ in very high-OPT cases, and in the relevant boundary cases it can be
only $1/2+\varepsilon$ for an inverse-polynomial or smaller
$\varepsilon$. In either case, the probability that an $m(s)$-example
filtered transcript is exactly clean for $c^\star$ can be about
$(1/2+o(1))^{m(s)}$, exponentially small in the source learner's transcript
length.

So validation failure does not manufacture the clean transcripts that the
source learner needs. It manufactures the promise that $c^\star$ is a weak
agnostic comparator on a derived distribution. That is precisely the target
kind of promise, not the source kind.

### A Concrete Boundary Example For The Filter

The bad case is not a technical corner. Let $h$ be a legal concept and let
the labels always oppose $h$ except that another legal concept $c^\star$
agrees with those labels on only a $1/2+\varepsilon$ fraction of the
marginal. Then $h$ has error $1$, while $c^\star$ has error
$1/2-\varepsilon$. The candidate $h$ fails the weak agnostic inequality for
every $\alpha<\varepsilon$.

But the filter $F=\{Y\ne h(X)\}$ is the whole distribution. On it,
$c^\star$ is only $1/2+\varepsilon$ accurate. A clean realizable weak learner
has no guarantee on such transcripts. Distribution-free uniformity says that
if we could feed the learner clean labels from $c^\star$ on this marginal,
then it would work uniformly; it does not provide those labels.

This also explains why legal complements solve a special case. If $-h$ is
legal, then the same failure that makes $h$ badly anti-correlated makes
$-h$ a validation-searchable proper fallback. Without complement closure,
the source learner has no way to turn "the complement of this bad candidate
would be useful" into a legal concept.

### Why This Blocks A Candidate-Repair Proof

The repair proof would need the following loop:

1. generate a legal candidate $h$;
2. validate $h$ against the agnostic sample;
3. if $h$ fails, filter or reweight the sample using $h$ and the labels;
4. call the distribution-free clean weak learner on the filtered problem to
   obtain a better legal candidate.

Step 4 is the invalid step. The filtered labels are not realizable and need
not be low-noise relative to any legal concept. They only certify that some
legal concept is weakly better than $h$ in an OPT-relative sense. Calling the
source on those labels is an off-promise call.

This is stronger than the earlier trace objection. The obstacle remains even
after a candidate has been found and even after validation supplies a
statistical certificate that the candidate is wrong. The certificate points
toward a complement/selector problem, not toward a clean-label problem.

### Consequences For True And False Routes

I still do not see an atlas-ready true proof. The most plausible positive
route would have to add one more ingredient beyond distribution-free weak
realizable proper learning:

- complement closure, legal constants, or a validation-searchable balanced
  proper sampler;
- a theorem saying that every failed candidate exposes a legal proper
  replacement without using agnostic learning on the filtered labels; or
- a finite-support proper selector theorem for weakly noisy trace labels.

The candidate-failure calculation also clarifies why a false witness has to
be unusual. To refute the edge, it is not enough to make recovery of
$c^\star$ hard from noisy labels. In the middle-noise regime a neutral wrong
proper hypothesis is acceptable. A witness must instead arrange that every
efficiently reachable legal hypothesis has correlation below the weak
threshold, while exact clean labels still allow distribution-free weak
proper learning. Standard parity, code, and LPN examples leak neutral wrong
hypotheses; handle-based examples leak legal fallbacks; hard active-slice
examples tend to break the arbitrary-marginal clean source.

### Depth 4 Directions

1. **Failure-filter barrier theorem.** Formalize the calculation as a
   black-box barrier: any wrapper that repairs failed candidates only by
   calling the source on labels measurable from $(h(X),Y)$ is either making
   off-promise calls or assuming a legal complement/neutralizer.
2. **Complement-extractor test.** Investigate whether distribution-free weak
   proper learnability plus the existence of a badly failed $h$ can ever
   force an efficiently findable legal concept behaving like $-h$ on the
   relevant high-disagreement region. Homogeneous parities and affine
   subfamilies are good sanity checks because literal constants and
   complements may be absent or representation-sensitive.
3. **No-neutral finite-support diagonal.** On one polynomial-size support,
   try to diagonalize against polynomial candidate generators by choosing
   regressions of the form "hidden comparator minus generated candidates,"
   while keeping the clean realizable problem distribution-free weakly
   proper learnable. The main obstacle is the Gram/Plotkin pressure that
   large classes usually contain neutral wrong concepts.

This branch does not resolve the implication. It does, however, make the
middle-noise independence cleaner: distribution-free source uniformity
removes the cost of auxiliary clean calls, but validation failure supplies
only weak noisy labels for the hidden comparator. Turning those labels into a
proper replacement is exactly the missing selector problem.

## Depth 4 Branch A: Regional Complements

### Aim

Depth 3 showed that a failed candidate $h$ does not expose clean labels for
the hidden comparator $c^\star$. I pushed that calculation one step further.
There is one sense in which the failure filter is perfectly clean: on the
event where $h$ disagrees with the observed label, the label is exactly
$-h(X)$. Thus a clean source call is available only if the class contains a
legal complement, or at least a legal regional complement, for $h$ on that
filtered marginal.

That is not a consequence of distribution-free weak realizable proper
learning. It is exactly another form of the missing proper neutralizer or
selector.

### The Failure Filter Splits Into Two Different Targets

Work with $\{\pm1\}$ labels. Fix a legal candidate $h$, a near-optimal legal
comparator $c^\star$, and the failure event
$$
F=\{Y\ne h(X)\}.
$$
As in Depth 3, if $h$ fails the weak agnostic target by slack $\alpha$, then
$$
\operatorname{err}(h)-\operatorname{err}(c^\star)>\frac12-\alpha,
$$
and with $D=\{h(X)\ne c^\star(X)\}$,
$$
\operatorname{err}(h)-\operatorname{err}(c^\star)
=\Pr(F\cap D)-\Pr(F^c\cap D).
$$
So
$$
\Pr(F\cap D)>\frac12-\alpha.
$$

This inequality has two incompatible readings.

First, relative to the hidden comparator $c^\star$, the filtered labels are
only noisy. On $F$, the observed label agrees with $c^\star$ exactly on
$F\cap D$, so the comparator's clean fraction under the filtered marginal is
$$
\Pr(c^\star(X)=Y\mid F)=\frac{\Pr(F\cap D)}{\Pr(F)}.
$$
The lower bound on $\Pr(F\cap D)$ does not force this ratio to be close to
one. It can be only $1/2+\varepsilon$. A call to the realizable source with
these labels is then an off-promise agnostic weak call.

Second, relative to the observable complement of the failed candidate, the
same filtered labels are perfectly clean:
$$
Y=-h(X)\qquad\text{on }F.
$$
Thus a source call on the conditional distribution $\mathcal D\mid F$ is an
on-promise clean call exactly when there exists some legal concept
$g\in\mathcal C_s$ with
$$
g(X)=-h(X)\quad Q_F\text{-almost surely},
$$
where $Q_F$ is the instance marginal induced by conditioning on $F$.

This is a regional complement condition. If $-h$ is legal, it holds. If the
class can patch "$-h$ on $F$ and $h$ off $F$" by a legal concept, it may also
hold in a globally useful way. But the weak realizable source itself only
says how to learn from clean labels once such a legal target exists. It does
not say that a legal regional complement exists, that it is findable, or that
a hypothesis learned only under $Q_F$ behaves acceptably on $P\setminus F$.

### Boundary Example For Observable Filters

The obstruction can be made visible without cryptography. Let $h$ be legal
and let the labels deterministically oppose it:
$$
Y=-h(X).
$$
Choose a legal comparator $c^\star$ that agrees with $Y$ on a
$1/2+\varepsilon$ fraction of the marginal and agrees with $h$ on the
remaining $1/2-\varepsilon$ fraction. Then
$$
\operatorname{err}(h)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{err}(c^\star)=\frac12-\varepsilon,
$$
so $h$ badly fails the target inequality.

But $F$ is the whole sample space. The observed labels are clean only for
the complement $-h$. They are not clean for $c^\star$; they give only a
weak noisy signal of advantage $\varepsilon$. If $-h$ is not legal, then the
clean complement call is off-promise. If the learner instead hopes to call
the source for $c^\star$, it again has only noisy labels.

This can be balanced across the cells of $h$. Arrange that, inside each
non-null cell $\{h=+1\}$ and $\{h=-1\}$, the comparator agrees with
$Y=-h$ on exactly a $1/2+\varepsilon$ fraction. Then every filter whose
selection and relabeling are measurable only from $(h(X),Y)$ sees the same
weak $1/2+\varepsilon$ purity for $c^\star$. Such filters never isolate a
low-noise realizable call for the hidden comparator. They either ask for the
observable complement, which requires extra legal structure, or they ask the
source to solve a weak agnostic subproblem, which is the target problem
again.

This is not an atlas counterexample. A non-black-box learner might exploit
the representation or the raw instance $X$ to locate the hidden
$1/2+\varepsilon$ set. The point is narrower: validation failure itself does
not create the clean labels needed by the distribution-free source.

### Why Regional Clean Calls Still Do Not Finish The Proof

Suppose optimistically that a legal $g$ exists with $g=-h$ on the failure
filter $F$. The source can then be run cleanly on $Q_F$ with labels $Y$.
It returns a proper $u$ whose correlation with $g$ under $Q_F$ is positive.
This is still only a local statement.

To repair $h$ globally, one needs more. Either $u$ itself must validate well
under the original agnostic distribution, or the class must support a legal
patch that uses the new behavior on $F$ and keeps $h$ on $F^c$. The source
does not provide such patching. A hypothesis can be excellent on $Q_F$ and
arbitrarily bad on the rest of $P$; properness prevents the wrapper from
freely splicing it with $h$.

So there are really three separate missing objects:

1. a low-noise filter for the hidden comparator $c^\star$;
2. a legal regional complement for the failed candidate $h$; and
3. a proper patching or validation guarantee that makes the local repair
   useful under the original marginal.

Depth 3 ruled out the first object as a consequence of failure alone. The
calculation above identifies the second and third as neutralizer assumptions
in disguise.

### Conditional Positive Lemma

This branch suggests a clean conditional theorem, but the condition is not
implied by the source.

Assume that for every marginal $P$, every legal $h$, and every agnostic
distribution for which $h$ fails the weak target, there is a
validation-searchable polynomial list of legal concepts containing some
$g$ with the following two properties:

1. $g$ agrees with $-h$ on enough of $F=\{Y\ne h(X)\}$ to gain inverse
   polynomial correlation under the failure filter; and
2. $g$ is not much worse than $h$ on $F^c$, or can be legally patched with
   $h$ off $F$.

Then the edge would be true. Run the low-noise coupling branch first. In the
middle-noise branch, maintain a candidate list, validate it on fresh
agnostic samples, and whenever a candidate fails, use the regional-complement
list plus the distribution-free source on the corresponding clean filtered
marginals to add new proper candidates. Polynomially many improvements would
reach the weak target because each successful repair raises validated
correlation by an inverse-polynomial amount.

The issue is that this lemma has essentially assumed the missing selector.
The source guarantees efficient weak learning once clean legal labels are
available; it gives no mechanism for finding the regional complement or for
patching a local complement into a globally legal proper hypothesis.

### False-Witness Implications

The same analysis sharpens the negative route. A false witness should not
only make the hidden comparator hard to recover from noisy labels. It should
also block the observable complement repair.

A useful witness would have one fixed marginal $P$ and a family of
regressions for which:

1. exact clean labels from any legal target remain distribution-free weakly
   properly learnable;
2. when a natural legal candidate $h$ fails, the event $F=\{Y\ne h(X)\}$
   labels examples as an observable complement or regional complement that
   is not legal, not searchable, or not globally patchable; and
3. every efficiently reachable legal concept with near-neutral correlation
   under $P$ either fails validation or decodes a hidden hard witness.

This is stronger than ordinary noisy recovery hardness. Parities and codes
fail because neutral wrong concepts satisfy the weak target. Handle
constructions fail because the handles are often exactly the
validation-searchable regional complements. Hard active slices fail because
marginals concentrating on the slice threaten the distribution-free clean
source. The witness must make "clean labels are easy" and "observable
complements are not legal neutralizers" coexist.

### Depth 5 Directions

1. **Regional-complement theorem test.** Try to prove or refute the
   conditional lemma above for standard source-positive classes: point
   functions, homogeneous parities, monotone conjunction-like classes without
   explicit complements, and fixed-$k$ DNF variants. The test is not whether
   the class is agnostically learnable, but whether every failed candidate
   exposes a legal local complement that validates globally.
2. **$X$-dependent filter barrier.** Extend the two-cell example to
   polynomially many $X$-dependent filters by putting the hidden
   $1/2+\varepsilon$ clean set in a pseudorandom or diagonal subset of a
   finite support. The goal is a formal statement that any polynomial
   failure-filter wrapper either calls the source off-promise or assumes a
   regional complement.
3. **No-neutral valid-code search.** Build a valid-code class where clean
   labels reveal enough signature information for distribution-free weak
   proper learning, but the observable complement of a failed candidate is
   not a valid codeword and every valid near-neutral output leaks the hidden
   signature. The hard part is preventing the signatures from becoming
   public handles.

### Conclusion

The candidate-failure filter obstruction is sharper than "the labels are
noisy." The filtered labels are clean for an observable complement and noisy
for the hidden comparator. Distribution-free source uniformity makes the
filtered marginal affordable, but it does not make the observable complement
legal or globally patchable. I therefore still do not see a true proof.
Nor is there a false witness yet, because a separation must rule out neutral
proper outputs while preserving distribution-free clean weak proper learning.

## Depth 5 Branch A: Hidden Patch Regions

### Aim

Depth 4 framed the obstruction as an observable-complement problem. I tried
to push that into either a positive theorem or a witness. The useful
clarification is a dichotomy:

1. if a candidate $h$ really fails the weak agnostic target, then a legal
   regional complement exists in an existential sense, namely the
   comparator $c^\star$ itself on the region where it disagrees with $h$;
2. weak realizable proper learning still gives no way to find the profitable
   disagreement region or to patch a local complement into a global proper
   output.

So the source does not force literal complements. But a false witness cannot
merely say "complements are absent." Whenever absence of complements prevents
large profitable flips, the weak target becomes easy or vacuous. A separation
has to make profitable legal patches exist but computationally hidden.

### Failure As A Disagreement-Region Selector

Fix a marginal $P$, regression $r(x)=\mathbf E[Y\mid X=x]$, and a legal
candidate $h\in\mathcal C_s$. For any legal $g$, write
$$
D_{h,g}=\{x:g(x)\ne h(x)\}.
$$
In $\{\pm1\}$ notation, changing from $h$ to $g$ changes correlation by
$$
\langle r,g\rangle_P-\langle r,h\rangle_P
=2\mathbf E_P[-r(X)h(X)\mathbf 1_{D_{h,g}}(X)].
$$
Thus, relative to a failed $h$, the repair problem is not "learn clean
labels." It is:
$$
\text{find a legal disagreement region }D_{h,g}
\text{ with large positive weight under }
\phi_h(x)=-r(x)h(x).
$$

This is a weak agnostic selection problem over the proper disagreement
family
$$
\Delta_h=\{D_{h,g}:g\in\mathcal C_s\}.
$$
Legal complements and patching assumptions solve this because they let us
turn a profitable region into a legal $g$. But the source learner only says
that, given clean labels from a legal concept, it can output a legal concept
weakly correlated with those labels. It says nothing about optimizing the
signed weight $\phi_h$ over $\Delta_h$.

This is exactly where the regional-complement proof attempt breaks. If $h$
fails, there is a near-optimal $c^\star$ such that
$$
\langle r,c^\star\rangle_P-\langle r,h\rangle_P>1-2\alpha.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\mathbf E_P[-r h\mathbf 1_{D_{h,c^\star}}]>\frac12-\alpha.
$$
So $c^\star$ is a legal patch: it flips $h$ on $D_{h,c^\star}$ and keeps
$h$ off that region. On the high-gain part
$B=\{Y\ne h(X),\,c^\star(X)\ne h(X)\}$, it is also a legal regional
complement of $h$.

But $D_{h,c^\star}$ and $B$ are hidden. The observable filter
$F=\{Y\ne h(X)\}$ contains $B$, yet it may also contain a large part where
$c^\star=h$. On all of $F$, the labels are clean for the observable
complement $-h$, not for $c^\star$. Unless $-h$ itself is legal, or unless
the class has a searchable way to localize $B$ or $D_{h,c^\star}$, a clean
source call on $F$ is still off-promise for the comparator.

### What Absence Of Complements Actually Shows

I tested the strongest possible negative reading: perhaps a class can be
weakly properly learnable in the clean distribution-free sense while lacking
regional complements so badly that the agnostic target fails. The simple
examples do not support that conclusion.

Point functions are the cleanest sanity check. Let
$c_i(x)=+1$ at one point and $-1$ elsewhere. They are distribution-free
properly learnable: if the positive atom has noticeable mass, clean samples
identify it; if it has tiny mass, many legal point functions are already
weakly close to the target. They have no useful global complements. For
$h=c_i$ under a marginal spread over many points, the observable complement
$-h$ is $+1$ on many atoms and is not a point function, nor even close to
one. But this absence does not make a false witness. If labels are
deterministically $Y=-h(X)$ on that spread-out support, then every legal
point function also disagrees with those labels on almost all points. The
best comparator is poor, so $h$ does not fail the weak target by a
constant-size amount. When a point function does beat $h$ by nearly
$1/2$, the gain is concentrated on one or a few heavy atoms, and samples
make the relevant legal regional complement visible.

Homogeneous parities give the opposite sanity check. Literal complements are
not legal if affine parities are excluded. Still, under the uniform marginal,
wrong homogeneous parities are neutral. In the middle-noise regime
$\operatorname{OPT}\ge\alpha_P$, neutral proper outputs already satisfy the
weak additive target. Under nonuniform marginals, a failed parity candidate
does imply a profitable legal disagreement parity, but finding it from noisy
labels is again a selector over disagreement regions, not a complement
existence theorem.

Monotone conjunction-like classes behave similarly. The complement of a
variable is not monotone. If the labels are simply $\neg x_i$, then the
failed variable has an observable complement that is not legal. But unless
some monotone conjunction captures a large profitable part of that
complement, the best monotone comparator is near random and the weak target
can be met by a bland legal fallback. If such a conjunction does capture the
profit, then a legal regional complement exists; the unresolved part is
finding it under noisy labels.

These examples show that clean weak proper learning definitely does not
force literal complements or arbitrary patching. They also show why that
fact alone does not refute the edge. The weak agnostic benchmark is
OPT-relative, so regions that no legal concept can exploit do not create a
hard requirement.

### The False-Witness Shape After This Reduction

A real false witness has to live in a narrower gap:

1. exact clean labels must make every legal target weakly properly learnable
   under every marginal, with one uniform polynomial;
2. for one fixed marginal $P$, the hard regressions must create a failed
   public or easily reached candidate $h$;
3. there must exist a legal profitable patch $c^\star$, otherwise the weak
   target does not ask for much; and
4. every polynomial-time deterministic proper output with correlation above
   the weak threshold must effectively identify such a hidden patch.

This rules out the easy "no complement" witnesses. If no legal regional
complement exists, $M(r)$ falls with it. If many neutral legal concepts are
available, one of them satisfies the middle-noise target. If public handles
are added so the clean learner can always output something proper when the
signature is absent from the sample, those handles tend to become the
agnostic fallbacks.

The remaining candidate is a valid-code or signature class where clean
examples expose the hidden patch but noisy examples do not. The difficulty
is still the no-neutral condition. Pairwise-neutral families, such as
parity-like codes, leak acceptable wrong proper outputs. Forcing every wrong
valid concept to be inverse-polynomially anti-correlated with the hidden
one runs into the same Gram/Plotkin pressure noted earlier unless the number
of possible hidden patches is only polynomial, in which case brute-force
validation is no longer a hardness source. Cryptographic or diagonal
validity restrictions might hide all neutral concepts, but then the clean
distribution-free source must explain how to output a valid proper concept
on marginals where the signature-bearing region is rarely sampled. That
usually reintroduces a public fallback, and the fallback is exactly what the
agnostic learner can validate.

### Conditional Positive Statement

The disagreement-region formulation gives a sharper conditional positive
lemma than Depth 4.

Assume that for every fixed marginal $P$ and every legal $h$, the weighted
selector problem over
$$
\Delta_h=\{\{x:g(x)\ne h(x)\}:g\in\mathcal C_s\}
$$
has a marginal-nonuniform weak proper implementation: given samples with
weights $\phi_h(x)=-r(x)h(x)$, it can either find a legal $g$ with
noticeably positive flip gain whenever such a $g$ exists, or certify that no
such gain exceeds the weak threshold. Then the original implication is true.
Use the low-noise clean-transcript branch when
$\operatorname{OPT}<\alpha_P$. In the middle-noise branch, start from any
deterministic proper output of the source on a dummy transcript, validate it,
and repeatedly invoke the $\Delta_h$ selector on failed candidates. Each
successful flip increases validated correlation by an inverse polynomial;
when no profitable flip remains, the candidate is within the required weak
OPT-relative tolerance.

This is not a proof from the source. It names the missing object more
precisely. The clean source gives weak learning of concepts from clean
concept labels. The conditional lemma asks for weak agnostic optimization of
legal disagreement regions around the current concept. That is a different
oracle unless the class has complements, patching, an efficiently searchable
proper cover, or some special algebraic structure.

### Depth 6 Directions

1. **Disagreement-selector hardness.** Work on a finite support and try to
   build a source-positive class for which $\Delta_h$ is weakly hard to
   optimize under one fixed marginal, even though clean labels for every
   concept in $\mathcal C$ are weakly properly learnable. This is sharper
   than a generic agnostic hardness attempt.
2. **Patch-localization theorem.** Try to prove that for natural
   source-positive classes, every high-gain disagreement region contains a
   polynomially searchable subregion or trace certificate. Point functions
   satisfy this for atomic reasons; parities satisfy the target for
   neutrality reasons. The useful test cases are monotone conjunctions
   without both constants and restricted DNF variants.
3. **No-public-fallback valid codes.** Revisit signature classes with the
   explicit requirement that the clean learner remains distribution-free
   proper when signature mass is tiny, but the mechanism used in that case
   does not give a validation-searchable neutral concept for the hard
   marginal.

I therefore still cannot resolve the atlas edge. Depth 5 narrows the
regional-complement obstruction: failed candidates do force an existential
legal patch, but only through the hidden comparator. The source learner does
not make that patch searchable, and absence of literal complements by itself
does not produce a counterexample because the OPT-relative benchmark ignores
unexploitable complement regions.

## Depth 6 Branch A: Hidden Patches Need A Selector

### Aim

Depth 5 reduced the middle-noise obstruction to hidden profitable
disagreement regions. I tried to decide whether the stronger
distribution-free source makes those regions searchable. My conclusion is
negative for the available proof strategies: distribution-free weak
realizable proper learning makes every clean auxiliary marginal affordable,
but it still does not turn the agnostic signed weights into clean legal
labels. A true proof still needs a new selector theorem, and a false proof
still needs a no-neutral hidden-patch witness.

The useful normalization is the following. Fix $P$, a regression
$r(x)=\mathbf E[Y\mid X=x]$, and a current legal candidate
$h\in\mathcal C_s$. For each legal $g$, let
$$
D_{h,g}=\{x:g(x)\ne h(x)\}.
$$
Then
$$
\langle r,g\rangle_P-\langle r,h\rangle_P
=2\mathbf E_P[-r(X)h(X)\mathbf 1_{D_{h,g}}(X)].
$$
So after $h$ fails validation, the missing repair is exactly a weak
optimizer for the signed measure
$$
\phi_h(x)=-r(x)h(x)
$$
over the proper disagreement family
$$
\Delta_h=\{D_{h,g}:g\in\mathcal C_s\}.
$$

This is not the same task as clean weak learning of $\mathcal C$. It is an
agnostic weighted selection problem for regions induced by pairs of proper
concepts.

### Why Distribution-Free Calls Do Not Locate The Patch

Suppose $h$ fails the target. Then some near-optimal $c^\star$ gives a
profitable region $D_{h,c^\star}$. The observed failure event
$$
F=\{Y\ne h(X)\}
$$
is public, but it is too coarse. On $F$, the observed label is perfectly
clean for the observable complement $-h$, not for the hidden comparator
$c^\star$. It is cleanly usable by the source only if that complement, or a
legal regional replacement for it, belongs to the class and can be patched
globally. That is precisely the extra structure we do not have.

The distribution-free source improves one issue from the sibling
marginal-source tree: if we somehow name a clean auxiliary marginal $Q$ and
a legal clean target, the source runs with the same polynomial bound. But it
does not supply the legal clean target. Reweighting or conditioning on
observable events gives samples from $Q$ with labels whose conditional mean
may be only $1/2+\varepsilon$ aligned with $c^\star$. Calling the realizable
source on those labels is still off-promise.

The finite-trace route has the same shape. On a trace of size $k=O(\log s)$,
one can enumerate all labelings and use the source to complete realizable
ones, but such local completions have uncontrolled behavior on the rest of
$P$. On a trace large enough to certify $P$-correlation at inverse-polynomial
scale, enumerating $2^k$ labelings is not polynomial. Distribution-freeness
removes the auxiliary-runtime objection, not the label-search or
off-support-control objection.

An adaptive wrapper around the source therefore has only three reliable
ways to generate candidates:

1. run low-noise coupled calls on the original marginal;
2. synthesize clean labels from proper concepts already found; or
3. use additional class structure, such as legal complements, patching,
   constants, a balanced sampler, or a searchable proper cover.

None of these operations, by itself, names the hidden
$D_{h,c^\star}$. Validation can choose among generated candidates, but it
cannot validate a candidate that never enters the list.

### Why Hiding The Patch Is Still Not A Witness

The false direction is narrower than "make complements illegal." If no legal
concept can exploit the public complement region, then
$M(r)=\sup_{c\in\mathcal C_s}\langle r,c\rangle_P$ drops as well, and the
weak OPT-relative target asks for less. To refute the edge, a witness must
make a profitable legal patch exist while hiding every acceptable patch from
polynomial-time agnostic sampling.

For noisy hidden-code regressions
$$
r_y=(1-2\eta)c_y,
$$
a wrong proper hypothesis $h$ is acceptable in the middle-noise regime
$\eta\ge\alpha_P$ whenever it is approximately neutral:
$$
\langle c_y,h\rangle_P\gtrsim
\frac{-2\eta+2\alpha_P}{1-2\eta}.
$$
Thus ordinary recovery hardness is insufficient. LPN, parity, and standard
codeword recovery examples leak neutral wrong proper hypotheses. To make the
target fail, every efficiently reachable nondecoding proper output would
have to be noticeably anti-correlated with the hidden concept.

That no-neutral requirement is exactly where the known witness shapes break:

1. Pairwise-neutral or near-orthogonal families leave acceptable wrong
   concepts.
2. Forcing all wrong valid concepts to be inverse-polynomially
   anti-correlated under one fixed marginal runs into the same Gram/Plotkin
   pressure noted in earlier branches unless the family is small enough to
   brute force.
3. Adding public handles to preserve distribution-free clean weak learning
   usually gives the agnostic learner a validation-searchable fallback.
4. Removing the handles can make exact clean learning fail on marginals
   concentrated where the hidden patch must be found.

So a separation would need something more specialized: exact clean labels
must reveal enough information for weak proper learning under every
marginal, while noisy labels under one fixed marginal must make it hard to
output even a neutral deterministic proper concept. I do not know a standard
cryptographic, PCP, lattice, LPN, or coding primitive that gives this package
without restating the target failure.

### Conditional Statement That Would Resolve The Edge

The branch does give a precise conditional positive theorem. If, for every
fixed marginal $P$ and every legal $h$, the induced disagreement-family
selector for
$$
\Delta_h=\{\{x:g(x)\ne h(x)\}:g\in\mathcal C_s\}
$$
is marginal-nonuniformly weakly solvable from samples weighted by
$\phi_h(x)=-r(x)h(x)$, then the implication is true.

The proof is direct. Use the same-$P$ low-noise coupling when
$\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}$ is below the source transcript scale.
Otherwise start from any proper candidate generated by an on-promise source
call or a fixed legal default. If validation says the candidate fails, a
near-optimal comparator supplies a positive-gain member of $\Delta_h$; the
selector returns a legal $g$ with inverse-polynomial flip gain. Validation
keeps only real improvements. After polynomially many gains, either no
profitable disagreement region remains or the candidate satisfies
$$
\langle r,h\rangle_P\ge M(r)-1+2\alpha_P(s).
$$

This conditional theorem is not a proof from the source. It says exactly
what is missing: weak agnostic optimization of legal disagreement regions.

### Depth 7 Directions

1. **Finite-support disagreement-selector separation.** Fix one polynomial
   support $X_s$, one marginal $P_s$, and one public candidate $h_0$. Build,
   or rule out, a class where clean labels for every legal concept are
   weakly properly learnable distribution-free, but optimizing
   $\mathbf E[\phi\mathbf 1_D]$ over
   $\Delta_{h_0}$ is hard even to inverse-polynomial advantage. This should
   be stated as a hardness problem for $\Delta_{h_0}$, not as generic
   agnostic learning.
2. **Exact-clean/noisy-no-neutral primitive.** Try to instantiate the needed
   witness directly: concepts $c_y$, a fixed marginal $P_s$, exact-clean
   weak decoding under every marginal, and noisy regressions for which every
   polynomial-time proper output with
   $\langle r_y,h\rangle_{P_s}\ge -s^{-k}$ decodes $y$. Test the primitive
   first against the neutral wrong-codeword leak and the weighted
   Gram/Plotkin obstruction.
3. **Reachable-hull theorem.** For a fixed source learner, define the convex
   hull of proper concepts reachable by polynomially many on-promise calls,
   self-labeling calls, finite-trace calls, and validation. Prove that this
   hull must contain a same-$P$ neutral point, or build an oracle-style class
   where all reachable points remain anti-correlated with a hidden patch.

I therefore leave the edge unresolved. Distribution-free source uniformity
does make clean regional calls cheap once the region and legal target are
known, but it does not make the profitable disagreement region searchable
from agnostic samples. Conversely, no current witness hides all such regions
while preserving every-marginal clean weak proper learning and blocking
neutral proper outputs.

## Depth 7 Branch A: Final Consolidation

### Final Question

This is the last allowed branch for the open implication

$$
\text{efficient weak realizable proper PAC}
\Rightarrow
\text{efficient marginal-nonuniform weak agnostic proper PAC}.
$$

I tried one final time to turn the distribution-free strength of the source
into the missing proper agnostic output. The attempt still stops at the same
place as Depths 5 and 6. The source gives a uniform weak learner on every
clean realizable marginal, including every empirical, conditional, and
reweighted marginal. That is a genuine improvement over the sibling
marginal-source edge. But the middle-noise agnostic problem asks for a
proper hypothesis selected from arbitrary signed label information, and the
source gives no mechanism for that selection once exact clean transcripts
disappear.

I therefore do not have a resolution as true or as false. The best final
state is an atlas-ready open note: the low-noise branch is positive; the
remaining obstruction is the proper selector/neutralizer problem in the
middle-noise regime; and the plausible counterexample templates all fail
because they either leak neutral proper outputs or break the
distribution-free clean source.

### Normalization

Fix a representation size $s$. Let the source learner have sample bound
$m(s,\log(1/\delta))$ and weak advantage $\gamma(s)>0$, both polynomial or
inverse-polynomial uniformly over all marginals. Thus, for every distribution
$Q$ over instances and every legal concept $c\in\mathcal C_s$, clean samples
$(X,c(X))$ with $X\sim Q$ let the learner output $h\in\mathcal C_s$ with

$$
\langle c,h\rangle_Q\ge 2\gamma(s)
$$

with high probability.

For the agnostic target, fix a marginal $P$ and write

$$
r(x)=\mathbf E[Y\mid X=x],
\qquad
M(r)=\sup_{c\in\mathcal C_s}\langle r,c\rangle_P.
$$

The target guarantee

$$
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(h)
\le
\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}(\mathcal D)
+\frac12-\alpha_P(s)
$$

is equivalent to

$$
\langle r,h\rangle_P\ge M(r)-1+2\alpha_P(s).
$$

Here $\alpha_P(s)$ may be marginal-dependent inverse-polynomial. Since the
source is distribution-free, the low-noise branch can even use a
$P$-independent inverse-polynomial scale, but the full target only needs a
fixed-$P$ one.

### What Is Resolved Positively

The low-noise branch is sound and should be kept in the atlas note. Let
$c^\star$ be a near-optimal legal comparator and let
$\eta=\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(c^\star)$. If $\eta$ is below the
inverse transcript scale, for example

$$
\eta \le O\left(\frac{\gamma(s)}{m(s)}\right),
$$

then a noisy sample fed to the source agrees with a clean sample labeled by
$c^\star$ with inverse-polynomial probability. On that event, the source
returns a proper $h$ with

$$
\operatorname{err}_P(h,c^\star)\le \frac12-\gamma(s).
$$

Therefore

$$
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(h)
\le
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(c^\star)
+\operatorname{err}_P(h,c^\star)
\le
\eta+\frac12-\gamma(s).
$$

Repetition and empirical validation over the proper outputs make this branch
algorithmic. The key point for this edge is that distribution-freeness makes
the source advantage and transcript bound uniform in $P$. No
marginal-nonuniform bookkeeping is needed in the low-noise regime.

### Where The Last Positive Attempt Fails

For the complementary regime, write

$$
\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}(\mathcal D)=\frac{1-M(r)}2.
$$

If $\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}(\mathcal D)\ge \alpha_P(s)$, then the
required correlation threshold is nonpositive:

$$
M(r)-1+2\alpha_P(s)\le 0.
$$

So the middle-noise branch would be solved by a validation-searchable proper
hypothesis with nonnegative, or only slightly negative, correlation against
$r$. Legal constants, complement pairs, balanced proper samplers, and public
proper shadow covers all give this kind of fallback. The source assumption
does not appear to force any of them.

The most precise obstruction is the hidden disagreement-region selector.
Given a current proper candidate $h$, every legal $g$ differs from $h$ on

$$
D_{h,g}=\{x:g(x)\ne h(x)\}.
$$

The correlation gain from replacing $h$ by $g$ is

$$
\langle r,g\rangle_P-\langle r,h\rangle_P
=
2\mathbf E_P[-r(X)h(X)\mathbf 1_{D_{h,g}}(X)].
$$

Thus a failed $h$ identifies an existential profitable patch: some
near-optimal $c^\star$ has a high-weight disagreement region
$D_{h,c^\star}$. But the region is hidden. The source can learn from clean
concept labels on a named marginal; it cannot optimize the signed measure
$\phi_h(x)=-r(x)h(x)$ over the family

$$
\Delta_h=\{D_{h,g}:g\in\mathcal C_s\}.
$$

That optimization is already a weak agnostic selector, now phrased as a
proper disagreement-family problem.

### Regional Complements And Hidden Patch Regions

The failure filter gives the same obstruction in observable form. If

$$
F=\{Y\ne h(X)\},
$$

then on $F$ the labels are perfectly clean for the observable complement
$-h$. They are not necessarily clean, or even low-noise, for the hidden
comparator $c^\star$. A clean call to the source on $F$ is therefore
on-promise only if $-h$ is legal on that conditional marginal, or if there is
a legal regional complement that can be found and patched into a globally
proper hypothesis.

When $h$ genuinely fails, $c^\star$ is itself a legal patch in the
existential sense: it flips $h$ on the profitable hidden region
$D_{h,c^\star}$. The hard part is not existence. It is localizing the region
or producing a legal global replacement from noisy samples. This is why
"the class lacks complements" is not enough for a false result. If no legal
concept can exploit the complement-like region, $M(r)$ drops and the
OPT-relative weak target relaxes. A counterexample would need profitable
legal patches that exist but are computationally hidden.

### Why Distribution-Free Uniformity Is Not Enough

Distribution-free source uniformity buys exactly this:

1. point masses, small traces, conditional marginals, and reweighted
   marginals are all affordable;
2. if a legal clean target is named on any such marginal, the source gives a
   proper weak completion; and
3. the low-noise clean-transcript branch is uniform in the original marginal
   $P$.

It does not buy any of the following:

1. a rule for choosing the hidden trace labeling of a near-optimal
   comparator;
2. a guarantee that noisy labels on a filter are clean enough for the source;
3. off-support control for concepts completed from small traces;
4. a polynomial enumeration of large trace labelings;
5. legal constants, complements, patching, or balanced samplers; or
6. a same-$P$ proper hypothesis whose correlation is neutral in the
   middle-noise regime.

This is the final reason the positive proof does not close. The stronger
source removes the auxiliary-marginal rate obstruction, but not the label
selection or proper neutralization obstruction.

### Failed Witness Audit

I also do not see an atlas-ready false witness.

Point functions show that absence of complements alone is too weak. When a
different point function gives a large gain, the heavy atom causing the gain
is sample-visible. When no such atom exists, the best point-function
comparator is itself poor enough that the weak OPT-relative target is easy.

Homogeneous parities, LPN, and codeword-recovery examples leak neutral wrong
proper hypotheses. For noisy hidden concepts

$$
r_y=(1-2\eta)c_y,
$$

a wrong proper hypothesis with near-zero correlation against $c_y$ already
meets the middle-noise target whenever $\eta\ge\alpha_P(s)$; when
$\eta<\alpha_P(s)$ the low-noise branch applies. Ordinary recovery hardness
therefore does not separate this edge.

Halfspaces are also not a witness for the atlas target. Known agnostic
proper hardness results are aimed at substantially stronger accuracy
requirements, and standard halfspace classes contain legal constant
halfspaces, giving the middle-noise neutralizer.

PCP, clause-satisfaction, hard-slice, and one-way handle constructions still
have the right selector flavor but the wrong package of quantifiers. If the
hard slice can receive arbitrary marginal mass, distribution-free clean weak
proper learning threatens to become as hard as solving the slice. If public
handles are added to preserve the clean source, the same handles tend to be
validation-searchable proper fallbacks for the agnostic target.

The remaining possible false template is a valid-code or signature class
with an exact-clean/noisy-no-neutral primitive: exact clean labels reveal
enough information for distribution-free weak proper learning under every
marginal, while noisy labels under one fixed marginal make every acceptable
proper output decode the hidden signature. I do not know how to instantiate
this. Near-orthogonal codes leak neutral outputs, and forcing all wrong valid
concepts to be inverse-polynomially anti-correlated under one fixed marginal
runs into Gram/Plotkin-type pressure unless the family is small enough for
brute-force validation.

### Conditional True Principles

The following principles would each resolve the edge as true, but none is
implied by the current source assumption.

**Proper neutralizer principle.** For every fixed marginal $P$, there is a
single marginal-nonuniform procedure that produces a polynomial list or
sampler of legal concepts containing a member with
$\langle r,h\rangle_P\ge -1/\operatorname{poly}_P(s)$ for every
middle-noise regression $r$. Legal constants, complement pairs, or balanced
proper samplers are special cases.

**Regional-complement principle.** Whenever a validated proper candidate
$h$ fails the weak target, a polynomially searchable list contains a legal
concept that behaves like the observable complement on enough of
$F=\{Y\ne h(X)\}$ and is not too damaging off that region, or can be legally
patched with $h$ off that region.

**Disagreement-selector principle.** For every fixed $P$ and legal $h$, the
weighted selector over

$$
\Delta_h=\{\{x:g(x)\ne h(x)\}:g\in\mathcal C_s\}
$$

can weakly optimize the signed weights $\phi_h(x)=-r(x)h(x)$ to
inverse-polynomial advantage. Then one can iterate: validate $h$, invoke the
selector when $h$ fails, replace $h$ by the returned legal $g$, and stop
after polynomially many validated inverse-polynomial gains.

These principles clarify the missing lemma. They do not follow from clean
weak proper learning itself, because they require agnostic signed-weight
selection among proper concepts.

### Proposed Atlas Summary

Suggested replacement summary:

Open: the distribution-free source gives a uniform low-noise agnostic
robustification by clean-transcript coupling, but the middle-noise regime
still needs a same-marginal proper neutralizer or hidden
disagreement-region selector. No known witness blocks all neutral proper
outputs while preserving distribution-free clean weak proper learning.

### Proposed Atlas Proof Status Body

**Goal.** Decide whether an efficient distribution-free weak realizable
proper learner yields an efficient marginal-nonuniform weak agnostic proper
learner.

**Uniform low-noise branch.** Let the source learner have uniform clean
advantage $\gamma(s)$ and sample bound $m(s,\log(1/\delta))$. If the
agnostic labels have error below the inverse transcript scale relative to a
near-optimal concept $c^\star$, a noisy source call agrees with a clean call
on labels $c^\star(X)$ with inverse-polynomial probability. Repetition and
validation over proper outputs give

$$
\operatorname{err}_{\mathcal D}(h)
\le
\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}(\mathcal D)
+\frac12-\Omega(\gamma(s)).
$$

This part is positive, and distribution-freeness makes it uniform over
marginals.

**Middle-noise obstruction.** When
$\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}(\mathcal D)\ge\alpha_P(s)$, the target is
satisfied by any legal hypothesis with roughly nonnegative correlation
against the regression $r(x)=\mathbf E[Y\mid X=x]$. Clean weak proper
learning does not supply a validation-searchable legal constant, complement,
balanced sampler, proper shadow cover, or OPT-relative selector. If a proper
candidate $h$ fails, a near-optimal comparator identifies a profitable
hidden disagreement region
$D_{h,c^\star}=\{x:c^\star(x)\ne h(x)\}$, and the needed repair is weak
optimization of

$$
\mathbf E_P[-r(X)h(X)\mathbf 1_D(X)]
$$

over legal disagreement regions
$D\in\Delta_h=\{D_{h,g}:g\in\mathcal C_s\}$. The source learner works once
a clean legal target on a chosen marginal is available; it does not find
this signed region from agnostic labels.

**Regional complements.** The observable failure filter
$F=\{Y\ne h(X)\}$ has labels clean for $-h$, not for the hidden comparator.
Thus a source call on the filter is justified only under extra structure:
legal complements, searchable regional complements, or proper patching.
These are neutralizer assumptions in another form.

**Why standard witnesses miss.** Parity, LPN, and code constructions leak
neutral wrong proper hypotheses in the middle-noise regime. Halfspaces have
legal constants and known hardness targets a stronger accuracy requirement.
Point-function and monotone examples show that missing complements do not
matter unless a legal profitable patch exists. PCP, hard-slice, and handle
templates either break distribution-free clean weak learning on concentrated
marginals or add public fallbacks that solve the agnostic weak target.

**Conclusion.** The implication remains open. A positive resolution would
derive a same-$P$ proper neutralizer or disagreement-region selector from
distribution-free weak realizable proper learning. A negative resolution
would require a fixed-marginal no-neutral-output witness: exact clean labels
remain distribution-free weak properly learnable, but every proper output
meeting the weak OPT-relative agnostic threshold decodes a hidden hard
patch.
