## Depth 1 Branch A: Fixed Slack Needs Observable Forcing

**Edge read first.** The atlas edge note currently marks

$$
\texttt{efficient-weak-agnostic-proper-pac}
\Rightarrow
\texttt{efficient-agnostic-improper-pac}
$$

as open. The note's diagnosis is accurate: proper weak outputs are not the
main obstruction, because the target may output an improper vote over the
proper hypotheses. The obstruction is whether the fixed-additive weak
agnostic guarantee can be boosted to arbitrary excess error in fully
representation-polynomial time.

**Interface translation.** Let the weak tolerance be
$\beta<1/2$, and write labels in $\{-1,1\}$. For a distribution $D$,
let

$$
\rho_C(D)=\sup_{c\in C}\mathbb E_D[Yc(X)].
$$

Since $\operatorname{err}(h)=(1-\operatorname{corr}(h,Y))/2$, the source
guarantee

$$
\operatorname{err}_D(h)\le \operatorname{OPT}_C(D)+\beta
$$

is equivalent to

$$
\operatorname{corr}_D(h,Y)\ge \rho_C(D)-2\beta.
$$

Thus the proper weak learner is a nontrivial agnostic correlation weak learner
in the da Cunha--Hogsgaard--Paudice sense with parameters
$(\gamma_0,\epsilon_0)=(1,2\beta)$, so $\gamma_0>\epsilon_0$. Its outputs are
in $C$, so any booster whose base hypotheses are weak outputs can aggregate
them improperly.

The same calculation also shows why fixed slack is weak in the wrong way for
observable residual boosting. A valid weak learner is forced to return a
positive-edge hypothesis only when

$$
\operatorname{OPT}_C(D)<\gamma,\qquad \gamma=1/2-\beta.
$$

When $\operatorname{OPT}_C(D)\ge \gamma$, the guarantee permits a
progress-neutral response even if the strong learner still needs much finer
information about the best comparator.

**Positive theorem check.** I checked the current primary sources around the
edge: Feldman 2010, Ghai--Singh 2024/2025, da Cunha--Hogsgaard--Paudice--Sun
2025, and da Cunha--Hogsgaard--Paudice 2026. The split is still the one
recorded in the edge note.

Feldman-style distribution-specific boosting and Ghai--Singh-style
unlabeled-data boosting have the right computational shape: they call weak
learners on observable relabeled distributions and output improper aggregates.
However, their weak interface needs accuracy at the final scale. In the
correlation formulation, Ghai--Singh's guarantee retains a residual term of
order $\epsilon_0/\gamma$; to get $\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$, the weak
oracle's additive correlation slack must be $O(\gamma\varepsilon)$. The atlas
source provides only the fixed slack $2\beta$.

The da Cunha 2026 theorem is the genuine near-positive result. It accepts any
nontrivial agnostic weak learner with $\gamma_0>\epsilon_0$, hence it accepts
the fixed-additive source after the translation above. But its stated running
time is polynomial in the sample size only with the weak-learner parameters
held fixed. The TeX source gives weak-learner call and runtime bounds of the
form

$$
O(n^{m_0+3})
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Eval}_H(1)\cdot
n^{O(m_0\min\{d^*,\ln n\}/\theta^2)},
$$

where $m_0$ is the weak learner's sample parameter, $d^*$ is a dual VC
dimension parameter, and $\theta=\gamma_0-\epsilon_0$. In the atlas efficient
node, $m_0$ may be polynomial in the representation size even when $\beta$ is
a fixed constant. Then $n^{m_0}$ is not polynomial in the representation size
and $1/\varepsilon$. So this proves a statistical or parameter-fixed positive
statement, not the full efficient PAC implication.

**Why the enumeration appears, and why properness does not fix it.** The
da Cunha proof makes the fixed slack useful by finding weak hypotheses that
work on clean or nearly clean subsets relative to a hidden comparator. If
$f\in C$ is near-optimal and

$$
S_f=\{(x_i,y_i): f(x_i)=y_i\},
$$

then on distributions supported on $S_f$, the comparator error is zero, so the
fixed-additive learner must return a real weak edge. The algorithm does not
know $f$ or $S_f$, so it enumerates $m_0$-tuples from the sample to make sure a
good weak-learner input is present.

This is exactly where the atlas runtime breaks. Properness of the weak outputs
only says those menu hypotheses lie in $C$; it does not provide observable
access to the hidden clean subset. Since the target is improper, once such
hypotheses are found they can be combined. The missing step is finding them in
polynomial time without enumerating $S^{m_0}$.

**Diagnostic obstruction.** Consider a noisy hidden-comparator distribution:
labels are generated by an unknown $f\in C$ and flipped with rate

$$
\eta\in(\gamma,1/2).
$$

The original distribution has $\operatorname{OPT}_C(D)\le \eta$, so the fixed
weak learner need not reveal anything on $D$ itself. A strong agnostic learner
with $\varepsilon\ll 1/2-\eta$ still needs information about $f$. Useful fixed
slack calls arise on low-noise or clean samples relative to $f$. If a query is
built by drawing an $m_0$-tuple from the observable noisy data, the probability
that its empirical flip rate is at most $\gamma$ is at most

$$
\Pr[\operatorname{Bin}(m_0,\eta)\le \gamma m_0]
\le \exp(-m_0 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\gamma\|\eta)).
$$

For $m_0=\operatorname{poly}(s)$, polynomially many random observable attempts
miss these low-noise witnesses with overwhelming probability. This explains
why brute-force tuple enumeration is so tempting. It is a black-box obstacle,
not a separation: a non-black-box learner could exploit the representation, and
the diagnostic does not itself construct a class satisfying the distribution-
free weak source.

**False-witness search.** I do not see an atlas-valid counterexample at this
depth. Halfspaces, noisy parity, LPN, and PRF-style classes fail for the same
reason: hard low-noise or realizable prediction already refutes the weak
agnostic source. Max-CSP and PCP approximation templates tend to refute proper
learning, but the target here is improper; all-one predictors, lookup tables on
polynomial supports, or validation over public handles often destroy the
intended hardness. Public weak shields can make fixed-additive learning easy,
but if they are public enough, improper strong learners can usually validate
and combine them; if the hard core is hidden enough to defeat improper strong
learning, the distribution-free adversary can put mass there and kill the weak
source.

**Depth 2 directions.**

1. Prove or refute an observable fixed-slack forcing lemma: from samples of
   $D$, a current predictor, and a proper fixed-additive weak learner, generate
   polynomially many samplable queries so that if the current predictor has
   excess error $>\varepsilon$, at least one query has $\operatorname{OPT}<\gamma$
   and every valid weak response gives progress.
2. Try to replace the $n^{m_0}$ clean-tuple enumeration with a polynomial
   hitting, compression, or stability argument. The object would need to hit
   every hidden comparator's adaptive clean-subset distributions for an
   arbitrary black-box weak learner.
3. Separate the parameter-fixed theorem from the full edge. If $m_0$, $d^*$,
   and $\theta$ are constants or otherwise bounded so that the da Cunha runtime
   is polynomial in the representation and accuracy parameters, the implication
   is true by known boosting. The full atlas edge still needs a fully
   polynomial dependence on those weak-learner parameters, or a hard class
   satisfying the source.

**Atlas action.** I would not mark the edge true or false. A precise atlas
update, if desired, would keep `status: "open"` and emphasize that the edge is
true for statistical or parameter-fixed fixed-slack boosting, while the
efficient distribution-free statement is blocked by the lack of a polynomial
observable clean-witness generator.

## Depth 2 Branch A: Coarse Slack Still Does Not Boost

**Branch goal.** I reread Depth 1 Branch A and compared the sibling
`efficient-weak-agnostic-improper-pac -> efficient-agnostic-improper-pac`
scratchpad. The sibling confirms that the proper/improper distinction is not
the active obstruction: once the weak learner has produced hypotheses in
$\mathcal C$, the target may output an improper signed vote or validation
aggregate over them. This branch therefore focuses only on the fixed-additive
slack.

Let the source tolerance be $\beta=1/2-\gamma<1/2$. In correlation notation the
proper weak learner gives, for every labeled distribution $Q$,

$$
\operatorname{corr}_Q(h,Y)
\ge
\rho_C(Q)-2\beta,
\qquad
\rho_C(Q)=\sup_{c\in C}\operatorname{corr}_Q(c,Y).
$$

Because the final learner is improper, it may validate the returned proper
hypothesis, flip its sign in the final vote, or assign it a real weight. Thus
the most generous black-box lower envelope one should credit the source with is

$$
\text{usable edge}\ge \max\{0,\rho_C(Q)-2\beta\}.
$$

This is already the best-case interpretation for proper weak outputs; if it is
insufficient, properness is not the missing ingredient.

**Accuracy-sensitive boosters still leave a constant floor.** Feldman,
Kalai--Kanade, and Ghai--Singh style boosters use a correlation oracle of the
form

$$
\operatorname{corr}(W,Y)\ge a\rho_C(Q)-\tau,
$$

where the final excess error scales like $\tau/a$ or otherwise requires this
ratio to be $O(\varepsilon)$. The fixed-additive source cannot instantiate such
an oracle at arbitrary accuracy. Indeed, to have

$$
\max\{0,\rho-2\beta\}\ge a\rho-\tau
$$

for all $\rho\in[0,1]$, evaluate at $\rho=2\beta$. The left side is $0$, so
$\tau\ge 2a\beta$, hence

$$
\tau/a\ge 2\beta.
$$

Shrinking $a$ only shrinks $\tau$ by the same factor; it does not make the
residual ratio small. This rules out the tempting parameter-rescaling repair
for the efficient accuracy-sensitive boosters. They prove the nearby theorem
with tunable slack, not the atlas fixed-slack edge.

**Repeated proper calls do not manufacture slack.** Repetition reduces failure
probability, but it does not improve the approximation scale. A valid source
learner may return the same neutral proper hypothesis on every high-OPT query.
A majority vote over repeated outputs then preserves the same coarse error, and
validation can only choose among candidates already generated. It cannot force
a candidate whose correlation is below the fixed observability floor.

This also blocks the simplest minimax interpretation. The source is a
constant-additive approximate linear optimizer over $C$. In a bounded
zero-one/correlation game, a constant-additive best-response oracle generally
solves the game only to constant additive accuracy. One cannot amplify the
objective by scaling the labels or weights, because weak-call distributions are
normalized probability distributions with labels in $\{\pm1\}$.

**No observable denoising route found.** The low-noise diagnostic from Depth 1
remains the cleanest obstruction. Suppose

$$
Y=f(X)N,\qquad f\in C,\qquad \Pr[N=-1]=\eta,
$$

with $\eta\in(\gamma,1/2)$. Then
$\operatorname{OPT}_C(D)\le \eta$, and the best raw correlation is
$1-2\eta<1-2\gamma=2\beta$. A strong agnostic learner with
$\varepsilon\ll 1/2-\eta$ still needs information about $f$, but the fixed
weak guarantee permits a neutral response on the original distribution.

Could an observable relabeling or reweighting amplify the hidden bias without
knowing $f$? In a generic label-channel call, the new label $Y'$ is generated
from $(X,Y)$, the current predictor, and fresh randomness. Conditional on
$X=x$, the $f$-dependent component still carries the factor $1-2\eta$:

$$
\mathbb E[Y'\mid X=x]=a(x)+b(x)(1-2\eta)f(x),
\qquad |b(x)|\le 1,
$$

with $a,b$ observable from the booster state. Reweighting can change the
average over $x$, but unless the weights are correlated with the hidden clean
event $\{Y=f(X)\}$, this does not raise the comparator signal above the fixed
threshold $2\beta$. Known fixed-slack boosters cross this gap by searching for
hidden clean or nearly clean weak-learner inputs; the known search is the
$n^{m_0}$-type enumeration discussed in Depth 1.

**Why this is not yet a false edge.** The obstruction above is still a barrier
to generic boosting, not a class separation. A false atlas update would require
a represented class with an efficient proper learner satisfying

$$
\operatorname{err}(h)\le \operatorname{OPT}_C+\beta
$$

for every labeled distribution, while no polynomial-time improper learner
achieves $\operatorname{OPT}_C+\varepsilon$. The standard templates still miss
one side.

Fixed-$k$ DNF separates weak proper learning from strong proper learning, but
it is not a witness here because the target is improper. Halfspaces, parity,
LPN, PRF, and hidden-region templates can make strong agnostic learning hard,
but distribution-free weak agnostic proper learning then fails on realizable or
low-noise distributions concentrated on the hard region. CSP and PCP
approximation templates run into the signed-label/improper-output problem:
positive-label encodings are defeated by constants, while signed encodings make
the weak source essentially the hard optimization problem.

**Current obstruction.** The edge remains open. The fixed-additive source gives
a proper hypothesis oracle with a real constant dead zone. Improper aggregation
removes the final-output constraint, but it does not remove the dead zone. The
known residual-free fixed-slack theorem accepts the source semantically, yet
currently pays for hidden clean witnesses with weak-sample or capacity
parameters in the exponent. The known fully polynomial boosters avoid that
enumeration only by assuming a tunable weak slack.

**Depth 3 directions.**

1. Formalize an observable forcing lemma or prove it impossible: if a current
   predictor has excess $>\varepsilon$, a polynomial procedure must generate a
   reweighted/relabelled weak call with $\operatorname{OPT}_C<1/2-\beta$ whose
   valid proper response gives progress.
2. Look for a fully polynomial replacement for the clean-witness enumeration:
   stability of the weak learner, compression search, or a hard-core-set
   construction whose list size is polynomial in $m_0$, capacity parameters,
   $1/(1/2-\beta)$, and $1/\varepsilon$.
3. Search for a semi-agnostic approximation witness: a class with efficient
   distribution-free proper $\operatorname{OPT}+\beta$ learning, but
   computational hardness of improper $\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$
   learning at a fixed high-noise distribution.

## Depth 3 Branch A: Certification Gap

**Branch target.** I focused on the remaining loophole after Depth 2:
perhaps the lower bound
$$
\tau/a\ge 2\beta
$$
only rules out Feldman/Kalai--Kanade/Ghai-style correlation interfaces, while
a more nonstandard booster could validate, flip, vote, filter, or otherwise
aggregate the proper weak outputs into an arbitrary improper predictor. I also
checked whether the same fixed floor can be promoted from a black-box barrier
to an atlas-valid separation.

I refreshed the primary theorem landscape on 2026-05-07. The relevant pages
still match the local edge note: Ghai--Singh 2025
<https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/ghai25a.html> is fully polynomial but
accuracy-sensitive, while da Cunha--Hogsgaard--Paudice 2026
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11265> accepts fixed nontrivial slack
semantically but states polynomial running time in the sample size only with
the other parameters fixed. I did not find a newer primary-source theorem that
gives a representation-uniform polynomial fixed-additive agnostic booster.

**Why aggregation does not by itself remove the floor.** Let
$\beta=1/2-\gamma$. On a weak-call distribution $Q$, the source only forces a
below-random proper response when
$$
\operatorname{OPT}_C(Q)<\gamma .
$$
The final learner may be improper, so it may validate a returned concept,
negate it, weight it, and combine it with all other returned concepts. That
helps after useful candidates have appeared. It does not force such candidates
to appear. If every observable weak call made by the booster has
$\operatorname{OPT}_C(Q_i)\ge\gamma$, then a valid source learner may return
progress-neutral proper hypotheses on all calls. A holdout sample can select
the best element of this neutral list, but it cannot certify the hidden
comparator-dependent low-OPT distribution that was never queried.

This is the same obstruction in convex-game language. The source is a
constant-additive approximate linear optimizer over $C$:
$$
\operatorname{corr}(h,Y)\ge \rho_C(Q)-2\beta .
$$
For any attempted interface
$$
\operatorname{corr}(h,Y)\ge a\rho_C(Q)-\tau ,
$$
the point $\rho_C(Q)=2\beta$ forces $\tau/a\ge 2\beta$. Passing to a convex
hull or signed vote over generated hypotheses cannot improve the oracle's
worst-case accuracy before the hypotheses are generated. It only optimizes over
the menu already exposed.

**Observable denoising remains circular.** The low-noise diagnostic can be made
more explicit. Suppose
$$
Y=f(X)N,\qquad f\in C,\qquad \Pr[N=-1]=\eta,
$$
with $\gamma<\eta<1/2$. Then $\operatorname{OPT}_C(D)\le\eta$, but the best
raw comparator correlation is $1-2\eta<2\beta$, so the fixed weak source may be
silent on $D$ even though an $\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$ learner must
recover information about $f$.

The simplest nonstandard trick is agreement filtering. If a current predictor
$g$ has hidden correlation
$$
r=\mathbb E[g(X)f(X)],
$$
and we keep only examples satisfying $Y=g(X)$, then the conditional label noise
relative to $f$ is
$$
\Pr[Y\ne f(X)\mid Y=g(X)]
=
\frac{\eta(1-r)}
{(1-\eta)(1+r)+\eta(1-r)}.
$$
This falls below the weak threshold $\gamma$ only when
$$
r>\frac{\eta-\gamma}{\eta+\gamma-2\eta\gamma}.
$$
So filtering can create a forced low-OPT weak call only after the algorithm
already has nontrivial, sometimes very large, correlation with the hidden
comparator. Random relabeling, gradient labels, and reweighting face the same
ceiling: conditional on $X=x$, any label channel whose inputs are observable
from $(X,Y)$ and the current transcript has its $f$-dependent part multiplied
by the original bias $1-2\eta$ unless the transcript already contains a
predictor correlated with $f$. Thus the denoising route is circular in the
distribution-free model, where repeated labels at the same $x$ or a product
noise structure are not available.

**Clean-tuple enumeration is still the only known fixed-slack escape.** The
da Cunha-style proof escapes the fixed floor by finding weak-learner inputs
that are clean, or nearly clean, relative to a hidden near-best comparator. If
the weak learner needs $m_0$ samples and a useful transcript requires an
$m_0$-tuple inside the hidden clean set $G\subseteq S$ of density $p$, then a
comparator-independent menu $L$ of $q$ ordered tuples hits a uniformly random
$G$ with probability at most about
$$
q p^{m_0}.
$$
For $m_0=\operatorname{poly}(s)$, no generic polynomial menu can hit every
possible hidden clean set. This explains why the known residual-free
fixed-slack algorithm pays $n^{m_0}$-type costs. A successful positive proof
would need to avoid clean-tuple hitting entirely, or use extra structure of the
class/weak learner that is not present in the atlas node.

**Why this is not yet a false edge.** The barrier above is black-box. It shows
that a polynomial reduction cannot rely only on the fixed-additive guarantee
and observable high-noise calls. It does not produce a class satisfying the
source while failing the target. An atlas-valid separation needs a represented
class with a public polynomial-time proper learner achieving
$$
\operatorname{err}(h)\le \operatorname{OPT}_C+\beta
$$
for every labeled distribution, and yet no polynomial-time improper learner
achieving $\operatorname{OPT}_C+\varepsilon$.

The usual candidates still fail one side. Halfspaces and LPN-like classes give
high-noise agnostic hardness, but they also fail weak agnostic learning on
low-noise or realizable hard distributions. PCP/CSP templates give
constant-approximation hardness in optimization form, but signed labels and
improper outputs either make constants or lookup tables too powerful, or make
the weak-source obligation as hard as the target. Public weak-handle
constructions make proper weak learning easy, but the same handles are visible
to an improper strong learner, which can validate, memorize, or aggregate them.

The most plausible separation route remains semi-agnostic: find a class with
an efficient distribution-free proper constant-factor or constant-additive
semi-agnostic learner that implies the fixed $\operatorname{OPT}+\beta$ source,
while $\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$ improper learning is hard under one
explicit distribution family. I do not know such a witness, and the existing
atlas witnesses do not supply it.

**Current resolution state.** I would not update the atlas edge to true or
false. The constant residual floor is a real obstruction to every observable
aggregation route I can make precise, including validation, voting, filtering,
surrogate relabeling, and polynomial clean-tuple menus. But the obstruction is
still a reduction/barrier statement, not a class separation. Conversely, the
only known theorem that accepts fixed additive slack still has weak-sample or
capacity parameters in exponents, so it is not a representation-uniform
efficient PAC proof.

**Depth 4 directions.**

1. Prove a formal black-box lower bound for fixed-slack boosters: construct an
   oracle that is neutral on all high-OPT observable queries and useful only
   on hidden low-OPT $m_0$-tuples, then show polynomial-query boosters fail.
   State explicitly that this is an oracle barrier, not an atlas separation.
2. Search for the semi-agnostic separation witness: a class with efficient
   distribution-free proper $\operatorname{OPT}+\beta$ learning, or a
   fixed-factor guarantee strong enough to imply it, but improper
   $\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$ learning hard under a standard assumption.
3. Try to prove an observable forcing lemma under an additional structural
   promise, such as stable compression, tolerant low-noise decoding, or a
   polynomial clean-set hitting family. That would identify exactly what extra
   node makes da Cunha-style fixed-slack boosting fully polynomial.

## Depth 4 Branch A: Low-Noise Parity Separation

**Branch target.** I revisited the earlier dismissal of parity/LPN witnesses.
That dismissal is right for a constant weak gap: if the source had to handle
constant-noise parity, it would already collide with LPN hardness. It is not
right under the atlas inverse-polynomial weak-gap convention recorded in
`agnostic-boosting-open`. With a gap of order $1/n$, affine parities look like
an atlas-valid conditional false witness.

The proposed witness is the class $\mathcal C_n$ of affine parities on
$\{0,1\}^n$:
$$
c_{a,b}(x)=\langle a,x\rangle \oplus b,\qquad a\in\{0,1\}^n,\ b\in\{0,1\}.
$$
This is a proper class: a hypothesis is just $(a,b)$. It contains both
constant concepts, because $a=0$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$ are legal.

**Source side: proper weak agnostic learning is easy at inverse-polynomial
gap.** Set
$$
\lambda_n=\Theta(1/n),\qquad \beta_n=1/2-\lambda_n .
$$
I claim affine parities are efficiently weak agnostically properly learnable
with tolerance $\beta_n$.

The learner builds a polynomial candidate list and validates it. First include
the two constant parities. Then repeat $O(\log(1/\delta))$ times:

1. draw a fresh batch of $m=O(n)$ labeled examples;
2. solve the affine linear system over $\mathbb F_2$ induced by the batch;
3. if it is consistent, add one consistent affine parity to the list.

Finally, draw a validation sample large enough to estimate the error of every
listed parity to accuracy $\lambda_n/4$, and output the empirically best
candidate. This is polynomial in $n$, $1/\lambda_n$, and $\log(1/\delta)$.

Let $\alpha=\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}(D)$, and fix a best affine parity
$f$ with $\operatorname{err}_D(f)=\alpha$.

If $\alpha\ge 2\lambda_n$, one of the two constant parities has error at most
$1/2$. Validation then returns a candidate with true error at most
$1/2+\lambda_n/4$, which is at most
$$
\alpha+1/2-\lambda_n=\alpha+\beta_n
$$
for this regime.

If $\alpha<2\lambda_n$, a random $O(n)$ batch is clean relative to $f$ with
constant probability, after choosing the hidden constant in
$\lambda_n=\Theta(1/n)$ small enough. Condition on such a clean batch. For any
affine parity $h$ with
$$
\operatorname{err}_D(h)>\alpha+1/2-2\lambda_n,
$$
write
$$
q=D[h(X)\ne f(X),\ Y=f(X)],\qquad
r=D[h(X)\ne f(X),\ Y\ne f(X)].
$$
Then
$$
\operatorname{err}_D(h)-\operatorname{err}_D(f)=q-r,
$$
so $q>1/2-2\lambda_n$. On the clean conditional distribution, $h$ disagrees
with the clean labels with probability at least
$$
\frac{1/2-2\lambda_n}{1-\alpha}.
$$
Since $\alpha<2\lambda_n$, the probability that this fixed bad $h$ is
consistent with all $m=O(n)$ clean examples is at most
$(1/2+O(\lambda_n))^m$. A union bound over the $2^{n+1}$ affine parities shows
that, with constant probability conditioned on cleanliness, every parity
consistent with the batch has error at most
$$
\alpha+1/2-2\lambda_n.
$$
Repeating produces such a candidate with high probability, and validation loses
only $\lambda_n/4$, so the final error is still at most
$$
\alpha+1/2-\lambda_n=\alpha+\beta_n .
$$

Thus the source is satisfied by a genuinely proper weak learner. The proof is
not black-box boosting; it uses the representation directly. The proper
constants are the high-OPT certificates, and Gaussian elimination on a clean
small batch is the low-OPT certificate.

**Target side: strong improper agnostic learning breaks decision-LPN.** Assume
constant-noise decision-LPN is hard: for some fixed $\eta\in(0,1/2)$, no
randomized polynomial-time algorithm distinguishes samples
$$
(X,\langle a,X\rangle\oplus b\oplus N),\qquad X\sim U_n,\quad
N\sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(\eta),
$$
from uniformly random labeled samples with non-negligible advantage. This is
the standard noisy-parity pseudorandomness assumption from the LPN line of
work; see Blum--Furst--Kearns--Lipton 1993
<https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-48329-2_24> and the agnostic-parity connection
in Feldman--Gopalan--Khot--Ponnuswami 2009
<https://doi.org/10.1137/070684914>.

If affine parities were efficiently agnostically learnable improperly, run the
learner on the uniform $\eta$-noisy parity distribution with accuracy
$$
\varepsilon < (1/2-\eta)/4.
$$
The optimum parity error is $\eta$, so the learner outputs an arbitrary
hypothesis $H$ with
$$
\operatorname{err}(H)\le \eta+\varepsilon<1/2
$$
with constant probability. On fresh labeled examples from the same oracle, the
correlation of $H(X)$ with $Y$ is then at least
$$
1-2(\eta+\varepsilon)>0.
$$
On fresh uniformly random labels, conditional on the training transcript and
on $H$, this correlation has expectation $0$ and concentrates near $0$.
Estimating the fresh correlation therefore distinguishes LPN samples from
random labels in polynomial time. This contradicts decision-LPN. The reduction
uses no properness restriction on the target; any improper predictor with
error below $1/2$ is already enough to distinguish.

**What this says about the certification gap.** The separation is exactly the
certification gap made concrete. In the constant-noise LPN distribution,
wrong affine parities and constants are neutral under the uniform marginal.
They are good enough for the weak source whenever
$\operatorname{OPT}\ge\lambda_n$, but they certify no information about the
hidden parity. Strong agnostic learning at fixed excess accuracy must cross
below $1/2$ at noise $\eta$, and that is precisely the forbidden LPN
prediction/distinguishing task.

This also explains why the previous black-box clean-witness obstruction was
not merely cosmetic. The weak learner only has to find clean witnesses when
the optimum error is below the inverse-polynomial gap; a small clean batch can
do that for affine parities. At the constant-noise point where strong learning
would be hard, the source is allowed to answer with a neutral proper
hypothesis.

**Atlas update if conditional assumptions are allowed.** Under the
parameterized inverse-polynomial reading of the weak additive tolerance, this
would justify changing the edge to conditional false:

- `status: "false"`
- `evidence: conditional-counterexample`
- `assumptions: ["constant-noise decision-LPN hardness"]`
- `witnesses: ["affine-parities"]`
- summary: affine parities have an efficient proper
  $\operatorname{OPT}+1/2-\Theta(1/n)$ weak agnostic learner by constants,
  clean-batch Gaussian elimination, and validation, but efficient strong
  agnostic improper learning would distinguish constant-noise LPN from random
  labels.

The caveat is schema-level, not mathematical: if this atlas edge is instead
intended to require a fixed constant gap $1/2-\beta$ independent of
representation size, then this parity witness does not satisfy the source, and
the edge returns to the earlier open state.

## Depth 5 Branch A: Fixed-Constant Gap Audit

**Branch goal.** This pass treats the atlas definition literally: the source
has one fixed additive tolerance
$$
\beta=1/2-\gamma<1/2
$$
with fixed constant $\gamma>0$, not merely a parameterized
$\gamma=\Theta(1/n)$ gap. Under that reading, the Depth 4 affine-parity
branch is a useful model-fork but not a resolution of the present edge.

The actual question remains:
$$
\text{fixed-constant-gap proper weak agnostic learning}
\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}
\text{fully polynomial strong agnostic improper learning}.
$$

**Why the affine-parity witness does not satisfy the source.** For affine
parities under the uniform marginal, choose any fixed $\gamma>0$ and let
labels be generated by a hidden parity with independent noise
$$
\eta<\gamma ,
$$
say $\eta=\gamma/2$. Then
$$
\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}=\eta
$$
and a fixed-gap weak agnostic proper learner would have to output an affine
parity with error at most
$$
\eta+1/2-\gamma=1/2-(\gamma-\eta)<1/2 .
$$
Every wrong affine parity, and every constant parity, has error exactly $1/2$
on this distribution. Thus the source learner would have to recover the
hidden affine parity with nontrivial probability from constant-noise LPN
samples. That is exactly the standard hard task used on the target side.

So the Depth 4 argument only separates a parameterized weak-gap variant:
clean-batch Gaussian elimination can handle $\gamma=\Theta(1/n)$ because a
small sample is clean with inverse-polynomial probability. It cannot be
upgraded to fixed constant $\gamma$ without solving constant-noise LPN or an
equivalent constant-radius robust decoding problem.

**Positive theorem audit.** The fixed constant gap makes the semantic weak
oracle stronger than the inverse-polynomial fork, but not in the way needed by
known fully polynomial agnostic boosters. In $\{\pm1\}$ notation the source
gives
$$
\operatorname{corr}_Q(h,Y)
\ge
\rho_C(Q)-2\beta,
\qquad
\rho_C(Q)=\sup_{c\in C}\operatorname{corr}_Q(c,Y).
$$
After validation and sign choice, the usable black-box lower envelope is
$$
\max\{0,\rho_C(Q)-2\beta\}.
$$
Any attempted reduction to a scale-sensitive correlation oracle
$$
\operatorname{corr}(h,Y)\ge a\rho_C(Q)-\tau
$$
still has the same floor: evaluating at $\rho_C(Q)=2\beta$ forces
$$
\tau/a\ge 2\beta=1-2\gamma .
$$
For fixed $\gamma$, this is a fixed constant residual, not the
$O(\varepsilon)$ residual needed for Feldman/Kalai--Kanade/Ghai--Singh style
boosting to reach $\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$.

The da Cunha--Hogsgaard--Paudice fixed-slack theorem remains the closest
positive result. The current arXiv page for the 2026 version says it was last
revised on 2026-02-03 and that its running time is polynomial in the sample
size when the other problem parameters are fixed:
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11265>. That accepts the fixed constant source
semantically, but the known bounds still put the weak learner's sample
parameter $m_0$ and capacity parameters in exponents. Since $m_0$ may be
polynomial in the representation size even when $\gamma$ is a fixed constant,
this is still fixed-parameter rather than representation-uniform polynomial
time. I did not find a primary-source theorem removing that dependence.

**Certification obstruction for a fixed gap.** The low-noise password picture
survives the fixed constant correction. Let
$$
Y=f(X)N,\qquad f\in C,\qquad \Pr[N=-1]=\eta,
$$
with
$$
\gamma<\eta<1/2.
$$
The original distribution has $\operatorname{OPT}_C\le \eta$, so the
fixed-additive source may legally return a neutral hypothesis on it, while a
strong learner with $\varepsilon\ll 1/2-\eta$ must still learn information
about $f$.

If an observable procedure tries to denoise by keeping examples on which the
label agrees with a current predictor $g$, and
$$
r=\mathbb E[g(X)f(X)],
$$
then the selected label noise relative to $f$ is
$$
\Pr[Y\ne f(X)\mid Y=g(X)]
=
\frac{\eta(1-r)}
{(1-\eta)(1+r)+\eta(1-r)}.
$$
This drops below the weak threshold $\gamma$ only if
$$
r>
\frac{\eta-\gamma}{\eta+\gamma-2\eta\gamma}.
$$
Thus observable filtering produces a forced low-$\operatorname{OPT}$ weak call
only after the transcript already contains substantial correlation with the
hidden comparator. Generic relabeling, reweighting, validation, and compression
have the same start-up problem: they can exploit a useful candidate after it is
generated, but they do not certify the hidden clean or low-noise call from
high-noise data.

This is not a proof of falsity. It is a barrier to generic fixed-slack
boosting. A non-black-box strong learner might exploit structure of the class
directly, and a source learner might be more generous than the fixed-additive
guarantee requires on high-$\operatorname{OPT}$ distributions.

**False-witness audit.** I do not see a fixed-constant-gap
source-positive witness.

Halfspaces and monomials have strong agnostic hardness evidence, but that
evidence is too strong for this edge: Tiegel's halfspace lower bound rules out
even weak improper prediction when the optimum is tiny, and the
Feldman--Guruswami--Raghavendra--Wu monomial result similarly attacks weak
agnostic learning in a low-OPT regime. These classes fail the source rather
than separating it from the target.

Fixed-$k$ DNF and the one-way weak-handle constructions explain proper
weak-versus-strong separations, but their hardness uses a proper target or a
fixed-marginal/proper-output trap. The present target is distribution-free and
improper. Once the weak handles or active atoms are visible, an improper
learner can often validate, aggregate, or memorize them, and the existing
arguments no longer force inversion or NP search.

Coding and LPN templates remain the most plausible high-noise hardness
families. The missing source lemma is severe: one would need an efficient
proper weak decoder for every PAC marginal and every weighted corruption
pattern with error below a fixed constant $\gamma$, while high-noise
deterministic improper prediction remains hard under some explicit marginal.
Ordinary unique decoding under a uniform Hamming metric does not satisfy this
distribution-free weighted requirement, and cryptographic high-noise hardness
usually also makes constant low-noise weak recovery hard.

The semi-agnostic route is also still only a target, not a witness. A proper
learner with a guarantee such as
$$
\operatorname{err}(h)\le A\,\operatorname{OPT}_C+\varepsilon_0
$$
for fixed $A$ and small enough $\varepsilon_0$, together with constants, would
imply a fixed-additive weak source after choosing $\gamma$ with
$A\gamma+\varepsilon_0<1/2$. If the same class were hard for improper
$\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$ learning, the edge would be false. I do not
know a class with that proper semi-agnostic positive theorem and an improper
strong agnostic lower bound for the same benchmark.

**Current resolution state.** The actual fixed-constant edge is still open.
Depth 4 gives a conditional false result only for a parameterized weak-gap
variant. For the atlas source, I found neither a fixed-constant-gap hard
witness nor a fully polynomial fixed-slack booster. The obstruction is now
sharper: fixed slack is useful only below a constant low-noise threshold, and
known ways to reveal that threshold either enumerate hidden clean witnesses or
assume extra structure not present in the node.

**Depth 6 directions.**

1. Formalize the fixed-gap posterior/label-channel barrier. Prove, in the
   noisy-comparator model with $\eta>\gamma$, that transcript-measurable
   relabeling or selection cannot certify a query with
   $\operatorname{OPT}<\gamma$ unless the transcript already has correlation
   at least
   $(\eta-\gamma)/(\eta+\gamma-2\eta\gamma)$ with the hidden comparator on the
   selected mass.
2. Search specifically for constant-radius weighted decoding classes: public
   proper weak decoding for every PAC marginal below a fixed error radius, but
   cryptographic or average-case hardness of high-noise deterministic improper
   prediction under one marginal.
3. Test the proper semi-agnostic approximation route. Find or rule out a class
   with efficient distribution-free proper $A\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$
   learning for constant $A$, while improper
   $\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$ learning remains hard.

## Depth 6 Branch A: Constant-Gap Phase Transition Audit

**Branch target.** I treated the source as a fixed-constant statement:
there is a constant
$$
\beta=1/2-\gamma<1/2,\qquad \gamma>0
$$
independent of the representation size. The inverse-polynomial affine-parity
branch from Depth 4 is therefore only a model fork. For the present edge, a
false witness must be source-positive at a constant low-noise radius, while a
positive proof must give a representation-uniform polynomial booster for this
constant fixed slack.

**Current theorem route still stops short.** I rechecked the live theorem
landscape on 2026-05-07. The relevant definitions line up as before. In
$\{\pm1\}$ notation the source gives
$$
\operatorname{corr}_Q(h,Y)\ge \rho_C(Q)-2\beta,
\qquad
\rho_C(Q)=\sup_{c\in C}\operatorname{corr}_Q(c,Y).
$$
Thus it is a da Cunha--Hogsgaard--Paudice agnostic weak learner with
$(\gamma_0,\epsilon_0)=(1,2\beta)$, and it is nontrivial because
$\epsilon_0<\gamma_0$.

The 2025 da Cunha--Hogsgaard--Paudice--Sun result proves the statistical
nontrivial-slack theorem, but explicitly says the algorithm is not
computationally efficient. The 2026 ALT version improves this to polynomial
time in the sample size when the other parameters are fixed, but Theorem 2
still invokes the weak learner up to
$$
O(n^{m_0+3})
$$
times and gives an overall bound of the form
$$
\operatorname{Eval}_H(1)\cdot
n^{O(m_0\min\{d^*,\ln n\}/\theta^2)} .
$$
Here $m_0$ is the source weak learner's sample parameter, $d^*$ is the dual VC
dimension of the base class, and $\theta=(\gamma_0-\epsilon_0)/2$. In the atlas
edge, $m_0$ and $d^*$ may grow polynomially with the representation size even
when $\gamma$ is a fixed constant. This is not the required polynomial in the
representation size and $1/\varepsilon$.

The efficient Ghai--Singh/Feldman/Kalai--Kanade style route remains
accuracy-sensitive. The 2026 comparison section states the same obstruction:
those algorithms need the weak residual $\epsilon_0$ to scale with the final
accuracy, e.g. $\epsilon_0=O(\varepsilon)$ or $O(\gamma_0\varepsilon)$. The
atlas source supplies only the fixed residual $2\beta$.

The Hopkins--Kane--Lovett--Mahajan realizable-to-agnostic reduction also does
not rescue the efficient edge. It gives the right sample-complexity moral, but
its basic reduction runs the realizable learner over all labelings of an
unlabeled sample and the paper explicitly notes that this is inherently
computationally inefficient. So the chain
$$
\text{weak agnostic proper}
\Rightarrow
\text{weak realizable proper}
\Rightarrow
\text{strong realizable improper}
\Rightarrow
\text{strong agnostic improper}
$$
is valid only information-theoretically, not as a representation-uniform
polynomial-time implication.

**A fixed-gap false witness has to be a noise-threshold class.** Adding
constants to a candidate class makes the source trivial whenever
$\operatorname{OPT}_C(D)\ge \gamma$, because a constant has error at most
$1/2\le \operatorname{OPT}_C(D)+1/2-\gamma$. Therefore the nontrivial source
obligation is exactly the low-noise regime:
if some $f\in C$ has error $\alpha<\gamma$, the proper learner must return a
legal concept with error at most
$$
\alpha+1/2-\gamma=1/2-(\gamma-\alpha).
$$
In words, it must be a distribution-free proper weak decoder for every
weighted, adversarial corruption pattern below a fixed constant radius.

A false witness must then be easy below this radius but hard at some larger
constant noise level $\eta\in(\gamma,1/2)$. This is a much sharper requirement
than ordinary agnostic hardness. Most known hard classes fail because their
hardness already reaches into the low-noise region where the source would need
to succeed.

- Affine parities satisfy this only for an inverse-polynomial radius. For any
  fixed $\gamma>0$, uniform parity with noise $\eta<\gamma$ would force a weak
  source learner to recover a nontrivial correlation with the hidden parity,
  which is already the low-noise LPN task.
- Halfspaces and monomials are worse for this edge. Tiegel-style halfspace
  hardness and the Feldman--Guruswami--Raghavendra--Wu monomial result rule
  out even weak prediction when the optimum is tiny or when a monomial is
  correct on a $1-\epsilon$ fraction of examples. These are source failures,
  not separations.
- DNF and conjunction hardness gives good improper-target pressure under
  refutation assumptions, but the known source-positive weak-DNF arguments are
  inverse-polynomial or proper-output separations. The monomial/conjunction
  agnostic hardness results again bite in the low-OPT regime needed by the
  source.
- One-way weak-handle classes can make a fixed weak proper source true, but
  the known handles are public enough that an improper learner can memorize,
  validate, or sparse-ERM over the exposed atoms. They separate proper targets,
  not the present improper target.

This leaves a very specific possible counterexample shape: a public
constant-radius weighted decoder for a concept family under every PAC
marginal, together with hardness of deterministic prediction at a larger
constant noise rate under one explicit marginal. I do not know a standard
coding, LPN, PCP, or cryptographic class with both properties. Usual codes
with efficient unique decoding assume full-word or structured random errors,
not arbitrary PAC marginals and weighted corruptions. Usual high-noise
cryptographic assumptions also make constant low-noise weak recovery hard.

**Why clean-transcript robustification does not give the fixed source.** One
might try to start with an efficient weak realizable learner and run it until
its training transcript is clean relative to the best comparator. If the weak
learner uses $m_0(s)$ examples and the noise rate is $\alpha$, this succeeds
with probability roughly
$$
(1-\alpha)^{m_0(s)}.
$$
For $m_0(s)=\operatorname{poly}(s)$ and fixed $\alpha>0$, this is
exponentially small. This is exactly why the affine-parity clean-batch argument
only gives an inverse-polynomial gap and why the da Cunha-style fixed-slack
algorithm pays an $n^{m_0}$ clean-witness cost. To get a fixed constant source
from this route, one would need an unusually tiny weak transcript or a genuine
noise-tolerant weak decoder, and either extra structure would have to survive
the hard high-noise target distribution.

**No polynomial fixed-slack forcing lemma found.** The noisy-comparator
obstruction from Depth 5 is still the right local barrier. Let
$$
Y=f(X)N,\qquad \Pr[N=-1]=\eta,\qquad \gamma<\eta<1/2.
$$
On the original distribution, the best correlation is $1-2\eta<1-2\gamma=2\beta$,
so the fixed source may be silent. Any observable label channel built from
$(X,Y)$ and the transcript has its fresh $f$-dependent signal multiplied by
$1-2\eta$ unless the transcript already contains a predictor correlated with
$f$. Agreement filtering makes this quantitative: conditioning on $Y=g(X)$
drops the noise below $\gamma$ only if
$$
\mathbb E[g(X)f(X)]>
\frac{\eta-\gamma}{\eta+\gamma-2\eta\gamma}.
$$
Thus the booster needs substantial hidden-comparator correlation before it can
force a low-$\operatorname{OPT}$ weak call. Validation and improper voting help
after such a candidate appears; they do not explain how to make the first
candidate appear in fully polynomial time.

**Resolution state.** I would still leave the atlas edge open. The fixed
constant correction kills the affine-parity separation, and the theorem route
is still short of a representation-uniform polynomial-time booster. The
remaining negative route is now narrow enough to state cleanly: find a class
with efficient distribution-free proper weak decoding under every weighted
corruption of rate below a fixed $\gamma$, but with improper
$\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$ learning hard at some fixed noise
$\eta>\gamma$. I do not currently have such a class.

**Depth 7 directions.**

1. **Weighted-decoder witness test.** Look for a represented code/concept
   family with an efficient proper algorithm that, for every marginal $P$ and
   every adversarial corruption rate $<\gamma$, returns a legal hypothesis with
   constant edge, while noisy prediction at some $\eta>\gamma$ under one
   simple marginal is LPN/LWE/refutation hard. Reject candidates that only
   decode full words, random errors, or one fixed metric.
2. **Polynomial fixed-slack theorem test.** Try to replace the
   $n^{m_0}$ clean-witness step in da Cunha--Hogsgaard--Paudice with a
   polynomial list generator whose size is polynomial in $m_0$, $d^*$,
   $1/\theta$, and $1/\varepsilon$. A successful lemma must produce the first
   low-$\operatorname{OPT}$ weak call without already having correlation with
   the hidden comparator.
3. **Formal oracle barrier.** Prove a black-box lower bound for fixed-slack
   boosters in the noisy-comparator model: an oracle is neutral on every
   transcript-measurable high-OPT query and useful only on hidden clean
   witnesses. This would not resolve the atlas edge, but it would justify that
   Depth 7 should focus on non-black-box structure or a concrete decoder
   witness.

## Depth 7 Branch A: Final Consolidation

**Final read of the edge.** Under the literal atlas definition, the source has
one fixed constant
$$
\beta=1/2-\gamma<1/2,\qquad \gamma>0,
$$
independent of the representation size. The target is fully strong and
improper: for every requested $\varepsilon>0$, it must output an arbitrary
hypothesis with error at most $\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathcal C}+\varepsilon$ in
time polynomial in the representation size, $1/\varepsilon$, and
$\log(1/\delta)$.

I do not see a final true or false resolution. The best state is an
atlas-open edge with sharp conditional principles on both sides.

**Fixed-constant slack barrier.** In $\{\pm1\}$ notation the source gives, on
every weak-call distribution $Q$,
$$
\operatorname{corr}_Q(h,Y)\ge \rho_C(Q)-2\beta,\qquad
\rho_C(Q)=\sup_{c\in C}\operatorname{corr}_Q(c,Y).
$$
After validation, sign choice, and improper voting, the most generous generic
usable edge is still
$$
\max\{0,\rho_C(Q)-2\beta\}.
$$
Thus the source is forced to reveal useful correlation only when
$\operatorname{OPT}_C(Q)<\gamma$. Any attempt to recast it as a
scale-sensitive oracle
$$
\operatorname{corr}_Q(h,Y)\ge a\rho_C(Q)-\tau
$$
has the same obstruction: at $\rho_C(Q)=2\beta$ the left side may be zero, so
$\tau/a\ge 2\beta$. This leaves a constant residual floor, not the
$O(\varepsilon)$ residual needed by the fully polynomial
Feldman/Kalai--Kanade/Ghai--Singh style boosters.

**Proper weak outputs are not the obstacle.** The source returns proper
hypotheses in $C$, but the target may output an improper function. Once useful
proper weak hypotheses have appeared, the final learner can validate them,
flip signs, assign real weights, and output a vote outside $C$. The remaining
problem is the first-useful-candidate problem: a fixed-slack weak learner may
legally be neutral on every observable query with
$\operatorname{OPT}_C(Q)\ge \gamma$, even when the original distribution still
has a hidden comparator whose error is far below $1/2$.

The noisy-comparator calculation captures this. If
$$
Y=f(X)N,\qquad f\in C,\qquad \Pr[N=-1]=\eta,\qquad \gamma<\eta<1/2,
$$
then the original distribution is above the weak-forcing threshold. Agreement
filtering with a current predictor $g$ lowers the selected noise below
$\gamma$ only once
$$
\mathbb E[g(X)f(X)]>
\frac{\eta-\gamma}{\eta+\gamma-2\eta\gamma}.
$$
So observable denoising needs substantial correlation with the hidden
comparator before it can force a useful weak call. Improper aggregation helps
after this correlation is available; it does not explain how to generate it.

**Known fixed-slack booster runtime issue.** The closest positive theorem
remains da Cunha--Hogsgaard--Paudice 2026
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11265>. It accepts a nontrivial agnostic weak
learner semantically: here $(\gamma_0,\epsilon_0)=(1,2\beta)$ and
$\gamma_0>\epsilon_0$. The arXiv page still describes the runtime as
polynomial in the sample size with the other parameters fixed. The displayed
bounds in the scratchpad audit include
$$
O(n^{m_0+3})
$$
weak-learner invocations and a total evaluation bound of the form
$$
\operatorname{Eval}_H(1)\cdot
n^{O(m_0\min\{d^*,\ln n\}/\theta^2)}.
$$
Here $m_0$ is the weak learner's sample parameter and $d^*$ is a dual VC
dimension parameter. In the atlas efficient node these may grow polynomially
with the representation size, even for fixed $\gamma$. Therefore this is a
statistical or fixed-parameter positive theorem, not a
representation-uniform polynomial-time implication. The Ghai--Singh 2025
PMLR result <https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/ghai25a.html> remains the
fully polynomial branch, but it uses an accuracy-sensitive weak interface
rather than the atlas fixed-additive one.

**Inverse-polynomial parity fork.** Depth 4 gives a clean conditional false
result only for a different, parameterized weak-gap model. Affine parities
with constants have an efficient proper
$\operatorname{OPT}+1/2-\Theta(1/n)$ weak learner by constants, clean-batch
Gaussian elimination, and validation. Strong improper agnostic learning would
distinguish constant-noise LPN from random labels.

That does not resolve this edge. For any fixed $\gamma>0$, take uniform
affine parity labels with independent noise $\eta<\gamma$, say
$\eta=\gamma/2$. A fixed-gap source learner would have to output an affine
parity with error
$$
\eta+1/2-\gamma=1/2-(\gamma-\eta)<1/2.
$$
Every wrong affine parity and every constant parity has error exactly $1/2$,
so the source learner would already have to recover nontrivial correlation
with the hidden parity in the low-noise LPN regime. Thus the parity witness is
source-negative for the fixed-constant atlas node.

**Constant-radius weighted decoder requirement for a false witness.** A real
fixed-gap separation should probably add both constants to the class. Then
when $\operatorname{OPT}_C(D)\ge \gamma$, a constant hypothesis with error at
most $1/2$ already satisfies
$$
1/2\le \operatorname{OPT}_C(D)+1/2-\gamma.
$$
The only nontrivial source obligation is the low-noise regime
$\alpha=\operatorname{OPT}_C(D)<\gamma$, where the proper weak learner must
return a legal concept with
$$
\operatorname{err}(h)\le \alpha+1/2-\gamma
=1/2-(\gamma-\alpha).
$$
So a false witness must have an efficient distribution-free proper weak
decoder for every PAC marginal and every weighted adversarial corruption
pattern below a fixed radius $\gamma$, while improper
$\operatorname{OPT}+\varepsilon$ learning is hard at some larger fixed noise
level $\eta\in(\gamma,1/2)$ under one explicit marginal.

I do not know such a class. Standard LPN/LWE/parity-style candidates lack the
constant-radius source decoder. Ordinary error-correcting code decoders do not
handle arbitrary PAC marginals and weighted corruptions. Halfspace and monomial
hardness already enters the low-$\operatorname{OPT}$ region and refutes the
source. Fixed-$k$ DNF and weak-handle constructions separate proper targets or
fixed-marginal traps, but the present target is improper and can validate,
memorize, or vote over public handles. PCP/CSP templates have the same issue:
making the weak source public enough tends to make the improper target easy,
while hiding it enough tends to break the weak source.

**Conditional true principles.** The edge should be recorded as true under
several extra hypotheses, none of which are part of the present node:

1. If the cost measure is statistical or fixed-parameter in
   $m_0,d^*,1/\theta$, the da Cunha--Hogsgaard--Paudice fixed-slack theorem
   boosts the source to an improper strong learner.
2. If the weak residual is tunable at the final accuracy scale, for example
   $\epsilon_0=O(\varepsilon)$ or $O(\gamma_0\varepsilon)$ in the correlation
   interface, the known efficient agnostic boosters apply.
3. If the class and weak learner admit a polynomial clean-witness generator,
   hitting family, stable compression search, or equivalent hard-core search
   whose size is polynomial in $m_0,d^*,1/\theta,1/\varepsilon$, then the
   fixed-slack theorem's $n^{m_0}$ enumeration can be replaced and the edge
   becomes true for that structured subclass.
4. If polynomial running time is dropped, the sample-complexity version is
   positive by the statistical weak-to-strong principles already recorded in
   the neighboring atlas notes.

**No known separation.** The negative evidence is still a barrier rather than
a witness. A black-box oracle can be neutral on all transcript-measurable
high-$\operatorname{OPT}$ queries and useful only on hidden clean witnesses,
which explains the clean-subset enumeration. But an atlas-valid false edge
needs an actual represented class satisfying the fixed-constant weak proper
source and failing the strong improper target. I have no such class after this
final pass.

**Proposed atlas fields.**

- `status: "open"`
- `evidence: unknown`
- `assumptions: []`
- `witnesses: []`
- `summary: "Open: fixed-additive proper weak agnostic learning gives a nontrivial constant-slack oracle and is boostable statistically or with fixed weak parameters, but known fixed-slack boosters use clean-witness enumeration with representation-dependent exponents; no fixed-gap class separation is known."`

**Proposed proof-status body.** The source guarantee is equivalent to a
constant-slack correlation oracle
$\operatorname{corr}(h,Y)\ge\rho_C-2\beta$. Because the target is improper,
properness of weak outputs is not the obstruction: any useful proper
hypotheses can be combined by validation, sign choice, and weighted voting.
The obstacle is that fixed additive slack only forces useful weak responses on
queries with $\operatorname{OPT}_C<1/2-\beta$. Known efficient agnostic
boosters require an accuracy-sensitive residual, while the known
nontrivial-slack booster removes that residual only by searching for hidden
clean witnesses with $m_0$ and capacity parameters in the exponent. Thus the
current theorem route proves a statistical or fixed-parameter positive result,
not the representation-uniform efficient PAC implication.

The attempted affine-parity/LPN counterexample separates only a
parameterized inverse-polynomial weak-gap variant. Under the fixed-constant
atlas definition it fails the source, since low-noise parity with
$\eta<1/2-\beta$ would already require weak recovery of the hidden parity. A
real false witness would need a class with a public distribution-free proper
weak decoder for every weighted corruption pattern below a fixed radius, but
with hard improper agnostic prediction at a larger constant noise rate. No
standard parity, coding, halfspace, monomial, DNF, PCP, or one-way-handle
template currently supplies both sides. The edge should therefore remain open
until either a fully polynomial clean-witness generator is found or such a
constant-radius weighted-decoder separation is constructed.
