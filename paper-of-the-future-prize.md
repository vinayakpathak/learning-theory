# AMR Paper of the Future Prize

Source: [2026 Paper of the Future Prize](https://amathr.org/prizes/paper-of-the-future-prize-prize/), Association for Mathematical Research. Retrieved April 30, 2026.

## Basic Information

- **Award:** Paper of the Future Prize
- **Organizer:** Association for Mathematical Research (AMR)
- **Prize amount:** $10,000 for a selected winner
- **Runner-up recognition:** Possible, at committee discretion
- **Deadline:** September 1, 2026
- **Submission email:** PotFPrize@amathr.org
- **Submission hashtag:** `#AMRPotF`
- **Eligibility:** The official page does not state separate eligibility restrictions.

## Purpose

The prize is meant to encourage serious experimentation in mathematical communication beyond the static PDF. AMR is looking for submissions that demonstrate mathematical exposition with essential communicative capabilities unavailable in a linear written paper.

The official examples include:

- Interactive exploration of parameter spaces
- Dynamic visualization of group actions or geometric structures
- Multi-perspective representations of algebraic or number-theoretic objects
- Nonlinear navigation of proof architectures or dependency graphs
- Embedded computation as part of exposition

AMR emphasizes that the prize is not for popularization, production polish, or short-form video content. The intended contribution is epistemic: the medium should help mathematicians understand, navigate, or work with mathematical ideas in a way that static exposition cannot.

## Submission Process

Initial submissions should consist of:

- A short public concept-demonstration video uploaded to YouTube
- A concise written explanation in the YouTube description
- An email to PotFPrize@amathr.org containing the YouTube link
- The hashtag `#AMRPotF` included with the video

The written explanation should describe both:

- The mathematical substance of the submission
- The communicative innovation enabled by the format

Finalists will be asked to provide a fully accessible prototype suitable for hosting or linking within AMR Reviews.

## Evaluation Criteria

Submissions will be judged on:

- Mathematical depth and rigor
- Conceptual insight enabled by the medium
- Communicative innovation beyond static exposition
- Scalability and reproducibility
- Transformative potential for research communication

The committee reserves the right to withhold the prize if no submission meets the stated standards.

## Publication

The winning submission will be published in AMR Reviews as an interactive exposition, with documentation and citation structure.

## Fit With the Learning Theory Atlas

The learning theory atlas is plausibly aligned with the prize because it is already organized around a proof-and-implication graph rather than a linear paper. A strong submission could emphasize:

- Nonlinear navigation through definitions, implications, separations, and open edges
- Explicit dependency structure between learning-theoretic notions
- Interactive filtering by assumptions such as realizability, properness, efficiency, distributional regime, and evidence type
- A concept-demo showing how a researcher can move from a learning model to its known implications, counterexamples, witnesses, and primary references

For the application, the central claim should be that the atlas is not just a digitized survey. Its graph structure should make some mathematical relationships easier to inspect, compare, and verify than they would be in a static PDF.
